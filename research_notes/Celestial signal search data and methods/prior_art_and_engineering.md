## 1. Existing technosignature efforts and what they produce

### Takeaway
Professional technosignature work in 2026 is dominated by instrument-bound radio/optical programmes (Breakthrough Listen, SETI Institute's ATA/COSMIC/LaserSETI, PANOSETI) with their own telescopes, petabyte data and GPU pipelines; none of them is a cross-archive miner of public optical/IR/X-ray time-series. Several volunteer-era efforts have closed or gone dormant (SETI@home hibernated and published its final analysis in 2025; SETI League winding down; both big Zooniverse projects are "out of data"), and Breakthrough Listen's original ten-year funding window is ending with no confirmed renewal found.

### Cited Findings

#### SETI@home (UC Berkeley) — hibernated; final analysis published 2025; ~100 candidates being re-observed
- Two final papers appeared in The Astronomical Journal in 2025: "SETI@home: Data Acquisition and Front-End Processing" (Korpela, Anderson, Cobb, Lebofsky, Liu, Werthimer; AJ 170, 112; DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/ade5a7) and "SETI@home: Data Analysis and Findings" (Anderson, Korpela, Werthimer, Cobb, Allen), the latter describing the back end "Nebula" (RFI removal and candidate ranking) — [arXiv:2506.14718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14718v1); [arXiv:2506.14737](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14737v1); [project copy of the Nebula paper](https://setiathome.berkeley.edu/SETI_Home_Nebula_final.pdf)
- The back-end paper says the project recorded and analysed Arecibo data from 2006 through 2020; another abstract describes data from multiple observatories 1998–2020 (the search summary could not reconcile the two ranges explicitly) — [arXiv:2506.14737](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14737v1); [arXiv:2506.14718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14718v1)
- Funnel: about 12 billion detections went into the back end; about a million candidate signals were winnowed to roughly 100 that are being re-observed with FAST; the team expects them to be RFI, and follow-up was still in progress in January 2026 — [HPCwire, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/01/14/setihome-didnt-find-aliens-but-was-it-a-distributed-computing-success/); [Space.com](https://space.com/space-exploration/search-for-life/this-seti-program-is-chasing-down-its-final-100-signals-could-one-of-them-be-from-aliens); [UC Berkeley L&S news](https://ls.berkeley.edu/news/21-years-enthusiasts-used-their-home-computers-search-et-uc-berkeley-scientists-are-homing-100)
- The volunteer-computing phase lasted 21 years (per the Berkeley headline "For 21 years, enthusiasts used their home computers to search for ET") — [UC Berkeley L&S news](https://ls.berkeley.edu/news/21-years-enthusiasts-used-their-home-computers-search-et-uc-berkeley-scientists-are-homing-100); [phys.org copy](https://phys.org/news/2026-01-enthusiasts-home-scientists-homing.html)
- Lessons stated by the team: David Anderson said that in the early years they "had not thought much" about how to vet the detections; Eric Korpela said of sensitivity/vetting "I don't think we know for most SETI searches, and that is really a lesson for SETI searches everywhere"; some design choices were conscious concessions to 1999 computer speeds — [phys.org, Jan 2026](https://phys.org/news/2026-01-enthusiasts-home-scientists-homing.html)
- Synthetic test signals ("birdies") were injected because the filters were heuristic; the final sensitivity run used 3,000 birdies, scored by the fraction of birdies ranking among the top 1,000 non-birdie multiplets, which yields detection probability versus flux, bandwidth and observation length — [commentary, Planetary Perspectives (secondary)](https://planetaryperspectives.substack.com/p/what-setihome-learned-from-its-own); [arXiv:2506.14737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14737)
- The project's public "Nebula results" web page is a live page whose cached copy showed a 2021 pipeline run, i.e. it is not a final summary — [Nebula results page](https://setiathome.berkeley.edu/nebula/index.php)

- Oxford was still recruiting BL staff in 2026: a senior research scientist post (closed June 2026), a senior researcher in technosignature research whose duties include securing research funding (closing 9 Nov 2026), and a fixed-term "intelligence engineer" post "subject to funding" — [jobs.ac.uk DRP609](https://www.jobs.ac.uk/job/DRP609/senior-research-scientist); [jobs.ac.uk DTH773](https://www.jobs.ac.uk/job/DTH773/senior-researcher); [jobs.ac.uk DSC592](https://www.jobs.ac.uk/job/DSC592/intelligence-engineer)
- Funding status: no announcement of a second decade or renewed core funding was found. One 2026 article says the ten-year observational window closes in 2026 and that work continues "under the original funding commitment"; an undated feature says BL "is still funded to this day by Yuri Milner and the Breakthrough Foundation" — [New Space Economy, 25 Mar 2026 (secondary)](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/25/breakthrough-listen-humanitys-most-ambitious-search-for-extraterrestrial-intelligence/); [Supercluster, "What has BL achieved after 10 years?"](https://www.supercluster.com/editorial/over-a-decade-later-what-has-setis-breakthrough-listen-achieved)
- Open data: Data Release 1.0 was "a defined set of publicly-available raw and reduced data totalling 1 PB" (Lebofsky et al. 2019); a February 2020 release brought the public total to nearly 2 PB and included APF optical data and FRB 121102 observations; at that time only ~20% of captured data had been analysed — [arXiv:1906.07391](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1906.07391v3); [Green Bank Observatory news](https://greenbankobservatory.org/news/breakthrough-listen-releases-2-pb-of-data-from-seti-survey-of-milky-way/); [Breakthrough Initiatives news](https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/news/28)
- Software (GitHub org "UC Berkeley SETI Program"): `turbo_seti` ("Public turboSETI — python based SETI search algorithm"), `blimpy` ("Breakthrough Listen I/O Methods for Python"), `blipss` ("Breakthrough Listen Investigation for Periodic Spectral Signals"), `gbt_seti`, `commensal-automator` — [github.com/ucberkeleyseti](https://github.com/ucberkeleyseti)
- `hyperseti`: GPU search for narrowband technosignatures built on a brute-force dedoppler CUDA core with a Python front end, described as "the spiritual successor to the turboSETI package"; installed with pip from the GitHub URL (it is not on PyPI) — [github.com/UCBerkeleySETI/hyperseti](https://github.com/UCBerkeleySETI/hyperseti); PyPI lookup `hyperseti` returned 404 on 14 Nov 2026 — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/hyperseti/)
- `seticore` (github.com/lacker/seticore) is a high-performance C++/CUDA "tree de-Doppler" search and beamformer used alongside turboSETI in BL work — [arXiv:2412.05786, "A Novel Technosignature Search in the BL GBT Archive"](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05786v2); [community table in backyard-seti](https://github.com/w4gon79/backyard-seti)
- PyPI state of the Python stack: `blimpy` 2.1.4 (last release 16 Nov 2022, BSD-3-Clause per its LICENSE), `turbo_seti` 2.3.2 (25 May 2022, MIT; README: "This code is stable, but new features are currently under development"), `setigen` 2.7.0 (2 Aug 2024, MIT) — [blimpy](https://pypi.org/project/blimpy/); [turbo_seti](https://pypi.org/project/turbo-seti/); [setigen](https://pypi.org/project/setigen/)
- New in 2026: `aetherscan` 1.0.0 (1 Aug 2026) → 1.2.0 (27 Aug 2026), BSD-3-Clause, self-described as "Breakthrough Listen's first end-to-end production-grade deep learning pipeline for SETI @ scale": a beta-VAE plus Random-Forest ensemble trained on ~30 million cadence snippets, extending Ma et al. 2023 to near-real-time inference; it requires NVIDIA GPUs and "RAM ≥288 GB" for full-scale training and catalogue-scale inference — [PyPI aetherscan](https://pypi.org/project/aetherscan/); [Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/zachtheyek/aetherscan)
- Deep-learning result: Ma et al. 2023 (Nature Astronomy) searched 820 targets / 480+ h of GBT data with a beta-convolutional VAE (paired with random forests) and flagged 8 signals of interest for re-observation; no detection claimed — [arXiv:2301.12670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12670); [BL ML overview page](https://seti.berkeley.edu/ml_gbt/overview.html)
- November 2025: BL/SETI Institute/NVIDIA collaboration produced an AI detection system for fast radio bursts (see §8) — [iTWire (secondary)](https://itwire.com/guest-articles/guest-opinion/yuri-milners-breakthrough-listen-partnered-with-nvidia-what-an-ai-scanning-800-million-radio-channels-is-actually-looking-for)

#### SETI Institute — Allen Telescope Array (ATA), COSMIC, LaserSETI: all active in 2026
- ATA: an upgrade programme funded by Franklin Antonio has been under way since 2019 (new feeds and digital signal processing; CPU/GPU correlator and beamformer) — [SETI Institute news, 2019](https://www.seti.org/news/qualcomm-founder-franklin-antonio-funds-allen-telescope-array-upgrade-program/); [URSI GASS 2023 paper](https://www.ursi.org/proceedings/procGA23/papers/2799.pdf); [ATA project page](https://www.seti.org/projects/ata)
- ATA 2026 campaigns: a 1–9 GHz search of interstellar object 3I/ATLAS identified nearly 74 million narrowband signals, of which about 200 remained for review after RFI rejection and motion filtering, none attributable to technology (AJ 172, 1, 2026); a joint ATA+VLA search of LTT 3780 found no candidates after multi-beam consistency tests — [SETI Institute news, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.seti.org/news/seti-institute-looks-for-signs-of-technology-in-interstellar-visitor-3iatlas/); [arXiv:2607.07182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07182)
- The ATA was hibernated in 2011 for lack of funding and resumed operation in December 2011 — [Scientific American blog](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/budget-crunch-mothballs-telescopes-built-to-search-for-alien-signals/)
- COSMIC ("Commensal Open-Source Multimode Interferometer Cluster") is led by the SETI Institute with NRAO and Breakthrough Listen; it takes a copy of the digital signals from each of the VLA's 27 antennas and searches commensally (alongside VLASS) without disturbing primary observers — [system paper arXiv:2310.09414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09414); [survey description and first results, arXiv:2501.17997](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17997v1); [SETI Institute press release](https://www.seti.org/press-release/massive-radio-array-search-extraterrestrial-signals-other-civilizations)
- COSMIC observed over 500,000 sources in its first six months (project scientist Chenoa Tremblay); its first high-frequency technosignature study (arXiv:2608.18275, Acta Astronautica 2026) searches coherent and incoherent beams for narrowband signals with drift rates up to ±50 Hz/s; a 2026 SETI Institute/AUI release describes it as operational — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/cosmic-seti-alien-life-search-underway); [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/987883); [Astrobiology.com, 21 Aug 2026](https://astrobiology.com/2026/08/21/the-vla-and-high-frequency-seti-expanding-the-search-for-life/); [AUI release](https://aui.edu/seti-institute-unlocks-mysteries-of-the-universe-with-breakthrough-technology-at-the-vla)
- LaserSETI (optical laser-flash search network "growing toward all-sky coverage"): two new stations in Sedona, AZ (July 2024); a fourth observatory at Isla Magueyes, Puerto Rico (2025); two stations at AlUla Manara, Saudi Arabia installed 19 Aug 2026, "fully operational", the first in Asia, to be run for three years with the Royal Commission for AlUla — [SETI Institute, Aug 2024](https://www.seti.org/news/new-laserseti-observatory-installed-in-sedona-az); [SETI Institute, Sep 2025](https://www.seti.org/news/laserseti-expands-network-to-puerto-rico-growing-toward-allsky-coverage); [EurekAlert release](https://eurekalert.org/news-releases/1148928); [The Debrief](https://thedebrief.org/?p=53999); [LaserSETI observatories page](https://laserseti.net/observatories)

#### PANOSETI (UC San Diego / UC Berkeley / UC Observatories and partners) — pathfinder phase, activity centred on Palomar
- Two prototype telescopes were installed in Lick Observatory's Astrograph Dome around early 2020; the planned full design is a dedicated observatory at each of two sites with 80 telescopes each — [UC San Diego Today](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/new-telescopes-aim-to-detect-extraterrestrial-intelligence); [instrument paper arXiv:1808.05772](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.05772.pdf)
- Three PANOSETI telescopes were deployed twice at Lick (March and October 2024), including Crab Nebula observations 30 Oct–1 Nov 2024; deployment of the "Dark100" array, planned to operate five years at Palomar Observatory, was under way — [Korzoun et al., arXiv:2508.05548](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05548v1)

#### VASCO (Vanishing & Appearing Sources during a Century of Observations) — active and contested
- Design paper compares mid-20th-century USNO/POSS plates with modern surveys to find vanished or appeared sources — [arXiv:1911.05068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05068)
- After tracing 600 million sources no real vanishing star was identified (Solano et al. 2022), giving a failed-supernova rate below one in a billion per 70 years — [arXiv:2206.00907](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2206.00907)
- 2025 claims: a Scientific Reports paper (20 Oct 2025) reported POSS-I transients were 45% more likely within 24 h of above-ground nuclear tests; a companion result reported an Earth-shadow deficit — [ScienceAlert](https://sciencealert.com/study-links-mysterious-lights-in-the-sky-to-historic-nuclear-tests); [Live Science via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/no-easy-explanation-scientists-debating-110000516.html)
- 2026 dispute: Watters et al. (2026) criticised sample selection and statistics (preferring the term "Selected POSS 1-E Features" to "transients"); Villarroel et al. replied (arXiv:2602.15171, association "remains significant (p<0.046)"); an independent automated POSS-I pipeline found "no statistically significant association between vanishing sources and nuclear test events" (arXiv:2604.04810); another preprint reports a replication using the VASCO catalogue of 107,875 transients (arXiv:2604.00056); a Hamburg plate study reports similar transients (arXiv:2603.20407) — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/?p=597499); [rebuttal summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.15171); [arXiv:2604.04810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04810); [arXiv:2604.00056](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00056v1); [arXiv:2603.20407](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20407)

### Inferences
- Every funded programme is tied to its own instrument and data format (filterbank/HDF5/GUPPI raw for radio, custom photon-counting for optical). Their software is reusable only if Stargazer chooses to touch BL radio products; it does not address optical/IR/X-ray archive mining.
- The radio stack on PyPI (blimpy, turbo_seti) has not been released since 2022 while newer work lives in GitHub-only or C++/CUDA code (hyperseti, seticore) and in a heavy deep-learning pipeline (Aetherscan, ≥288 GB RAM). For a solo developer, raw BL data and these pipelines are out of reach; only small reduced products are practical.
- Status flags for 2025–2026: SETI@home (hibernated, final papers 2025, FAST follow-up ongoing); BL (HQ at Oxford since 2023; decade funding ending, renewal unconfirmed); SETI League (winding down); UCLA SETI Zooniverse and Planet Hunters TESS (both "out of data"); ATA, COSMIC, LaserSETI, PANOSETI (active and expanding); VASCO (active, results disputed in 2026).
- Citizen-science projects stall when the upstream team stops preparing data, not for lack of volunteers — both Zooniverse projects are idle while reporting tens of thousands of participants.

## 2. Has anyone already built a cross-archive, multi-wavelength technosignature or anomaly-mining tool?

### Takeaway
No maintained, peer-reviewed, cross-archive multi-wavelength technosignature miner was found. The closest analogues are (a) one-off scientific catalogue cross-matches (Project Hephaistos, VASCO), (b) a 2025 paper showing how to run SETI-style searches through existing alert brokers, (c) single-survey anomaly frameworks (SNAD, Astronomaly), and (d) very recent single-author GitHub projects — notably `seti-tools` — that already attempt much of the Stargazer concept at hobby scale but without visible validation.

### Cited Findings
- **`seti-tools` (CharlesCNorton; mirrored on Hugging Face as phanerozoic/seti-tools)** describes itself as "Technosignature searches on public astronomical archives: the code, the papers, and a database of what every run covered". Entry point is a single script taking a family, an approach and a mode (`seti.py highenergy xmm` is an example); there are twelve family modules, a SQLite coverage database with one row per run and one per target, and a results file with one line per approach. Code is MIT; papers, documentation and the coverage store are CC BY 4.0 — [GitHub](https://github.com/CharlesCNorton/seti-tools); [Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/phanerozoic/seti-tools)
- `seti-tools` archive coverage as listed: Gaia RVS; ESO spectrographs (HARPS, ESPRESSO, UVES, FEROS); GALAH DR4; APOGEE DR17; DESI DR1; LAMOST DR10 MRS; CARMENES DR1; an XMM-Newton periodicity search on 5XMM-DR15 EPIC-pn time series; IRSA holdings for WISE, CatWISE2020 and ZTF; one search summary of the GitHub README names dark-companion, vanished-star and "Gaia dippers" searches (a second search, of the Hugging Face mirror, did not confirm those module names); acknowledgements list Gaia, SIMBAD, MAST, IRSA, Breakthrough Listen and the ESO archive — [GitHub](https://github.com/CharlesCNorton/seti-tools); [Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/phanerozoic/seti-tools)
- **`trimcrae/Seti`** states that "the objective of this repository is a detection", runs on GitHub Actions with no GPU, and includes search channels for narrow absorption lines and Gaia XP spectral anomalies (seen in one search; a second search could not re-find it) — [GitHub](https://github.com/trimcrae/Seti)
- **Gallay, Davenport & Croft 2025, "Technosignature Searches with Real-time Alert Brokers"** (AJ 170; DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/ade4bb): notes nine brokers stream up to 1 million ZTF alerts per night and LSST will raise that by an order of magnitude; evaluates spatial and temporal SETI methods on existing brokers; shows examples combining ZTF alert and archival data with Gaia cross-matches — [arXiv:2506.14744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14744); [Astrobiology.com](https://astrobiology.com/2025/06/18/technosignature-searches-with-real-time-alert-brokers/)
- **Project Hephaistos** (Suazo et al. 2024) screened ~5 million stars in Gaia + 2MASS + WISE for infrared excess and reported seven Dyson-sphere candidates; Ren, Garrett & Siemion argued all seven are very likely contaminated by background hot dust-obscured galaxies in WISE W3/W4 (three matched to radio sources); a July 2026 JWST/MIRI study found candidates D and E to be chance alignments with a Hot DOG (z≈0.9) and a dusty starburst (z≈0.4) — [arXiv:2405.14921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14921); [Science Times, 29 Jul 2026 (secondary)](https://www.sciencetimes.com/articles/62255/20260729/jwst-observes-two-promising-dyson-sphere-star-candidates-debunks-them-simple-cosmic-alignment.htm); [Universe Magazine (secondary)](https://universemagazine.com/en/version-about-dyson-spheres-around-two-stars-has-been-definitively-rejected/)
- **VASCO** is a cross-epoch catalogue comparison (plates vs modern surveys), see §1 — [arXiv:1911.05068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05068)
- **Single-survey anomaly searches**: SNAD's active anomaly detection on ZTF data releases; Chan et al. on anomalies in the ZTF catalogue of periodic variables (Gaussian-process interpolation with `george`); an unsupervised exploration of TESS light curves linking anomalous behaviour to stellar properties — [Coniferest paper arXiv:2410.17142](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17142v2); [arXiv:2112.03306](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.03306); [arXiv:2301.10264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10264)
- The 2018 NASA Technosignatures Workshop report already pointed out that archival space data (WISE/NEOWISE in the mid-IR, Gaia astrometry and time series) "can be searched for transient or unique signatures" — [arXiv:1812.08681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.08681)
- Event-triggered strategies exist as papers rather than tools, e.g. "Real-time Technosignature Strategies with SN 2023ixf" and a simultaneous dual-site LOFAR search — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10423087); [arXiv:2310.15704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.15704)

### Inferences
- The remaining gap is not "nobody has tried" but "nobody has done it rigorously and reusably": there is no tool that combines (i) uniform access to several public time-domain archives, (ii) null-model-calibrated anomaly/periodicity scoring, (iii) cross-wavelength contamination checks, (iv) injection–recovery sensitivity numbers, and (v) a public ledger of what was searched and found.
- `seti-tools` is the nearest design precedent (single CLI, per-run coverage database, mixed licence for code vs. documents) and should be read before designing Stargazer; its coverage-database idea directly answers the "what did every run cover" problem that SETI@home's authors say most searches cannot answer.
- The appearance of several AI-assisted solo repositories in 2025–2026 (seti-tools, trimcrae/Seti, backyard-seti, seti-cookbook) means novelty will come from validation quality (null tests, injections, documented false-positive handling), not from breadth of archive connectors.
- Hephaistos shows the characteristic failure of cross-archive mining: an apparently clean multi-catalogue outlier that is really source confusion at the coarsest survey's angular resolution.

### Gaps
- None of the GitHub repositories could be opened; licence, activity, code quality and any results for `seti-tools`, `trimcrae/Seti`, `backyard-seti` and `seti-cookbook` rest on search-result summaries only.
- No search was possible for other possible analogues (e.g. Breakthrough Listen's "Exotica" catalogue, SETI-ellipsoid searches with Gaia/ZTF/TESS, Boyajian's-star analogue searches, the SETI Institute's Technosearch database, quasar-modulation SETI proposals); they are known to exist from prior knowledge but were not verified here.

## 3. Time-domain infrastructure that could be reused or learned from

### Takeaway
A mature, open, Rubin-era stack already ingests, cross-matches, classifies and serves optical transient alerts (seven full-stream brokers, live on Rubin since 24 Feb 2026), and LINCC Frameworks' LSDB/HATS makes billion-row catalogue cross-matching a pip-install. Stargazer should consume these services and formats rather than rebuild ingest, cross-match or feature extraction; what the brokers do not provide is archival, non-transient, technosignature-oriented scoring.

### Cited Findings

#### Rubin alert ecosystem (context)
- Rubin issued its first public alerts on the night of 24 Feb 2026 (~800,000 alerts); alerts flow through seven community brokers plus two downstream services: ALeRCE, AMPEL, ANTARES, Babamul, Fink, Lasair, Pitt-Google, SNAPS and POI Broker — [NOIRLab announcement](https://noirlab.edu/science/news/announcements/sci26008); [SLAC news, 25 Feb 2026](https://www6.slac.stanford.edu/news/2026-02-25-nsf-doe-vera-c-rubin-observatory-launches-real-time-discovery-machine-monitoring); [Rubin "Alerts and brokers"](https://rubinobservatory.org/for-scientists/data-products/alerts-and-brokers)
- LSST survey operations reportedly began 30 June 2026 with up to seven million alerts per night expected — [TechTimes, 1 Jul 2026 (secondary)](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319434/20260701/rubin-observatory-begins-lsst-nightly-7-million-alert-stream-will-test-if-dark-energy-evolves.htm)
- A 2025 review compares the seven Rubin community brokers and notes the ZTF stream is a precursor at ~10% of LSST volume sharing schema, serialisation format and streaming platform (Apache Kafka) — [Vujčić et al. 2025, CAOSP 55(2)](https://ftp.ta3.sk/caosp/Eedition/FullTexts/vol55no2/pp95-105.pdf)

#### Brokers: architecture, openness, 2026 status
- **ALeRCE** (Chile): alerts ingested via Kafka into sequential/parallel pipeline steps (S3 upload of Avro packets, cross-match against external catalogues, a rotation-invariant CNN "stamp classifier", a light-curve classifier on multiband features, an outlier-detection step), with annotated/classified light curves re-emitted on a Kafka stream — [ALeRCE pipeline page](https://alerce.science/alerce-pipeline); [system paper arXiv:2008.03303](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.03303); [light-curve classifier arXiv:2008.03311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.03311)
- ALeRCE Python client `alerce` 2.4.0 (5 Nov 2026) is MIT-licensed and now multi-survey: most query methods take `survey="ztf"` or `survey="lsst"` (LSST object IDs are numeric) — [PyPI alerce](https://pypi.org/project/alerce/)
- **Fink** (France, IN2P3): Apache Spark Structured Streaming ingest; community-written "science modules" add cross-matches and ML scores; filtered alerts are redistributed via Kafka; enriched alerts are stored in Apache HBase; services run on OpenStack with Spark, HBase, Kafka, Ceph/HDFS — [Fink paper arXiv:2009.10185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10185); [fink-broker repo](https://github.com/astrolabsoftware/fink-broker); [Fink docs "why Fink"](https://fink-broker.readthedocs.io/en/latest/why-fink/)
- `fink-client` 12.3.0 (14 Oct 2026, Apache-2.0) wraps Kafka for three services (Livestream, Data Transfer, Xmatch) for both ZTF and LSST; credentials are issued after filling a subscription form; `fink-filters` 7.47 was released 13 Nov 2026 — [PyPI fink-client](https://pypi.org/project/fink-client/); [PyPI fink-filters](https://pypi.org/project/fink-filters/)
- **Lasair** (UK, Edinburgh/Belfast): Kafka ingest → full copy archived in Cassandra, cut-outs on a shared filesystem → "Sherlock" context classifier (each Sherlock node holds a 5 TB SQL database of sources from ~40 catalogues) → user filters written in a restricted SQL that can be promoted to streaming filters writing to output Kafka topics — [Lasair "About"](https://lasair.readthedocs.io/en/main/about.html); [EPCC article](https://www.epcc.ed.ac.uk/whats-happening/articles/lasair-alert-broker)
- Lasair API throttling (from the client README): the public free token is limited to 10 calls per hour; a personal account token allows 100 calls per hour; "power users" can request unlimited use; client `lasair` 0.1.5 (4 Nov 2026, MIT) defaults to endpoint `https://api.lasair.lsst.ac.uk/api` — [PyPI lasair](https://pypi.org/project/lasair/)
- **ANTARES** (NSF NOIRLab): filters Rubin alerts and feeds NOIRLab's GOATS follow-up system; in the first end-to-end run 18 Rubin alerts flagged as likely supernovae were followed up — [Newswise/NOIRLab release](https://www.newswise.com/articles/first-nsf-noirlab-follow-up-observations-triggered-by-nsf-doe-rubin-alerts); [GOATS paper arXiv:2606.28645](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28645)
- ANTARES client `antares-client` 2.0.2 (22 Oct 2026; MIT per its LICENSE file; source on GitLab): "all versions of this package prior to 2.0 will stop being supported November 4, 2026" because the v1 API is being retired — [PyPI antares-client](https://pypi.org/project/antares-client/)
- **AMPEL** (DESY/Humboldt): four independently scheduled execution tiers exchanging information through a MongoDB database — T0 filters the alert stream, T1 handles datapoint/state updates, T2 derives properties (light-curve fits, photo-z, ML classification, catalogue matching), T3 runs scheduled reactions; designed for provenance (components mapped to the IVOA Provenance Data Model, logs tagged by channel and transient) and for reprocessing archived alerts — [Nordin et al. 2019, arXiv:1904.05922](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.05922); [A&A 631, A147](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2019/11/aa35634-19/aa35634-19.html)
- `ampel-core` (BSD-3-Clause): latest stable 0.10.4.post0 (8 Jan 2025) with alpha builds through 5 Nov 2026; `ampel-lsst` alphas through 14 Oct 2026 — [PyPI ampel-core](https://pypi.org/project/ampel-core/); [PyPI ampel-lsst](https://pypi.org/project/ampel-lsst/)
- **Babamul / BOOM** (Caltech + University of Minnesota; NSF award #2432476): a new Rust broker using MongoDB, a Valkey in-memory queue and Kafka, composed of worker types (alert, enrichment/ML, filter) with a REST API for marshals such as SkyPortal; cross-matches ZTF and Rubin streams continuously; one of the seven full-stream brokers; described in arXiv:2511.00164 and PASP (June 2026) — [arXiv:2511.00164](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00164v1); [ZTF BOOM/Babamul page](https://www.ztf.caltech.edu/ztf-boom-babamul.html); [PASP record](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/publication/2026PASP..138f4503J)
- `babamul` 0.2.0 (25 Oct 2026, MIT) is the Kafka client; it needs a Kafka username/password and API token, and topics are organised by survey and class (e.g. `babamul.lsst.ztf-match.stellar`, `babamul.lsst.no-ztf-match.hostless`) — [PyPI babamul](https://pypi.org/project/babamul/)
- **Pitt-Google**: `pittgoogle-client` 0.3.25 (10 Nov 2026, BSD-3-Clause) is the client for the Pitt-Google broker — [PyPI pittgoogle-client](https://pypi.org/project/pittgoogle-client/)

#### Follow-up/target management and marshals
- **TOM Toolkit** (Las Cumbres Observatory; PI Rachel Street; NSF grant 2209852): "a web framework for building TOMs: Target and Observation Managers" for curating target lists, requesting observations and organising data; plugin architecture (e.g. `tom-antares`, `tom-fink`, `tom_registration` with open or admin-approved sign-up); `tomtoolkit` 3.2.0 released 12 Nov 2026 with 39 stable releases in the preceding 24 months; licence is GPL-3.0 (LICENSE file; the PyPI trove classifier incorrectly says BSD) — [PyPI tomtoolkit](https://pypi.org/project/tomtoolkit/); [PyPI tom-antares](https://pypi.org/project/tom-antares/); [PyPI tom-fink](https://pypi.org/project/tom-fink/); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/4437764/latest)
- **SkyPortal / Fritz**: Fritz (the ZTF collaboration marshal, 200+ active users, successor to the GROWTH Marshal) combines SkyPortal with the Kowalski archive, which stores ZTF alerts and executes user-defined filters; design is async Python back ends with a React/Redux front end; Kowalski is MIT; SkyPortal's own licence shows as "other" in repository metadata — [SkyPortal GitHub org](https://github.com/skyportal); [SkyPortal paper, ApJS](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4365/acdee1/meta); [Kowalski repo](https://github.com/dmitryduev/kowalski); [ecosyste.ms metadata](https://repos.ecosyste.ms/hosts/GitHub/repositories/skyportal%2Fskyportal)
- A third-party MCP server for SkyPortal exists (`skyportal-mcp`) — [glama.ai listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/AnastasiaKWei/skyportal-mcp/schema)

#### Anomaly-detection pipelines
- **SNAD** is an international consortium focused on anomaly detection in large light-curve catalogues; its `light-curve` feature extractor is used by several ZTF/LSST brokers; it runs the public "SNAD Viewer" web portal for expert inspection of ZTF data-release light curves — [Coniferest paper arXiv:2410.17142](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17142v2); [IN2P3 seminar page](https://indico.in2p3.fr/event/31905/)
- `coniferest` (MIT; 0.2.2 on 14 Oct 2026; 13 stable releases in 24 months) provides a Rust-accelerated multi-threaded `IsolationForest`, `AADForest` (Active Anomaly Discovery) and SNAD's own `PineForest` active-learning model; the tutorial states its isolation forest is up to 100× faster than scikit-learn's; cite Kornilov et al. 2025 (Astronomy & Computing) and ASCL 2507.009 — [PyPI coniferest](https://pypi.org/project/coniferest/); [AISSAI 2024 tutorial](https://coniferest.snad.space/en/latest/pre_executed/AISSAI_2024_tutorial.html)
- SNAD's human-in-the-loop scale: the SNAD VIII workshop ran PineForest on ZTF DR23 light curves in four fields overlapping LSSTComCam and visually inspected 400 candidates — [arXiv:2507.06217](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06217)
- **Astronomaly** (Lochner & Bassett 2021, Astronomy & Computing 36, 100481): a general anomaly-detection framework with an active-learning loop that gives "personalised recommendations"; code at github.com/MichelleLochner/astronomaly — [arXiv:2010.11202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11202); [ADS record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A%26C....3600481L)

#### LINCC Frameworks (LSDB / HATS / nested-pandas)
- **LSDB** ("scalable analysis of large catalogs, i.e. querying and crossmatching ~10⁹ sources", Dask-parallel over HATS-partitioned surveys): 0.11.2 released 13 Nov 2026, BSD-3-Clause, 33 stable releases in the last 24 months; supported by Schmidt Sciences, NSF AST-2003196 and the DIRAC Institute — [PyPI lsdb](https://pypi.org/project/lsdb/)
- **HATS** (Hierarchical Adaptive Tiling Scheme; adaptive HEALPix partitioning of Parquet to balance file sizes): `hats` 0.11.2 (11 Nov 2026) and `hats-import` 0.11.2 (13 Nov 2026), BSD-3-Clause; an IVOA Note standardising HATS is dated 22 Aug 2025 — [PyPI hats](https://pypi.org/project/hats/); [PyPI hats-import](https://pypi.org/project/hats-import/); [IVOA HATS Note](https://www.ivoa.net/documents/Notes/HATS/20250822/NOTE-hats-ivoa-1.0-20250822.pdf)
- Renames/retirements: the predecessor package `hipscat` stopped at 0.3.9 (27 Aug 2024) and `hats` first appeared as 0.4.0 on 16 Oct 2024; LINCC's earlier time-series package `lf-tape` last released 0.4.1 on 30 Apr 2024 — [PyPI hipscat](https://pypi.org/project/hipscat/); [PyPI hats](https://pypi.org/project/hats/); [PyPI lf-tape](https://pypi.org/project/lf-tape/)
- **nested-pandas** (pandas extension storing per-object nested tables such as light curves, PyArrow-backed): 0.7.3 on 10 Nov 2026, MIT — [PyPI nested-pandas](https://pypi.org/project/nested-pandas/)
- The LSDB paper (Caplar et al., arXiv:2501.02103, v2 Oct 2025) says HATS catalogues are served online primarily via data.lsdb.io; public demonstration catalogues include Gaia, ZTF and Pan-STARRS; LSDB is used in Rubin tutorials ("Catalog access with LSDB", DP2) and in "Variability-finding in Rubin Data Preview 1 with LSDB" — [arXiv:2501.02103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02103); [AAS 245 abstract](https://baas.aas.org/pub/2025n2i471p02); [Rubin DP2 tutorial](https://dp2.lsst.io/tutorials/notebook/102/notebook-102-6.html); [arXiv:2506.23955](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23955)
- **LightCurveLynx** (LINCC; time-domain simulation with realistic survey strategies, "to evaluate observing strategy, characterize biases, and test pipelines"): 0.7.2 on 13 Nov 2026, MIT — [PyPI lightcurvelynx](https://pypi.org/project/lightcurvelynx/)

### Inferences
- Common architecture across brokers: Kafka in → durable alert archive (S3/HBase/Cassandra/MongoDB) → cross-match/enrichment → feature extraction and ML scoring → user filters → Kafka/REST out → human review in a marshal/TOM. Stargazer's batch equivalent is: archive pull → Parquet cache → cross-match (LSDB) → features (`light-curve`) → anomaly/periodicity scores → candidate store → review UI.
- Brokers are tuned for *new* transients; they will not flag slow, persistent or periodic oddities in non-transient sources (the Gallay et al. paper is essentially a how-to for bending them to SETI use). That archival niche is where Stargazer fits, using broker APIs only as one data source.
- AMPEL's tiered, provenance-first design and its "re-run the archive through the same code" capability is the best architectural model for reproducible candidate generation; a full TOM/SkyPortal deployment (web app, database, user management) is heavier than a solo project needs — a SQLite/DuckDB candidate table plus a light viewer is the proportionate choice.
- Service churn is real: ANTARES retired its v1 API on 4 Nov 2026, ALeRCE's client changed to a multi-survey signature, Kowalski is being succeeded by BOOM, and hipscat was renamed HATS. Connector code should be thin, versioned and covered by recorded-response tests.

### Gaps
- Astronomaly's licence, its 2025–2026 maintenance state and the "Protege" follow-on could not be confirmed; SkyPortal's exact licence text and database engine (believed PostgreSQL) were not confirmed.
- TOM Toolkit being Django-based, Pitt-Google being built on Google Cloud Pub/Sub/BigQuery, and Lasair's current LSST deployment details are from prior knowledge or pre-2024 sources, not verified for 2026.
- No 2026 funding statements were found for individual brokers (ANTARES in particular, given NOIRLab budget pressure in the FY2026 request).
- The current list and sizes of HATS catalogues on data.lsdb.io were not retrieved.

## 5. Free or low-cost compute and co-located data

### Takeaway
Three no-cost hosted platforms are usable by an independent developer with registration (NASA Fornax, MAST's TIKE, NOIRLab Astro Data Lab), each with modest quotas; the Rubin Science Platform is closed to people without Rubin data rights (though Rubin alerts are public). The cheapest robust pattern is to read public cloud buckets and HATS/Parquet catalogues in place and keep only derived features and candidates.

### Cited Findings
- **NASA Fornax Science Console** (resource-allotment document v1.3, 17 Jul 2026): each user gets 1,000 credits per fiscal year (renewed 1 October) covering compute, storage and egress, and may ask the helpdesk for more; home directory has a 200 GB ceiling; storage prices are 0.090 credits/GB/month (home, FSx), 0.023 (additional private S3) and 0.080 (/scratch, EBS); scratch can be expanded from the dashboard up to 3 TB for days or weeks; running out of credits may temporarily limit compute and storage — [Fornax allotments and costs](https://docs.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/user-resource-allotments-and-costs/); [scratch-storage announcement](https://discourse.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/t/manage-scratch-storage-from-the-dashboard/1109)
- Fornax access terms: a January 2026 user agreement restricts use to scientific research and education; MFA is required at every login; external beta users are now being admitted ("in this first phase, we are focusing on researchers, but will eventually open to students and the broader public"); accounts are requested by email to the Fornax helpdesk — [user agreement](https://docs.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/user-agreement); [forum announcement](https://discourse.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/t/recent-updates-to-fornax-science-console-and-welcoming-external-beta-users/642); [console capabilities](https://docs.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/intro-forsc/)
- Fornax announced new co-located SPHEREx and ZTF data (including ZTF DR24) — [forum announcement](https://discourse.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/t/new-spherex-and-ztf-data-plus-fornax-science-environment-updates/738)
- **MAST TIKE** (Timeseries Integrated Knowledge Engine): "a free JupyterHub platform" from STScI designed for TESS, Kepler and K2 time-series work; STScI's cloud-platform page says per-user storage "is typically on the order of 25–50 GB" depending on platform — [TIKE landing page](https://timeseries.science.stsci.edu/); [MAST cloud science platforms](https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/MASTDOCS/pages/183797523/Cloud+Science+Platforms); [STScI newsletter, Dec 2021](https://archive.stsci.edu/contents/newsletters/december-2021/explore-mast-data-in-the-cloud-with-tike)
- **NOIRLab Astro Data Lab**: free account with a private Jupyter notebook server, MyDB and virtual storage backed by an allocation described as "1+ TB" (2021 guide: "~1 TB"; the 2025 accounts page says only "a large server-side resource allocation"); all access is "best-effort … with no guaranteed service level" and accounts may be suspended — [JupyterLab guide](https://datalab.noirlab.edu/docs/manual/UsingAstroDataLab/JupyterNotebooks/JupyterNotebooks.html); [Accounts page](https://datalab.noirlab.edu/docs/manual/UsingAstroDataLab/WebPortal/Accounts/Accounts.html); [disclaimers](https://datalab.noirlab.edu/disclaimers.php)
- **SciServer**: documentation for the MPE-hosted instance says "we don't enforce a specific quota" but monitor fair use; user volumes have `persistent` and `scratch` folders (/workspace/Storage, /workspace/Temporary); that instance migrated from bare metal to a cloud-based setup during 20–24 April 2026 — [SciServer (MPE) FAQ](https://docs.sciserver.mpe.mpg.de/faq.html); [migration guide 2026](https://docs.sciserver.mpe.mpg.de/migration.html)
- **Rubin Science Platform**: only Rubin data-rights holders (all scientists and students in the US and Chile, plus named members of international in-kind teams) may hold an account and access DP1/DP2; DP1 (~3.5 TB) and early DP2 are proprietary for two years; Data Preview 0 is public; prompt alerts and Solar System data at the MPC are public and shareable; quotas "will start being enforced starting with DP1" (amounts not published in the sources found); Rubin's "no reserved science" policy lets people without data rights co-author — [DP1 access](https://dp1.lsst.io/access/index.html); [DP2 access](https://dp2.lsst.io/access/index.html); [recent data releases](https://rubinobservatory.org/for-scientists/data-products/recent-data-releases); [RSP roadmap](https://rsp.lsst.io/roadmap.html); [DP1 paper arXiv:2603.23786](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23786v1); [CASRAI guide (secondary)](https://casrai.org/guides/vera-c-rubin-observatory)
- **AWS Registry of Open Data** (astronomy tag): TESS in `s3://stpubdata/tess` (us-east-1, listable without an AWS account; public domain, attribution required for refereed papers); TESS-Gaia Light Curves in `stpubdata/mast/hlsp/tglc`; ZTF enhanced Objects and Lightcurves tables in `s3://ipac-irsa-ztf` (us-east-1, updated about twice a year); NEOWISE Reactivation data in `s3://nasa-irsa-wise` (us-west-2, updated annually); a Gaia DR3 entry was added in January 2026 — [TESS](https://registry.opendata.aws/mast-tess/); [TGLC](https://registry.opendata.aws/mast-tglc/); [ZTF](https://registry.opendata.aws/ztf/); [NEOWISE-R](https://registry.opendata.aws/wise-neowiser/); [change log](https://registry.opendata.aws/change-log/); [astronomy tag](https://registry.opendata.aws/tag/astronomy/)

### Inferences
- Fornax's credit prices equal typical AWS list prices in dollars (0.023/GB-month for S3-class storage), suggesting roughly one credit ≈ one US dollar (unverified). On that reading: a full 200 GB home directory costs ~216 credits/year (200 × 0.090 × 12), 1 TB of private S3 ~276 credits/year, and 1 TB of scratch ~80 credits/month — so the free 1,000-credit allotment supports about 1–3 TB of working data for a year with modest compute, not more.
- Practical tiering for a solo developer: (1) laptop + DuckDB/Parquet for subsets of 10⁵–10⁷ objects; (2) Fornax or Data Lab notebooks next to the data for ZTF/NEOWISE/TESS-scale scans; (3) anonymous S3 reads of `stpubdata`, `ipac-irsa-ztf`, `nasa-irsa-wise` and data.lsdb.io for everything else. Rubin catalogues are simply unavailable without data rights; only the alert stream via brokers is.
- Hosted platforms are best-effort and policy-bound ("research and education only", suspension rights, account approval). Stargazer should run identically off-platform, with platform use as an optimisation.

### Gaps
- TIKE's CPU/RAM/session limits and current registration rules; Astro Data Lab notebook CPU/RAM limits; JHU SciServer's own quotas and 2026 status; RSP numeric quotas — not found.

- Whether Fornax currently approves independent (unaffiliated) researchers is not stated in the sources found.

## 6. Data volumes, costs and harvesting constraints

### Takeaway
Full public time-domain datasets are far beyond a small team's storage (ZTF DR24: 5.32 billion light curves and ~10¹² detections; Gaia DR4 on 2 Dec 2026: ~400 TB; TESS full-frame products: ~1 TB per sector; Breakthrough Listen: petabytes), but million-object subsets are only gigabytes. Interactive APIs are rate-limited or scoped for small queries, so bulk work must use the archives' bulk Parquet/S3 products.

### Cited Findings
- **ZTF**: DR24 (released 22 Jan 2026) contains 5.32 billion light curves built from single-exposure extractions, 69.7 million single-exposure images and 1,043 billion source detections; DR25 is scheduled for 20 Jan 2027 — [ZTF newsletter](https://www.ztf.caltech.edu/newsletter/HTML/index.html); [ZTF newsletter issue 20](https://sites.astro.caltech.edu/ztf-newsletter/issue-20/index.html); [Fornax forum](https://discourse.fornax.sciencecloud.nasa.gov/t/new-spherex-and-ztf-data-plus-fornax-science-environment-updates/738)
- ZTF bulk format: IRSA offers the Objects table and light curves in Parquet for bulk download; for scale, the DR7 bulk Parquet light curves totalled 3.7 TB; a third-party DR23/DR24 extractor reports 177,218 Parquet files in the DR23 index and five stored values per epoch (hmjd, mag, magerr, clrcoeff, catflags) — [IRSA ZTF mission page](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/Missions/ztf.html); [DR7 release notes](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/ZTF/docs/releases/dr07/ztf_release_notes_dr07.pdf); [ztf_lc_bulk (third party)](https://github.com/vasilybelokurov/ztf_lc_bulk)
- ZTF funding/operations: NSF granted an additional US$1.6 million to continue operations in 2025 and 2026; the ZTF-3 phase runs 1 Jan 2025–31 Dec 2026; the survey was active in mid-2026 (Z+LIONS began 6 Feb 2026); no confirmed funding beyond December 2026 was found — [ZTF newsletter issue 14](https://sites.astro.caltech.edu/ztf-newsletter/issue-14/index.html); [ZTF newsletter index](https://www.ztf.caltech.edu/ztf-newsletter.html); [LIONS scanning log](https://sites.astro.caltech.edu/LIONS/scanning_summary/2026-07.html)

- **Gaia**: DR3 totals 10 TB; DR4 is scheduled for 2 Dec 2026, based on 66 months of data, with epoch photometry (G, BP, RP time series) for all sources; ESA's DR4 content page gives ~400 TB (epoch astrometry alone 62 TB), while 2024–2026 talks quote ~500 TB and >140 tables — [Gaia DR4 content](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/en/web/gaia/dr4); [Gaia release scenario](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/en/web/gaia/release); [workshop abstract, Jan 2026](https://indico.icc.ub.edu/event/675/contributions/4979/)

- **TESS**: all products are public at MAST with no proprietary period; a sector is ~370 GB of raw full-frame images and ~710 GB SPOC-calibrated; 2-minute light curves are ~5–6 GB per sector; QLP released light curves for 9.1 million targets from sectors 27–39 alone; TGLC lists 6,022,929 light curves / 5.1 TB for Sector 40; GSFC-ELEANOR-LITE exceeds 150 million light curves; the T16 project released 83,717,159 light curves for 56,401,549 stars from Cycle 1 — [TESS mission paper arXiv:2410.12905](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12905); [QLP arXiv:2110.05542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05542); [TGLC HLSP](https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/tglc); [GSFC-ELEANOR-LITE HLSP](https://stdatu.stsci.edu/hlsp/gsfc-eleanor-lite); [T16 arXiv:2502.13792](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13792)
- **Rubin**: DP1 is ~3.5 TB; the alert stream is expected to reach up to seven million alerts per night — [DP1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23786v1); [TechTimes (secondary)](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319434/20260701/rubin-observatory-begins-lsst-nightly-7-million-alert-stream-will-test-if-dark-energy-evolves.htm)
- **Breakthrough Listen**: 1 PB in DR1 (2019), nearly 2 PB public by February 2020 — [arXiv:1906.07391](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1906.07391v3); [Green Bank Observatory](https://greenbankobservatory.org/news/breakthrough-listen-releases-2-pb-of-data-from-seti-survey-of-milky-way/)
- Radio deep learning at BL scale: Aetherscan documents measured peaks of ~260 GB RAM (training) and ~215 GB (inference), ~147 GB of training data per retained round (up to ~7.4 TB for 50 rounds) and ~1 GB of cached stamps per cadence, on 5–6 workstation GPUs — [PyPI aetherscan](https://pypi.org/project/aetherscan/)
- **Rate limits and access policies**: Lasair API 10 calls/hour on the shared token, 100/hour with an account, unlimited for approved power users; Fink, Babamul and ANTARES streaming require issued credentials; ANTARES retired its v1 API on 4 Nov 2026 — [PyPI lasair](https://pypi.org/project/lasair/); [PyPI fink-client](https://pypi.org/project/fink-client/); [PyPI babamul](https://pypi.org/project/babamul/); [PyPI antares-client](https://pypi.org/project/antares-client/)
- An LSST community thread on bulk downloads reports ~8 Gbit/s sustained from IPAC for ZTF images, with throughput limited by a concurrent-query quota (anecdotal) — [community.lsst.org thread](https://community.lsst.org/t/guidance-on-bulk-downloads/12352)
- Funding risk context: the FY2026 President's Budget Request proposed a 47% cut to NASA science and steep cuts to Green Bank Observatory ($10M→$3M) and NOIRLab's Community Science and Data Center line (−74.7%); Congress rejected most of this in January 2026 (NASA science $7.25 billion, −1.1%) — [AAS, FY26 PBR details](https://aas.org/posts/news/2025/06/fy26-presidents-budget-request); [AAS, Jan 2026](https://aas.org/posts/news/2026/01/congress-passes-fiscal-year-2026-spending-bills-nsf-nasa-and-doe); [Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/articles/advocacy-success-fy2026-nasa-budget)

### Inferences
- ZTF DR24 averages ~196 detections per light curve (1,043 billion / 5.32 billion). At ~22 bytes per detection for the five stored values, 1 million light curves ≈ 4 GB uncompressed, 100 million ≈ 430 GB, and the whole release ≈ 23 TB uncompressed (Parquet compression should reduce this severalfold; DR7 was 3.7 TB). These are back-of-envelope estimates, not published sizes.
- All TESS 2-minute light curves to date are a few hundred GB (5–6 GB × number of sectors); full-frame-image light-curve collections (TGLC at ~5 TB per sector) are not download targets — read them from `stpubdata` in us-east-1.
- Gaia DR4 (2 Dec 2026, ~40× DR3 by volume, epoch photometry for every source) arrives weeks after this review and will immediately become the largest uniform all-sky time-series set; Stargazer's Gaia connector should be written against DR3 but parameterised for DR4 table names, and should plan on TAP/DataLink (5,000 sources per request → 200 requests per million sources) or cloud mirrors rather than bulk download.
- Running cost for a realistic first year: essentially zero if work stays within free platforms and laptop-scale subsets; of order a few hundred US dollars per TB-year if derived data are parked in commercial object storage (using the Fornax price list as a proxy for AWS list prices).
- Broker REST APIs (e.g. Lasair's 100 calls/hour) are for candidate enrichment, not harvesting; design connectors with a persistent on-disk cache, per-host rate limiters, and a "bulk first" path.
- ZTF's confirmed funding ends 31 Dec 2026 and US facility budgets were under serious threat in 2025; pin data-release versions (DR24) and cache what the analysis depends on rather than assuming live services persist.

### Gaps
- MAST, SIMBAD/VizieR, HEASARC and IRSA numeric rate limits or bulk-harvest policies were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- Total on-disk size of ZTF DR24 light-curve Parquet, the Gaia DR3 epoch-photometry subset size, the total number of TESS sectors/light curves to date, and the current BL archive size were not found.
- Actual 2026 AWS/GCP storage, compute and egress prices were not retrieved.

## 7. Engineering patterns and lessons

### Takeaway
The recurring lesson from comparable projects is that detection is easy and vetting is the whole problem: every large search produced millions of "hits" and zero survivors, and the projects that stalled had not planned the back end, the sensitivity measurement or the false-positive process up front. The proven patterns are synthetic-signal injection, an explicit verification checklist, provenance for every run, human review in a purpose-built viewer, and immediate independent-data cross-checks before any public statement.

### Cited Findings
- **Candidate funnels (how hits collapse)**: SETI@home ~12 billion detections → ~1 million candidates → ~100 for FAST re-observation — [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/01/14/setihome-didnt-find-aliens-but-was-it-a-distributed-computing-success/); ATA on 3I/ATLAS ~74 million narrowband signals → ~200 for review → 0 — [SETI Institute](https://www.seti.org/news/seti-institute-looks-for-signs-of-technology-in-interstellar-visitor-3iatlas/); UCLA SETI 82 million candidate signals from 55,000 stars — [Zooniverse](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/ucla-seti-group/are-we-alone-in-the-universe/about/research); Ma et al. 820 stars → 8 signals of interest — [arXiv:2301.12670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12670); Hephaistos 5 million stars → 7 candidates, all disputed — [arXiv:2405.14921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14921); CRTS ~243,000 quasars → 111 periodic candidates, largely consistent with false positives — [arXiv:1703.10611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10611.pdf); VASCO 600 million sources → no confirmed vanished star — [arXiv:2206.00907](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2206.00907)
- **Synthetic-signal injection**: SETI@home injected "birdies" (3,000 in the final run) to test whether heuristic filters discard real signals and to measure sensitivity — [arXiv:2506.14737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14737); `setigen` injects synthetic narrowband signals into real filterbank frames and whole observing cadences and can synthesise raw voltages — [PyPI setigen](https://pypi.org/project/setigen/); LightCurveLynx simulates optical light curves under realistic survey cadences to "characterize biases and test pipelines" — [PyPI lightcurvelynx](https://pypi.org/project/lightcurvelynx/)
- **Verification framework (BLC1)**: the Proxima "blc1" signal passed BL's ON/OFF filters but was shown to be "an electronically drifting intermodulation product of local, time-varying interferers aligned with the observing cadence"; the team found dozens of similar-morphology signals at harmonically related frequencies, including 36 look-alikes that the pipeline had already discarded as obvious RFI; the paper ends with a ten-part technosignature verification framework to apply to any future signal of interest — [Sheikh et al. 2021, arXiv:2111.06350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06350); [ADS record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5.1153S/abstract); [Centauri Dreams summary](https://www.centauri-dreams.org/2021/10/27/blc1-the-proxima-signal-and-what-we-learned/)
- **Periodicity in red noise (directly relevant to "pulsing quasars")**: Vaughan et al. 2016 showed Gaussian red-noise models frequently produce false periodicity detections, especially at periods comparable to the data length, flagged PG 1302-102 as a possible false positive and stressed calibrating the false-positive rate when a search covers very many targets; re-analysis of the Graham et al. 2015 CRTS sample (111 candidates from ~243,000 quasars) found the false-positive null preferred at ~2.3σ (CRTS) and ~3.6σ (PTF); a 2019 study concluded most candidates from few-cycle, unevenly sampled, seasonally gapped data may be stochastic variability — [arXiv:1606.02620](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/1606.02620); [arXiv:1703.10611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.10611.pdf); [arXiv:1907.06676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.06676)
- **Source confusion in cross-archive mining**: the Hephaistos Dyson-sphere candidates were explained by background dusty galaxies falling in the WISE beam, later confirmed for two candidates with JWST — [arXiv:2405.14921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14921); [Science Times (secondary)](https://www.sciencetimes.com/articles/62255/20260729/jwst-observes-two-promising-dyson-sphere-star-candidates-debunks-them-simple-cosmic-alignment.htm)
- **Replication pressure**: VASCO's 2025 nuclear-test correlation drew a formal critique, a rebuttal and two independent re-analyses within about six months, one of which (an independent automated pipeline) found no significant association — [arXiv:2604.04810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04810); [rebuttal summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.15171); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/?p=597499)
- **Back-end planning**: SETI@home's authors say they had not thought through vetting in the early years, that the Nebula back end came late, and that most SETI searches cannot state their sensitivity; Anderson reportedly concluded a proper sky survey needs dedicated, controlled telescope time — [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-01-enthusiasts-home-scientists-homing.html); [Planetary Perspectives (secondary)](https://planetaryperspectives.substack.com/p/what-setihome-learned-from-its-own)
- **Provenance and reproducibility**: AMPEL records every process execution in its database, tags logs by channel and transient, maps its entities to the IVOA Provenance Data Model, and can replay archived alerts through the same code — [arXiv:1904.05922](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.05922); `seti-tools` keeps a SQLite coverage database with one row per run and one per target — [Hugging Face mirror](https://huggingface.co/phanerozoic/seti-tools)
- **Caching/workflow tooling available off the shelf**: HTTP response caching (`requests-cache` 1.3.3), checksum-verified data fetching (`pooch` 1.9.0), data/pipeline versioning (`dvc` 3.67.1), workflow engines (`snakemake` 9.27.1), dataframe schema validation (`pandera` 0.36.0), property-based testing (`hypothesis`) — [requests-cache](https://pypi.org/project/requests-cache/); [pooch](https://pypi.org/project/pooch/); [dvc](https://pypi.org/project/dvc/); [snakemake](https://pypi.org/project/snakemake/); [pandera](https://pypi.org/project/pandera/); [hypothesis](https://pypi.org/project/hypothesis/)
- **Candidate databases and vetting interfaces**: SNAD Viewer (web portal for expert inspection of ZTF DR light curves, linked from active-learning sessions) — [arXiv:2410.17142](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17142v2); Fritz/SkyPortal (marshal with user filters, 200+ users) — [SkyPortal slides](https://indico.desy.de/event/38330/contributions/143157/attachments/81930/107824/Skyportal.pdf); TOM Toolkit (target lists, observation requests, data products) — [PyPI tomtoolkit](https://pypi.org/project/tomtoolkit/); Zooniverse branching classifier with 20 RFI classes (UCLA SETI) — [Zooniverse stats](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/ucla-seti-group/are-we-alone-in-the-universe/stats); Lasair lets registered users push annotations back onto objects via Kafka/HTTP — [PyPI lasair](https://pypi.org/project/lasair/)
- **Human-in-the-loop budgets are small**: SNAD's workshop inspected 400 candidates by eye; active-learning forests (AAD, PineForest) exist precisely to spend that budget on the most informative objects — [arXiv:2507.06217](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06217); [PyPI coniferest](https://pypi.org/project/coniferest/)
- **Why comparable efforts stalled or ended**: ATA hibernated in 2011 for lack of operating funds — [Scientific American blog](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/budget-crunch-mothballs-telescopes-built-to-search-for-alien-signals/); SETILive ended live operations in October 2014 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SETILive); the SETI League and Project Argus are winding down, with the editorial's author describing dismantling his own station before retiring — [SETI League editorial](https://setileague.org/editor/FN11lh.htm); UCLA SETI's and Planet Hunters TESS's Zooniverse projects are idle for lack of new data — [UCLA SETI project page](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/ucla-seti-group/are-we-alone-in-the-universe); [PHT FAQ](https://zooniverse.org/projects/nora-dot-eisner/planet-hunters-tess/about/faq)
- **Software rot signals**: gatspy (last release 2016), pyinform (2019), astrobase (2021), turbo_seti and blimpy on PyPI (2022), hipscat renamed to hats (2024), ANTARES v1 API retired (Nov 2026), astro-datalab client pinned to Python <3.12 — [gatspy](https://pypi.org/project/gatspy/); [pyinform](https://pypi.org/project/pyinform/); [astrobase](https://pypi.org/project/astrobase/); [turbo_seti](https://pypi.org/project/turbo-seti/); [hipscat](https://pypi.org/project/hipscat/); [antares-client](https://pypi.org/project/antares-client/); [astro-datalab](https://pypi.org/project/astro-datalab/)

### Inferences
- Design the vetting half first. A minimal Stargazer should ship, from the first release: an injection–recovery harness per detector, a per-run coverage/provenance table (inputs, data-release versions, code version, parameters, random seeds), a candidate table with an explicit status lifecycle, and a written verification checklist adapted from the BLC1 framework to optical/IR/X-ray data (instrument artefact? neighbour contamination at the coarsest resolution? present in an independent survey? consistent with a red-noise null? known variable class? look-alikes among rejected objects?).
- For quasar "pulsing", the published record says naive periodogram peaks in damped-random-walk light curves are overwhelmingly false positives when periods are comparable to the baseline and thousands of objects are searched; significance must be calibrated by simulating red-noise light curves on the real sampling and by correcting for the number of objects and trial periods. A detector without this will reproduce a known failure.
- Borrow BLC1's most transferable trick: when something survives, search the *rejected* pile for look-alikes; finding them is strong evidence of a systematic.
- Expect adversarial replication of any claim (VASCO, Hephaistos, BLC1). Publishing the candidate list, the code version and the coverage table together is the cheapest way to make that replication constructive.
- Projects end when a single funder, founder or data-preparation step stops. For a one-developer project the mitigations are: no always-on infrastructure, cached and version-pinned inputs, plain file formats (Parquet/SQLite), and documentation sufficient for someone else to rerun a search.

### Gaps
- The ten items of the BLC1 verification framework were not retrieved verbatim.
- How Breakthrough Listen, the SETI Institute and others handled press and embargo around specific candidates (e.g. the leak of BLC1 before analysis was complete) was not searched.
- No documentation was retrieved on caching/provenance practices inside astroquery, lightkurve or the brokers beyond AMPEL.

## 8. Use of AI in such pipelines, 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Supervised and semi-supervised ML (CNN stamp classifiers, feature-based forests, VAE + random forest for radio spectrograms, active-learning anomaly forests) is routine and productive; LLMs have so far been demonstrated on narrow, checkable tasks (explaining image classifications, natural-language-to-SQL for a broker, telescope-control plans with human approval) and as multi-agent "research assistant" systems whose autonomy is still experimental. Documented pitfalls are hallucinated or unverifiable explanations, degraded performance on partial data, opaque latent features, and extreme hardware demands for radio deep learning.

### Cited Findings
- **Radio SETI deep learning**: Ma et al. 2023 used a beta-convolutional VAE with random forests on 820 targets (480+ h, GBT) in a "semi-unsupervised" way, keeping the false-positive rate manageable and yielding 8 signals of interest — [arXiv:2301.12670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12670)
- Aetherscan (2026) turns that prototype into a production pipeline "capable of near real-time inference", trained on ~30 million cadence snippets with a composite loss (reconstruction, KL divergence, true/false clustering); its documented v1.0.0 runs used 6× RTX A4000 for training and 5× RTX PRO 6000 for inference with ~500 GB host RAM, and it supports only NVIDIA GPUs — [PyPI aetherscan](https://pypi.org/project/aetherscan/)
- **Real-time inference at the telescope**: a SETI Institute/Breakthrough Listen/NVIDIA pipeline built on NVIDIA Holoscan handles ~100 Gb/s at the ATA without dedispersion and reports latency 150× faster than real-time production constraints (press releases cite a 600× speed-up: the old pipeline needed ~59 s per 16.3 s of data); the Institute announced a move to NVIDIA IGX Thor in October 2025 — [A&A 2025](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2025/10/aa55217-25/aa55217-25.html); [SpaceConnect (secondary)](https://www.spaceconnectonline.com.au/discovery/6830-ai-delivers-600-fold-speed-breakthrough-in-search-for-signals-from-space); [Astrobiology.com, 28 Oct 2025](https://astrobiology.com/2025/10/28/seti-institute-accelerates-the-search-for-life-beyond-earth-with-nvidia-igx-thor/)
- A 3 Nov 2026 trade article reports NVIDIA and the SETI Institute have begun using agentic AI to operate the radio array in real time; no detection tied to extraterrestrial intelligence is reported and the one confirmed real-time result cited is a Crab pulsar detection — [GCN.com, 3 Nov 2026 (secondary)](https://gcn.com/?p=22979)
- **Citizen-science-to-ML loop**: UCLA SETI uses volunteer RFI classifications to develop AI tools — [Zooniverse research page](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/ucla-seti-group/are-we-alone-in-the-universe/about/research)
- **Optical time-domain ML in production**: ALeRCE's CNN stamp classifier (rotation-invariant, uses image stamps plus alert metadata) and light-curve classifier — [arXiv:2008.03303](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.03303); Fink's community science modules for ML/deep-learning classification — [arXiv:2009.10185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10185); SNAD's active anomaly discovery — [arXiv:2410.17142](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17142v2)
- Pretrained light-curve embeddings are now a pip install: `light-curve` exposes AstraCLR, Astromer2, ATAT and ATCAT models via ONNX; `astromer` 0.1.8 (3 Oct 2025, MIT) is also on PyPI — [PyPI light-curve](https://pypi.org/project/light-curve/); [PyPI astromer](https://pypi.org/project/ASTROMER/)
- Generic time-series foundation models are openly packaged: `chronos-forecasting` 2.3.2 (Apache-2.0), `timesfm` 3.0.2 (Apache-2.0), `momentfm` 0.1.4 (MIT), `uni2ts` 2.0.0 (Apache-2.0) — [chronos-forecasting](https://pypi.org/project/chronos-forecasting/); [timesfm](https://pypi.org/project/timesfm/); [momentfm](https://pypi.org/project/momentfm/); [uni2ts](https://pypi.org/project/uni2ts/)
- **LLMs as classifiers/explainers**: a Nature Astronomy paper (Oct 2025) used Google's Gemini on image triplets from Pan-STARRS, MeerLICHT and ATLAS, reaching ~93% average accuracy while producing textual explanations; explanations were graded on a scale whose lowest score is "completely incorrect/hallucination"; the model's coherence self-scores correlated with correctness; the authors suggest a second LLM to audit the first — [arXiv:2510.06931](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06931); [PMC copy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12708355/)
- **LLMs as interfaces**: an A&A paper describes a natural-language-to-SQL system for the ALeRCE broker, developed and evaluated on 110 NL/SQL pairs, and cites ReplicationBench (a benchmark of whether agents can replicate astronomy papers) — [arXiv:2606.18108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18108)
- **Agents**: StarWhisper turns natural-language requests into telescope control sequences, presents the plan for approval and executes it; Mephisto is a self-improving agent for galaxy SED modelling; cmbagent is a multi-agent system (built on AG2) reported to complete an end-to-end cosmology task "with no human-in-the-loop at any point"; these are covered in a survey of LLM-based scientific agents — [survey arXiv:2503.24047](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.24047); [cmbagent paper arXiv:2507.07257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07257)
- Packaging/licences: `cmbagent` 0.0.1.post63 (last PyPI release 26 Sep 2025, Apache-2.0 per its bundled LICENSE) and `denario` 1.0.1 (4 Nov 2025, GPLv3; "multiagent system designed to be a scientific research assistant", AG2 + LangGraph with cmbagent as analysis back end; paper arXiv:2510.26887) — [PyPI cmbagent](https://pypi.org/project/cmbagent/); [PyPI denario](https://pypi.org/project/denario/)
- The GOATS paper (Gemini/NOIRLab follow-up software for Rubin alerts) proposes — but has not deployed — an AI agent to optimise follow-up configurations; other 2026 position papers discuss "decision-aware AI" for LSST-scale time-domain astronomy and AI/ML opportunities for Rubin DESC — [arXiv:2606.28645](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28645); [arXiv:2606.05285](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05285v1); [arXiv:2601.14235](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.14235)
- Community assessment: a 2025 review concludes LLM agents "are already demonstrating value in well-scoped astronomical tasks" while full autonomy remains distant — [arXiv:2510.10713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10713)
- **Documented pitfalls and safeguards**: lecture notes on agentic scientific systems name hallucination "especially when context is missing or ambiguous" as the main risk for a triage agent and recommend bounded, tool-based agents, unit tests for every tool the agent can call, and benchmarks with hidden labels — [Mahabal, Caltech Ay 119 slides](https://sites.astro.caltech.edu/~george/ay119/Mahabal_AY119_BeyondLLMs.pdf); CNN real/bogus classifiers' "reliance on opaque latent representations hinders interpretability" — [arXiv:2510.06931](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06931); light-curve classifiers degrade substantially on partial light curves (5–20 days after detection) — [arXiv:2511.17564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17564)
- Tooling trend: PyOD 3 advertises LLM-powered model selection and an "agentic workflow for AI agents"; the Model Context Protocol Python SDK is at 2.3.0; a community MCP server for SkyPortal exists — [PyPI pyod](https://pypi.org/project/pyod/); [PyPI mcp](https://pypi.org/project/mcp/); [glama.ai listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/AnastasiaKWei/skyportal-mcp/schema)

### Inferences
- Sensible division of labour for Stargazer: deterministic, tested numerical code produces scores; classical ML (isolation/active forests on `light-curve` features, optionally pretrained embeddings) ranks candidates; an LLM is confined to orchestration, query translation, literature/catalogue cross-checks and drafting candidate dossiers — each through unit-tested tools, with every number in a dossier traceable to a stored computation rather than model text.
- An LLM must never be the arbiter of "anomalous" or "artificial": the one peer-reviewed accuracy figure (~93% on real/bogus image triplets) is far below what a one-in-a-million search needs, and the documented failure mode is confident, incorrect explanation.
- Evaluate any agent step the same way detectors are evaluated: hidden-label benchmarks made of injected signals and known astrophysical look-alikes, rerun on every prompt or model change.
- Radio spectrogram deep learning (Aetherscan-class) is not feasible for a solo developer's hardware; optical light-curve ML is, because feature extraction and forests run on CPUs.
- Since an AI coding assistant will write much of Stargazer, the same pitfalls apply to the codebase itself: prefer thin wrappers over well-tested libraries to newly generated algorithms, and keep regression tests against published results.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2025–2026 evaluation of LLM agents doing light-curve anomaly or technosignature triage was found; details and quantitative evaluations of StarWhisper, Mephisto, cmbagent/Denario, and any LLM-based light-curve classification preprints were not retrieved.
- The agentic-AI-at-ATA report rests on a single trade article; no SETI Institute primary source was retrieved.
- Astronomy-specific MCP servers (for astroquery, ADS, SIMBAD) were not located on PyPI under obvious names; `astro-mcp` exists only as an early alpha (0.0.0a5, March 2025) — [PyPI astro-mcp](https://pypi.org/project/astro-mcp/).

## 9. Licensing, citation and policy

### Takeaway
The SETI community's post-detection rules were overhauled for the first time in 15 years (IAA "2026 Update": verify, be open, do not reply without international consultation), and a quantitative communication tool exists (Rio Scale 2.0) though it was never formally ratified. Data from the main archives are free to use with acknowledgement, but Rubin catalogue products are restricted to data-rights holders for two years, several broker APIs require registration, and a few key libraries are GPL.

### Cited Findings
- **IAA post-detection protocols**: the IAA's "Declaration of Principles Concerning the Conduct of the Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence" has a "2026 Update", described by the IAA as the first major overhaul in over 15 years (original 1989, last revised 2010); it retains the principle that no response to a confirmed detection should be sent until international consultation (e.g. through the United Nations) has taken place — [IAA press release 26PR-01](https://iaaspace.org/wp-content/uploads/iaa/Communication/26PR-01.pdf); [arXiv:2510.14506, "SETI Post-Detection Protocols: Progress Towards a New Version"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14506)
- The IAA SETI Committee site lists a version dated 27 Nov 2025 as the one voted on and describes a streamlined replacement with eight principles; reporting says the IAA Board approved it, that Professor Michael Garrett led the revision, and that a permanent post-detection subcommittee (social science, law, ethics) is being created — [IAA SETI Committee site](https://iaa-seti-committee.github.io/); [SpaceConnect (secondary)](https://www.spaceconnectonline.com.au/discovery/7124-dont-reply-iaa-seti-agree-to-alien-contact-protocols)
- Related community discussion: "SETI Post-Detection Futures: Directions for Technosignature Research and Readiness" — [arXiv:2507.11587](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11587)
- **Rio Scale 2.0** (Forgan et al. 2019, Int. J. Astrobiology, DOI 10.1017/S1473550418000162): a questionnaire-based 0–10 score meant to calibrate public and media expectations; the authors urge all SETI groups to assign every signal a value and revise it as analysis proceeds; they state no signal, including the Wow! signal, scores above 1; it was submitted to the IAA Permanent Committee on SETI for ratification, a critic later noted it had not been adopted, and its subjectivity has been criticised (Traphagan) and defended — [St Andrews record](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/rio-20-revising-the-rio-scale-for-seti-detections/); [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1017%2FS1473550418000162); [authors' rebuttal](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5F685C0DAAD388D65973FB487EDBC09C/S1473550418000435a.pdf/rebuttal-to-deconstructing-the-rio-scale-problems-of-subjectivity-and-generalization.pdf)
- **Verification before announcement**: BL's ten-part technosignature verification framework was published with the BLC1 analysis — [arXiv:2111.06350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06350)
- Community norms on over-claiming are enforced in print in neighbouring fields too, e.g. "K2-18b Does Not Meet The Standards of Evidence For Life" — [arXiv:2508.05961](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05961)

### Inferences
- Stargazer should encode disclosure policy in the tool: candidates start as "unverified anomaly", carry a verification checklist and a Rio-style score, and the documentation should state that outputs are leads for follow-up, that verification with independent data and independent observers precedes any public claim (IAA principles), and that the tool never frames an anomaly as a detection.
- Generate acknowledgements automatically: each run's provenance record should list the archives, data releases and software versions used and emit the required acknowledgement/citation text, since requirements differ per archive and per library.
- Choose the project licence with the GPL dependencies in mind (see §4); a split like MIT/BSD or GPL for code and CC BY 4.0 for documentation and result tables follows existing precedent.
- Do not redistribute restricted data (Rubin DP products) or broker-credentialed streams in the repository or in cached test fixtures; public alerts, ZTF/TESS/Gaia/NEOWISE releases and derived features are safe to cache with attribution.

## KQ1 — How do quasars normally vary? (DRW/CAR(1), deviations, structure functions, PSD slopes, CARMA/DHO, other bands, causality limit)

### Takeaway
Quasars do not pulse: their emission in every band is aperiodic red noise, approximated in the optical (months to years) by a damped random walk with rest-frame damping time of order 100–1000 days and asymptotic amplitude of roughly 0.1–0.3 mag, but with documented departures (steeper-than-f⁻² spectra on short timescales, τ biased low by short baselines, long-term trends, better fits from damped-harmonic-oscillator/CARMA models). A search module must use this stochastic process, not white noise, as its null hypothesis, and should treat variability faster than the light-crossing time (R_g/c ≈ 4.9 s per 10⁶ M☉) as suspicious only after relativistic beaming is excluded.

### Cited Findings

**DRW / CAR(1) model and typical optical parameters**
- [R] Kelly, Bechtold & Siemiginowska (2009) introduced the continuous-time first-order autoregressive process, CAR(1), equivalent to a damped random walk (DRW), for ~100 quasar light curves; the model has a relaxation time τ and a driving-noise amplitude, and they reported that τ correlates with black-hole mass and luminosity — [Kelly et al. 2009, arXiv:0903.5315](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.5315)
- [R] Kozłowski et al. (2010) confirmed the DRW description on ~2,700 OGLE quasars behind the Magellanic Clouds — [Kozłowski et al. 2010, arXiv:0909.1326](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.1326)
- [V] MacLeod et al. (2010) modelled ~9,000 spectroscopically confirmed SDSS Stripe 82 quasars (~2.7 million photometric points over ~10 yr) as DRWs and found the model reproduces light curves at a fidelity of 0.01–0.02 mag — [MacLeod et al. 2010, arXiv:1004.0276](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0276)
- [V] DRW structure function: SF(Δt) = SF∞·(1 − e^(−|Δt|/τ))^(1/2) with SF∞ = √2·σ; the process is an ordinary random walk for Δt < τ and has a flat power spectrum for Δt > τ — [MacLeod et al. 2010, arXiv:1004.0276](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0276)
- [V] Parameter dependences in MacLeod et al. (2010): SF∞ rises with black-hole mass (power-law index 0.18 ± 0.03) and falls with luminosity and rest wavelength; τ rises with black-hole mass (index 0.21 ± 0.07) and with rest wavelength (index 0.17), and is nearly independent of redshift and luminosity; variability amplitude is anti-correlated with Eddington ratio — [MacLeod et al. 2010, arXiv:1004.0276](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0276)
- [R] MacLeod et al. (2010) scaling relation (widely reused in LSST simulations): log f = A + B·log(λ_RF/4000 Å) + C·(M_i + 23) + D·log(M_BH/10⁹ M☉), with (A, B, C, D) = (−0.51, −0.479, 0.131, 0.18) for f = SF∞ [mag] and (2.4, 0.17, 0.03, 0.21) for f = τ [rest-frame days]; i.e. τ ≈ 250 d and SF∞ ≈ 0.31 mag at 4000 Å for M_i = −23, M_BH = 10⁹ M☉ (the D values and the τ wavelength index are confirmed by the search extract; A, C and the SF∞ wavelength index are recalled) — [MacLeod et al. 2010, arXiv:1004.0276](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0276)
- [V] In Stripe 82 the typical observed-frame τ is ~301 d against a typical light-curve length of ~2,800 d (ratio ~11%), so τ and SF∞ are individually poorly constrained; the combination SF∞/√τ is the more robust quantity — [Guo et al. 2017, arXiv:1709.05271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05271)
- [V] A review by Ivezić & MacLeod notes that the power-law rise of the quasar structure function cannot be extrapolated beyond a few years — [Ivezić & MacLeod 2014, arXiv:1312.3966](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3966)
- [V] Kozłowski (2017): the light curve must be at least 10× longer than the decorrelation timescale, otherwise fitted τ is underestimated and simply tracks the survey length — [Kozłowski 2017, A&A 597, A128](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A%26A...597A.128K/abstract)
- [V] Kozłowski (2021): the variance of light curves shorter than ~30 τ is smaller than that of the input process, so ~30 τ is the stricter length guideline — [Kozłowski 2021, arXiv:2109.03896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03896)
- [V] Stone et al. (2022): 190 Stripe 82 quasars with ~20-yr (1998–2020) SDSS + Pan-STARRS1 + DES light curves (~200 nightly epochs per filter); the median rest-frame g-band τ_DRW reaches ~750 d and keeps rising with baseline; some objects show gradual long-term trends, so either DRW fits need very long baselines to converge or the process is more complex than one DRW — [Stone et al. 2022, arXiv:2201.02762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02762)
- [V] Burke et al. (2021, Science): optical damping timescales of 67 AGN correlate with black-hole mass across the whole supermassive range, match the expected thermal timescale at the UV-emitting disk radius, and accreting white dwarfs fall near the same relation; typical values are several hundred days — [Burke et al. 2021, arXiv:2108.05389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05389)
- [R] Burke et al. (2021) fitted relation: τ_damping ≈ 107 d × (M_BH/10⁸ M☉)^0.38 (rest frame) — [Burke et al. 2021, arXiv:2108.05389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05389)
- [R] Suberlak, Ivezić & MacLeod (2021) combined SDSS and Pan-STARRS1 to lengthen Stripe 82 baselines to ~15 yr and re-derived DRW parameters, confirming the baseline sensitivity of τ — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Improving%20Damped%20Random%20Walk%20Parameters%22)
- [R] Lewis & Brewer (2023, Nature Astronomy) detected cosmological time dilation in the variability of 190 quasars, with timescales stretching as (1+z)^n, n = 1.28 (+0.28/−0.29), consistent with n = 1 — [Lewis & Brewer 2023, arXiv:2306.04053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04053)

**Deviations from the DRW**
- [V] Kepler: four type 1 AGN showed unbending PSD slopes of about −2.6 to −3.3 (Mushotzky et al. 2011), steeper than the DRW high-frequency slope of −2; Smith et al. (2018) analysed 21 type 1 AGN, found slopes generally steeper than the DRW allows, and six objects with PSD flattening at characteristic timescales that roughly correlate with black-hole mass — [Smith et al. 2018, arXiv:1803.06436](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06436)
- [V] Kasliwal, Vogeley & Richards (2015) also found Kepler AGN light curves inconsistent with a pure DRW; Aranzana et al. (2018) found a wide range of slopes in Kepler/K2, and blazars show shallower slopes (Wehrle et al. 2013) — [Kasliwal et al. 2015, MNRAS 451, 4328](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.4328K); [Smith et al. 2018, arXiv:1803.06436](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06436)
- [V] Space-photometry systematics can mimic AGN variability: a previously unidentified Kepler/K2 instrumental signature was confused with AGN variability and is not removed by standard pipelines — [arXiv:2012.14452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14452)
- [R] Zu et al. (2013) tested alternative covariance kernels on OGLE quasars: DRW is adequate on month-to-year scales, with hints of less power on short timescales — [Zu et al. 2013, arXiv:1202.3783](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3783)
- [V] For the individual object PG 1302−102, a Bayesian analysis disfavoured the plain DRW red-noise model at >99.9% credibility — [Zhu & Thrane 2020, arXiv:2004.10944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10944)

**Higher-order CARMA and damped-harmonic-oscillator (DHO) models**
- [R] Kelly et al. (2014) generalised CAR(1) to CARMA(p,q) processes fitted with an O(N) Kalman-filter likelihood (the `carma_pack` code) — [Kelly et al. 2014, arXiv:1402.5978](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5978)
- [V] Moreno et al. (2019) "handbook": higher-order models such as the DHO = CARMA(2,1) are sensitive to departures from a single-slope power law, unlike the DRW whose PSD slope is fixed — [Moreno et al. 2019, Drexel record](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Stochastic-Modeling-Handbook-for-Optical-AGN/991019169796204721)
- [V] Yu et al. (2022) fitted 12,714 Stripe 82 quasars as CARMA(2,1)/DHO; after wavelength correction the DHO amplitude anti-correlates with Eddington ratio and black-hole mass — [Yu et al. 2022, arXiv:2201.08943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08943)
- [V] **[2025]** Yu et al. (2025) fitted 21,767 Stripe 82 quasars with 22-yr SDSS + PS1 + ZTF light curves; the best-fit DHO describes the variability better than the best-fit DRW — [Yu et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.12076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12076)

**X-ray, radio, gamma-ray and blazar behaviour**
- [V] X-ray PSDs show a bend whose timescale grows with black-hole mass and, at fixed mass, shrinks with accretion rate (McHardy et al. 2006); the same spectral-timing behaviour is seen in Cyg X-1 — [review "Eight powers of ten: similarities in black hole accretion on all mass scales", arXiv:0706.3838](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3838); [Papadakis et al. 2009, arXiv:0910.5365](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5365)
- [R] McHardy et al. (2006) fit: log T_B[days] = 2.1·log(M_BH/10⁶ M☉) − 0.98·log(L_bol/10⁴⁴ erg s⁻¹) − 2.32, i.e. T_B ∝ M^1.1/ṁ_Edd (a search extract printed the mass exponent with the wrong sign; the form above is the published one as recalled) — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Active%20galactic%20nuclei%20as%20scaled-up%20Galactic%20black%20holes%22)
- [V] **[2025]** A 2025 review summarises optical-UV and X-ray continuum variability results and open problems — [arXiv:2506.23899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23899)
- [V] Gamma-ray blazar light curves have PSDs "essentially consistent with red noise alone" in a homogeneous re-analysis of claimed periodic sources — [Covino, Sandrinelli & Treves 2019, arXiv:1810.02409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02409)
- [V] Radio (15 GHz) blazar light curves are also red noise; tests of sinusoidal claims are framed as rejecting "random fluctuations in the red noise tail of the power spectral density" — [Kiehlmann et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09647)
- [R] Radio intraday variability of compact flat-spectrum quasars is largely interstellar scintillation, a propagation effect with an annual modulation of the variability timescale (e.g. J1819+3845; MASIV survey) — [ADS title search: J1819+3845](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Interstellar%20scintillation%20as%20the%20origin%20of%20the%20rapid%20radio%20variability%22); [ADS search: MASIV](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22MASIV%22%20author%3A%22Lovell%22)
- [V] Large-amplitude "normal" behaviour: ~10% of quasars vary by >1 mag in g over ~15 yr (extreme-variability quasars), with an intrinsic fraction of ~30–50% among g < 22 quasars once selection is corrected — [Rumbaugh et al. 2018, arXiv:1706.07875](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07875)

**Shortest timescales and the causality (light-crossing) argument**
- [R] TeV flux doubling in ~200 s in PKS 2155−304 (2006) — far shorter than the horizon light-crossing time of a ~10⁹ M☉ black hole, requiring very high Doppler factors or sub-horizon emitting regions — [Aharonian et al. 2007, arXiv:0706.0797](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0797)
- [R] IC 310: TeV doubling times below 4.8 min, about 20% of the event-horizon light-crossing time, interpreted as sub-horizon structures (magnetospheric gaps) — [Aleksić et al. 2014, arXiv:1412.4936](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4936)
- [R] 3C 279: GeV flux doubling in under ~5 min during the June 2015 outburst — [Ackermann et al. 2016, arXiv:1605.05324](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.05324)
- [V] **[2025]** 1ES 1927+654: an X-ray QPO whose period fell from ~18 min to 7.1 min in two years corresponds to coherent motion on scales under 10 gravitational radii — [Masterson et al. 2025, arXiv:2501.01581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01581)

### Inferences
- Computed from GM☉/c³ = 4.93 µs: R_g/c = 4.9 s, 49 s, 8.2 min, 1.4 hr, 13.7 hr for M_BH = 10⁶, 10⁷, 10⁸, 10⁹, 10¹⁰ M☉; Schwarzschild-radius crossing is twice that; the Schwarzschild ISCO orbital period (92.3 GM/c³) is 7.6 min, 1.3 hr, 12.6 hr, 5.3 d, 53 d for the same masses (shorter by up to ×7 for a maximally spinning hole). Observed timescales scale as (1+z)/δ, where δ is the Doppler factor (δ ~ 10–50 in blazars), so the causality bound is only a strong anomaly test for unbeamed (radio-quiet) quasars.
- Using the MacLeod scaling, a DRW with τ = 250 d and SF∞ = 0.31 mag gives expected rms differences of ~0.02 mag at 1 day, ~0.06 mag at 10 days, ~0.10 mag at 30 days, ~0.18 mag at 100 days and ~0.27 mag at 1 year (rest frame). Intrinsic "noise" is therefore weakest at high frequency and dominates on year scales, which is exactly where survey periodicity claims have been made.
- The Burke relation implies τ_damping ≈ 19, 45, 107, 257, 616 d for 10⁶–10¹⁰ M☉ (rest frame). With the ≥10 τ rule, a 10⁹ M☉ quasar at z = 1.5 needs ≳ 10 × 257 × 2.5 ≈ 6,400 d ≈ 17.6 yr of observed baseline before its DRW parameters are trustworthy; most single-survey light curves fail this.
- Because Kepler-quality data show high-frequency slopes of −2 to −3.5 while ground-based multi-year data look DRW-like, a robust null model needs a free high-frequency slope or a second timescale (bending power law, DHO/CARMA(2,1)), and the null-model choice should be treated as a systematic (run at least two nulls).
- All timescales should be analysed in the rest frame (divide by 1+z); the time-dilation detection supports doing so.

### Gaps
- Typical quantitative PSD slopes and amplitudes in radio (OVRO 15 GHz), gamma-ray (Fermi-LAT) and X-ray bands could not be re-verified (search budget exhausted); published values recalled but not confirmed are: gamma-ray blazar PSD indices ≈ 1–1.5, radio ≈ 2, X-ray high-frequency ≈ 2 bending to ≈ 1.
- The exact Burke et al. (2021) coefficients, the MacLeod A/C coefficients and the McHardy plane coefficients are recalled, not re-read.
- No verified source was retrieved for typical intranight optical microvariability amplitudes of blazars or for the fraction of flat-spectrum radio sources showing scintillation-driven intraday variability.

## KQ2 — Which periodicity claims exist and what happened to them?

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 no quasar periodicity is accepted as a confirmed supermassive-black-hole binary: the large optical-survey samples (CRTS 111, PTF 33, Pan-STARRS 26, Gaia 181) have mostly failed extended-baseline, out-of-sample or stricter statistical tests, and the flagship PG 1302−102 remains contested. The claims that have held up best over many cycles are jet-dominated blazars (PKS 2131−021 with a 47.9-yr coherent radio sinusoid; PG 1553+113 with a ~2.2-yr gamma-ray cycle tracked for 17 yr), while OJ 287's binary model is disputed after the 2021–2022 tests and the "Tick-Tock" merger forecast failed.

### Cited Findings

**PG 1302−102**
- [R] Graham et al. (2015, Nature) reported a 1,884 ± 88 d (≈5.2 yr observed) near-sinusoidal optical modulation of amplitude ~0.14 mag in this z = 0.278 quasar and proposed a sub-parsec binary — [Graham et al. 2015a, arXiv:1501.01375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01375)
- [V] Vaughan et al. (2016): a few thousand simulations of steep-spectrum red noise readily produce few-cycle "periodicity" like PG 1302−102's; a Bayesian comparison preferred a simple DRW over a sinusoid; false-positive rates must be calibrated when 10⁵–10⁷ targets are searched — [Vaughan et al. 2016, arXiv:1606.02620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02620)
- [V] Liu, Gezari & Miller (2018): adding ASAS-SN photometry, the CRTS-derived periodic model is not consistent with the newer data; persistence of the periodicity is not supported and the binary model is disfavoured — [Liu, Gezari & Miller 2018, arXiv:1803.05448](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05448)
- [V] Zhu & Thrane (2020) reached the opposite emphasis: Bayes factor >10⁶ for quasi-periodicity, with the data slightly favouring a quasi-periodic oscillation over a strict sinusoid, and plain DRW disfavoured at >99.9% — [Zhu & Thrane 2020, arXiv:2004.10944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10944)
- [R] D'Orazio, Haiman & Schiminovich (2015) explained the modulation as relativistic Doppler boosting and showed archival UV amplitudes about 2–2.6× the optical, as that model predicts; Xin et al. (2020) added nine simultaneous Swift epochs and found B-band/UV behaviour roughly consistent but the joint analysis including V band disfavouring the model — verdict inconclusive — [D'Orazio et al. 2015, arXiv:1509.04301](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.04301); [Xin et al. 2020, arXiv:1907.11246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11246)

**Systematic optical searches**
- [R]/[V] CRTS: Graham et al. (2015b) searched 243,500 spectroscopically confirmed quasars over a 9-yr baseline, requiring ≥1.5 cycles, and published 111 candidates (secondary sources quote "~245,000"/"≈250k" quasars and confirm the 111) — [Graham et al. 2015b, arXiv:1507.07603](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07603); [D'Orazio & Charisi 2023 review, arXiv:2310.16896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16896)
- [V] PTF: Charisi et al. (2016) searched ~35,000 quasars (35,383 as recalled), initially reported 50 candidates, of which 33 remained significant after adding CRTS data — [Charisi et al. 2016, arXiv:1604.01020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01020); [D'Orazio & Charisi 2023, arXiv:2310.16896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16896)
- [V] **PTF re-assessment**: Robnik et al. (2024) re-analysed the 33 PTF candidates with "null-signal templates" and a Bayes-factor approach and found no statistically significant periodic detections in the PTF data — [Robnik et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.17565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17565)
- [V] Pan-STARRS1 Medium Deep Survey, field MD09: three candidates were found and none persisted when the baseline was extended to 7–14 yr — [Liu et al. 2016, arXiv:1609.09503](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09503)
- [V]/[R] Pan-STARRS1 MDS, full survey: ~9,000 colour-selected quasars yielded 26 initial candidates with >1.5 cycles; after extended-baseline imaging and a maximum-likelihood test against a DRW, one statistically significant candidate remained (the "26 from ~9,000" is confirmed by search; the "one survivor" is recalled from the abstract) — [Liu et al. 2019, arXiv:1906.08315](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08315); [D'Orazio & Charisi 2023, arXiv:2310.16896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16896)
- [R] The first PS1 candidate, PSO J334.2028+01.4075 (542 ± 15 d, z = 2.06), lost significance with extended data — [Liu et al. 2015, arXiv:1503.02083](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02083)
- [V] DES + SDSS: Chen et al. (2020) searched 625 quasars (median z = 1.8) in a 4.6 deg² overlap of DES-SN fields and Stripe 82 with 20-yr griz light curves and found five candidates with observed periods of ~3–5 yr (1–2 yr rest frame) spanning ~4–6 cycles; if all are real the detection rate is ~0.8% or ~1.1 per deg² — [Chen et al. 2020, arXiv:2008.12329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12329)
- [V]/[R] One of these, J0252 (Liao et al. 2021), was argued to match bursty circumbinary-accretion variability better than Doppler boosting (title confirmed; z = 1.53 and a ~4.4-yr observed period are recalled) — [Liao et al. 2021, NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10225457)
- [V] ZTF: Chen et al. (2022 preprint; MNRAS 2024) searched 143,700 quasars (SDSS and Véron-Cetty & Véron catalogues), started from 184 candidates and retained 106 (DRW null) or 86 (single-power-law null) with the most significant periodic variations; the answer depends on the red-noise model (the first preprint version quoted 127, as recalled) — [Chen et al. 2024, arXiv:2206.11497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11497)
- [V] **[2025]** An A&A (2025) catalogue of candidate milliparsec massive black-hole binaries from long-term optical monitoring reports that for 58 objects previously proposed from CRTS data alone, adding ZTF data shows the claimed modulation is due to red noise (authors/title not confirmed in this session) — [A&A 2025, aa52818-24](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2025/07/aa52818-24/aa52818-24.html)
- [V] **[2025]** Gaia DR3: Huijse et al. (2025) compared a sinusoid against a DRW with a Bayes factor (threshold log B > 3.0423) on ~770,000 Gaia AGN and reported 181 new binary candidates — [Huijse et al. 2025, arXiv:2505.16884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16884)
- [V] **[2025]** Follow-up test of those 181 Gaia candidates: in all 116 cases with ZTF coverage, the Gaia-inferred periodic model fails to predict the subsequent variability, which looks stochastic; red-noise contamination is identified as the main source of false detections (paper title: "Active galactic nuclei do not exhibit strictly sinusoidal brightness variations") — [arXiv:2509.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10601)
- [V] **[2026]** Lin, Charisi & Haiman (2026, ApJ 997, 316) state that systematic searches (through Huijse et al. 2025) have revealed over 200 candidates to date, and argue that sinusoid-based searches miss most real binaries — [ISTA record](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/21712); [arXiv:2505.14778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14778)
- [V] Population-level check: assuming the CRTS and PTF candidates are all binaries implies a nanohertz gravitational-wave background above pulsar-timing limits; the "false positives" hypothesis is preferred at ~2.3σ (CRTS) and ~3.6σ (PTF) — [Sesana et al. 2018, arXiv:1703.10611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10611)

**PKS 2131−021 (radio blazar)**
- [V] O'Neill et al. (2022): a 45.1-yr 15 GHz light curve shows two epochs of strong sinusoidal variation with the same period (within ≲2%) and phase (within ~10%) separated by ~20 yr without the signal; significance 4.6σ against red noise; rest-frame period 2.082 ± 0.003 yr at z = 1.285 — [O'Neill et al. 2022, arXiv:2111.02436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02436)
- [V] Kiehlmann et al. (2024 preprint): data through 2023 show coherence over 47.9 yr, P(15 GHz) = 1739.8 ± 17.4 d, red-noise hypothesis rejected at p = 2.09 × 10⁻⁷; the sinusoid is seen from below 2.7 GHz to the optical (P_optical = 1764 ± 36 d) with a smooth monotonic phase shift with frequency, and hints in gamma rays — [Kiehlmann et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09647)
- [R] A second OVRO blazar, PKS J0805−0111, was reported with a highly significant radio sinusoid (rest-frame period ≈1.4 yr as recalled), with the authors estimating roughly 1 in 100 blazars may be binary candidates — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22PKS%20J0805-0111%22)

**PG 1553+113 (gamma-ray blazar)**
- [V] Ackermann et al. (2015): a 2.18 ± 0.08 yr gamma-ray cycle, initially described as of marginal significance, presented with multiwavelength evidence — [Ackermann et al. 2015, INAF record](https://openaccess.inaf.it/handle/20.500.12386/24243)
- [V] Peñil et al. (2022 proceeding): re-analysis of 351 blazars with 12 yr of LAT data lists PG 1553+113 at 2.1 ± 0.2 yr with >5σ, and states that a look-elsewhere correction was applied for global significance (the extract does not make clear whether the 5σ is before or after trials) — [PoS(Gamma2022)094](https://pos.sissa.it/417/094/pdf)
- [V] **[2026]** A 2026 A&A letter cites Peñil et al. (2024) and Abdollahi et al. (2024) as confirming and extending the oscillation; a 17-yr (2008–2025) LAT spectral study of the QPO is the most recent analysis found — [A&A 2026 letter](https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202659239); [arXiv:2603.04527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04527)
- [V] Counterpoint: a homogeneous re-analysis of Fermi blazars with claimed year-scale quasi-periods found "no strong cases" and spectra consistent with red noise — [Covino et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.02409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02409)

**OJ 287 (~12-yr cycle) and the 2021–2026 tests**
- [V] The standard model (Valtonen and collaborators): a ~1.5 × 10⁸ M☉ secondary crosses the accretion disc of a primary of ~1.8 × 10¹⁰ M☉ twice per ~12-yr orbit, producing predictable flares; the 2015 "GR centenary flare" was used to infer the primary's spin — [Valtonen et al. 2016, arXiv:1603.04171](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04171); [Univ. ## KQ3 — Short-timescale and repeating phenomena (X-ray QPOs, QPEs, repeating partial TDEs, blazar QPOs/IDV, changing-look and extreme-variability quasars)

### Takeaway
The closest real analogues of "pulsing" in galactic nuclei are quasi-periodic X-ray eruptions (about a dozen sources, recurrence 2.4 hours to ~4.5 days), a handful of X-ray QPOs (minutes to an hour), and repeating nuclear flares such as ASASSN-14ko (~114 days); none is clock-like, and all have leading natural explanations involving orbiting bodies or disc/jet instabilities around a single massive black hole. These are mostly low-luminosity, low-mass nuclei rather than luminous quasars, and 2025–2026 results (period drifts in both directions, QPEs appearing years after tidal disruptions) are still reshaping the models.

### Cited Findings

**X-ray QPOs in AGN**
- [V] RE J1034+396: a ~1-hour X-ray periodicity with a peak at 3730 ± 130 s (Gierliński et al. 2008); the signal wanders in phase/amplitude/frequency rather than being strictly periodic; its period decreased by ~250 s over 11 yr (Jin et al. 2020) — [Gierliński et al. 2008, arXiv:0807.1899](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.1899); [arXiv:2401.04926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04926)
- [V] **[2025]** A proposed QPO triplet and a ~92.2-day cyclic evolution of the RE J1034+396 QPO, interpreted with relativistic precession (a proposal, not established) — [Xia et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.13173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13173)
- [V] **[2025]** 1ES 1927+654 (Masterson et al., Nature 2025): a millihertz QPO first seen in 2022 at ~18 min shortened to 7.1 min over two years with a decelerating evolution never before seen in SMBH QPOs; orbital-decay models of a stellar-mass companion need stable mass transfer to fit, and instability models do not explain everything — [Masterson et al. 2025, arXiv:2501.01581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01581)
- [R] Other single-epoch or transient X-ray QPO claims include 2XMM J123103.2+110648 (~3.8 hr), Swift J1644+57 (~200 s) and ASASSN-14li (131 s), the latter two after tidal disruption events — [ADS title search: 200-second quasi-periodicity](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22A%20200-Second%20Quasi-Periodicity%22); [ADS title search: loud QPO](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22A%20loud%20quasi-periodic%20oscillation%20after%20a%20star%20is%20disrupted%22)

**Quasi-periodic eruptions (QPEs)**
- [V] GSN 069 was the first: ~1-hour soft X-ray eruptions recurring about every 9 hours (Miniutti et al. 2019; another paper quotes ~8 hours) — [QPE observations review 2026, arXiv:2607.15359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15359); [arXiv:2504.21456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21456); primary (identifier recalled): [Miniutti et al. 2019, arXiv:1909.04693](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04693)
- [V] eROSITA sources: eRO-QPE1 has ~7.6-hr eruptions separated by ~18.5 hr (with overlapping bursts revealed only by continuous NICER coverage); eRO-QPE2 has ~27-min eruptions every 2.4 hr; eRO-QPE3 and eRO-QPE4 followed; the eROSITA survey has yielded five (eRO-QPE1 to eRO-QPE5) — [Arcodia et al. 2024, arXiv:2401.17275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17275); [arXiv:2506.05517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05517); [arXiv:2607.15359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15359)
- [V] Population properties: duty cycle typically ~10–30%, peak X-ray luminosity ≈ 10⁴¹–10⁴³ erg s⁻¹, more than ten sources; a 2025 radio study (Goodwin et al.) counted 12 bona fide QPE sources; targeting late-time TDEs and large-amplitude nuclear flares has yielded three further QPE sources — [arXiv:2607.15359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15359); [arXiv:2506.14417 (identifier as surfaced by search, not opened)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14417)
- [V] QPEs after tidal disruption events: AT2019qiz showed QPEs in late-time Chandra data about four years after the optical TDE — the main evidence tying QPEs to TDE discs — [arXiv:2607.15359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15359); [Nicholl et al. 2024, arXiv:2409.02181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02181)
- [R] Recurrence times recalled for other sources: RX J1301.9+2747 ≈ 3.6–5.6 hr (alternating), AT2019qiz ≈ 48 hr, "Ansky" (ZTF19acnskyy) ≈ 4.5 d — [Giustini et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.08967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08967); [Nicholl et al. 2024, arXiv:2409.02181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02181); [ADS title search: Ansky](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Discovery%20of%20extreme%20quasi-periodic%20eruptions%22)
- [V] **[2026]** Ansky is the most extreme QPE source (longest recurrence and durations); monitoring caught 23 bursts including 19 consecutive ones from January 2025 to January 2026, and gave the first direct period derivative in a QPE source — the period is increasing, Ṗ ≈ (1.7 ± 0.02) × 10⁻² d/d, contrary to simple orbital-decay expectations — [Chakraborty et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.16776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16776); [AAS Nova, 29 Apr 2026](https://aasnova.org/2026/04/29/a-black-holes-puzzling-x-ray-bursts/)
- [V] **[2026]** Ansky also shows UV modulations lagging the X-ray eruptions by ≈0.96 d — first evidence of a delayed UV counterpart — [arXiv:2603.02517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02517)
- [V] **[2026]** New source J2344: thermal flares recurring every ~12 hr and lasting ~2 hr from a nucleus that hosted a TDE ~5 yr earlier; the authors say it challenges current models — [Baldini et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.03932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03932)
- [V] Leading models: an extreme-mass-ratio companion (star or compact object) colliding with the accretion disc twice per orbit, with the disc often supplied by a TDE ("EMRI + TDE = QPE"); alternatives include disc instabilities and repeated mass transfer — [Linial & Metzger 2023, arXiv:2303.16231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16231); [arXiv:2504.21456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21456); reviews: [Kara & García 2025, arXiv:2503.22791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22791); [arXiv:2503.17609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17609)

**Repeating partial tidal disruption events and other repeating nuclear transients**
- [V] ASASSN-14ko: optical flares with mean period P₀ = 114.2 ± 0.4 d and Ṗ = −0.0017 ± 0.0003 (Payne et al. 2021); later fits give Ṗ ≈ −0.0026 and P ≈ 115 d; over 20 flares observed (other candidates have 2–5); gravitational-wave emission falls short of the period decay by orders of magnitude; a repeating partial TDE of a star on a bound orbit is the leading explanation — [Payne et al. 2021, arXiv:2009.03321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03321); [arXiv:2206.11278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11278); [arXiv:2406.03675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03675)
- [R] Other repeating-flare candidates: AT2018fyk (rebrightening after ~1,200 d), AT2022dbl (two flares ~700 d apart), eRASSt J045650.3−203750 (~223 d), AT2020vdq (~2.6 yr), Swift J0230+28 (~3-week X-ray eruptions) — [ADS: AT 2018fyk](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Live%20to%20Die%20Another%20Day%22); [ADS: AT 2022dbl](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22The%20Unluckiest%20Star%22); [ADS: J045650.3-203750](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22eRASSt%20J045650.3-203750%22); [ADS: Swift J0230](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22X-ray%20eruptions%20every%2022%20days%22)

**Blazar quasi-periodic oscillations and intraday variability**
- [V] BL Lacertae, 2020 outburst: ~13-hour quasi-periodic oscillations in optical flux, optical polarisation and gamma rays, attributed to a kink instability triggered where a shock crossed a recollimation shock (Jorstad et al. 2022) — transient, not persistent — [Blazar variability review 2025, arXiv:2511.18975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18975)
- [V] Long-term Fermi-LAT analysis of BL Lac (2008–2021) found no significant long-lived periodicity, and several claimed Fermi periodicities were not confirmed on re-analysis — [arXiv:2511.18975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18975); [Covino et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.02409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02409)
- [V] TESS: tentative 2.8–6.5-day QPOs in five Fermi blazars at ≥3σ by Lomb–Scargle and wavelet methods (and a related three-blazar study); **[2025]** further "probable" TESS QPOs in X-ray-selected blazars, with the caution that red noise mimics periodic signals; **[2025]** transient Fermi-LAT QPOs are being used to search for kink events — [arXiv:2402.04352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04352); [arXiv:2312.14144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14144); [arXiv:2512.11266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11266); [arXiv:2504.10308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10308)
- [R] The first systematic Fermi-LAT periodicity search (Peñil et al. 2020) examined ~2,300 AGN and reported 11 with local significance above 4σ — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Systematic%20search%20for%20gamma-ray%20periodicity%20in%20active%20galactic%20nuclei%22)

**Changing-look and extreme-variability quasars**
- [V] Changing-look AGN divide into changing-obscuration objects (variable line-of-sight column) and changing-state objects (intrinsic accretion changes); events typically take less than a few years in the rest frame, and large photometric change alone does not identify them — [Ricci & Trakhtenbrot 2023, Nature Astronomy](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-023-02108-4); [Graham et al. 2020, arXiv:1905.02262](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02262)
- [V] Extreme-variability quasars (>1 mag): ~10% observed, 30–50% intrinsic over ~15 yr; most are not explained by dust obscuration — [Rumbaugh et al. 2018, arXiv:1706.07875](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07875)
- [V] A 2024 review catalogues the extremes: outbursts, deep fades, changing looks, exceptional spectral states and semi-periodicities — [Komossa et al. 2024, arXiv:2408.00089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00089)
- [R] Rare single flares: 51 major flares among >900,000 CRTS quasars (candidate causes: microlensing, superluminous supernovae, TDEs, accretion instabilities), and slow, blue "hypervariable" nuclei consistent with microlensing by intervening stars — [ADS: major AGN flares](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Understanding%20extreme%20quasar%20optical%20variability%20with%20CRTS%22); [ADS: slow-blue hypervariables](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Slow-blue%20nuclear%20hypervariables%22)

### Inferences
- Coherence is the discriminator: QPEs are "quasi" periodic (alternating long/short intervals, drifting periods of either sign), X-ray QPOs wander in frequency by ~10% or evolve monotonically, blazar QPOs last a handful of cycles. None would pass a strict-clock test, so a strict-clock detection would at least be new phenomenology.
- QPE and QPO timescales are consistent with orbital periods at tens to hundreds of gravitational radii around 10⁵–10⁷ M☉ black holes (e.g. the Schwarzschild ISCO period for 10⁶ M☉ is ~7.6 min), which is why orbital-body models lead.
- Most of these phenomena are X-ray and episodic; optical survey light curves (ZTF, Gaia, CRTS) will essentially never see QPEs or QPOs, but can see repeating optical flares of the ASASSN-14ko type (months) and changing-look transitions (months to years). A "repeating flare" detector (recurrent outbursts with stable spacing) is a distinct and tractable sub-module from a sinusoid detector.
- The 2026 Ansky result (period lengthening) and the 1ES 1927+654 result (period shortening with deceleration) show that period derivatives of both signs occur naturally; period drift alone is not anomalous.

### Gaps
- The exact count of confirmed QPE sources in the July 2026 review, and details of eRO-QPE5 and of QPEs in AT2022upj, were not retrieved (search extracts imply roughly 12 sources).
- Recurrence values for RX J1301.9+2747, AT2019qiz, Ansky and the repeating-TDE candidates are recalled, not re-verified; the outcome of predicted third flares (e.g. AT2022dbl) is unknown.
- No verified numbers were retrieved for optical microvariability duty cycles of blazars or for scintillation-driven radio intraday variability statistics.

## KQ4 — Statistical pitfalls, best practice, and the software actually used

### Takeaway
Red noise concentrates power at the lowest frequencies a light curve can sample, so a few apparent cycles and a strong periodogram peak are the expected outcome of pure noise; standard Lomb–Scargle false-alarm formulas (which assume white noise) are invalid for quasars. Credible practice in 2024–2026 is: fit an explicit stochastic null (DRW and at least one alternative) with Gaussian processes, compare against null-plus-periodic with Bayes factors or likelihood ratios, calibrate on simulations or null-signal templates with the real sampling, apply a trials factor across frequencies and objects, demand many cycles, and test predictions on held-out or later data — and even then expect low completeness (roughly 20–45% for sinusoids, ~1–9% for sawtooth shapes) and false-positive-dominated samples.

### Cited Findings

**Why red noise fakes periodicity, and how many cycles are needed**
- [V] Few-cycle sinusoid-like behaviour appears in "many" of a few thousand simulated steep-spectrum light curves; the false-positive rate must be calibrated for the survey size (10⁵–10⁷ targets); for PG 1302−102 a DRW was preferred to a sinusoid — [Vaughan et al. 2016, arXiv:1606.02620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02620)
- [V] Unmodelled red noise leaks power and mimics spurious quasi-periods; after homogeneous re-analysis no year-scale gamma-ray quasi-periodicity in blazars could be strongly confirmed — [Covino et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.02409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02409)
- [V] Intrinsic noise is "impressively capable at mimicking periodicity, particularly in sparsely sampled or short-baseline time series"; periodic amplitude is overestimated for about two-thirds of simulated binaries; purely photometric periodicity samples with amplitude above 0.1 mag would be overwhelmed by false positives — [Witt et al. 2022, arXiv:2110.07465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07465)
- [R] The early optical searches required only ≥1.5 cycles (CRTS, PTF, PS1); Vaughan et al. and later authors recommend several (commonly quoted as ≳5) cycles before a period is credible — [Graham et al. 2015b, arXiv:1507.07603](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07603); [Vaughan et al. 2016, arXiv:1606.02620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02620)
- [V] The DES/SDSS search emphasised that its five candidates span ~4–6 cycles thanks to a 20-yr baseline — [Chen et al. 2020, arXiv:2008.12329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12329)
- [V] **[2025]** Out-of-sample failure: periodic models fitted to Gaia data failed to predict later ZTF data in 116 of 116 testable candidates — [arXiv:2509.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10601)

**Null-model dependence and Bayesian model comparison**
- [V] The candidate list changes with the red-noise model: 106 candidates under a DRW null versus 86 under a single power law in ZTF — [Chen et al. 2024, arXiv:2206.11497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11497)
- [V] Two Bayesian analyses of the same object disagree: DRW preferred over a sinusoid (Vaughan et al. 2016) versus Bayes factor >10⁶ for quasi-periodicity with the DRW disfavoured (Zhu & Thrane 2020) — [arXiv:1606.02620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02620); [arXiv:2004.10944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10944)
- [V] **[2025]** Gaia search used a sinusoid-versus-DRW Bayes factor with a fixed threshold (log B > 3.0423) — and its candidates still failed external validation — [arXiv:2505.16884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16884); [arXiv:2509.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10601)
- [V] Null-signal templates (Robnik et al. 2024): build a template by stretching/compressing the cycles of the periodic template so that searching with it measures the no-signal response on the real data, avoiding reliance on noise simulations that "do not capture the complexity of the real data"; claimed sensitivity gain over an order of magnitude; result: no significant detection in PTF — [Robnik et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.17565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17565)
- [V] The Witt et al. Bayesian method costs several CPU-hours per source, prohibitive at survey scale; Davis et al. therefore test inexpensive sinusoid fits on millions of simulated LSST light curves — [Davis et al. 2024, arXiv:2311.10851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10851)
- [V] **[2026]** Banaszak et al. compared generalised Lomb–Scargle, a nested Bayesian sampler and the weighted wavelet Z-transform on simulated AGN light curves: the Bayesian sampler detects best; a Fourier false-alarm cut used as triage ahead of the Bayesian stage gives true- and false-positive rates of ~40% and ~0.5%, with ~10–30 hours of run time for ~10⁷ light curves — [Banaszak et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.15379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15379)

**Completeness, pulse shape, sampling and gaps**
- [V] **[2026]** With DRW noise, the Lomb–Scargle periodogram recovers only ~45%, ~24% and ~23% of injected sinusoids in PTF-like, idealised and LSST-like light curves, and ~9%, ~1%, ~1% of sawtooth signals; theory and hydrodynamic simulations predict non-sinusoidal binary pulse shapes, so past searches likely missed most binaries (preprint values differ: 39.1% and 28.1%) — [Lin, Charisi & Haiman 2026, arXiv:2505.14778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14778); [ISTA record](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/21712)
- [V] Gaps exacerbate red-noise false positives in binary searches (space-photometry white paper) — [arXiv:2406.15558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15558)
- [V] DRW parameter recovery itself is biased unless the baseline is ≥10 τ (stricter: ~30 τ) — [Kozłowski 2017](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A%26A...597A.128K/abstract); [Kozłowski 2021, arXiv:2109.03896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03896)
- [V] Host-galaxy contamination raises false-positive rates and degrades parameter recovery in LSST simulations; longer light curves suppress false positives — [Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/the-consequences-of-rubin-observatory-time-domain-survey-design-a/)

**Look-elsewhere effect and expected false-positive rates**
- [V] Chance coincidence is high even for a "confirmation" test: the Doppler-boost UV/optical colour signature arises by chance in ~20% (near-UV) and ~37% (far-UV) of aperiodic control quasars — [Charisi et al. 2018, arXiv:1801.06189](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06189)
- [V] Global significance must include trials: the Fermi 351-blazar re-analysis applies a look-elsewhere correction; the NANOGrav targeted search uses trials-factor accounting and random-target experiments before interpreting its two mild outliers as noise — [PoS(Gamma2022)094](https://pos.sissa.it/417/094/pdf); [NANOGrav 2025, arXiv:2508.16534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16534)
- [V] Population-level sanity check: assuming all candidates are real over-predicts the gravitational-wave background (null preferred at 2.3σ CRTS, 3.6σ PTF) — [Sesana et al. 2018, arXiv:1703.10611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10611)

**Standard methods (primary references)**
- [R] Generalised (floating-mean, error-weighted) Lomb–Scargle periodogram; its analytic false-alarm probabilities assume uncorrelated noise — [Zechmeister & Kürster 2009, arXiv:0901.2573](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.2573); [VanderPlas 2018, arXiv:1703.09824](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09824)
- [R] Weighted wavelet Z-transform (time-localised periodogram for uneven sampling; used with the autocorrelation function in the CRTS search) — [ADS: Foster 1996](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Wavelets%20for%20period%20analysis%20of%20unevenly%20sampled%20time%20series%22)
- [R] Phase dispersion minimisation (shape-agnostic folding statistic) — [ADS: Stellingwerf 1978](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Period%20determination%20using%20phase%20dispersion%20minimization%22)

### Inferences
- Expected false positives scale brutally with sample size. At a per-object false-alarm probability of 1.35 × 10⁻³ ("3σ"), pure noise yields ~48 candidates from 35,383 quasars, ~194 from 143,700, ~329 from 243,500 and ~1,040 from 770,000 — the same order as the published candidate lists. At the Banaszak et al. triage operating point (0.5% false-positive rate), 10⁷ LSST AGN would give ~50,000 false candidates against a 40% true-positive rate, so a second, far stricter stage is unavoidable.
- For fewer than one expected false alarm across a survey, the per-trial threshold must reach ~5σ: with ~50 independent frequencies per object, 143,700 quasars are ~7 × 10⁶ trials (p < 1.4 × 10⁻⁷, 5.1σ); 10⁶ quasars × 100 frequencies need 5.6σ; 10⁷ × 100 need 6.0σ. These thresholds are only meaningful if the per-trial p-values are calibrated against red noise.
- A defensible pipeline for the module, assembled from the cited practice: (1) clean and merge surveys with per-survey offsets, convert to rest frame; (2) fit stochastic nulls (DRW and DHO or bending power law) with a celerite-family GP; (3) fit null + periodic (sinusoid and at least one non-sinusoidal template such as sawtooth or periodic flares); (4) score with a Bayes factor or likelihood ratio and calibrate it empirically with null-signal templates and with simulated light curves drawn from the null posterior on the true sampling; (5) require a minimum number of cycles (≥3 to shortlist, ≥5 to report) and veto periods near 1 yr, the lunar month, and known instrument periods; (6) apply the across-sample trials factor and report expected false positives; (7) hold out the last season or a second survey and test the prediction; (8) run injection–recovery to publish completeness.
- Fourier-based triage followed by GP/Bayesian vetting (Banaszak et al.) is the only approach shown to scale to 10⁷ light curves within tens of hours.
- For the open-source build: celerite2, tinygp, EzTao, Stingray, astropy and dynesty/UltraNest are pip-installable and maintained in 2026; JAVELIN and carma_pack are GitHub-only with compiled dependencies and should be optional; `pip install javelin` installs the wrong package.

### Gaps
- The numerical false-positive rates and the exact cycle-count recommendation inside Vaughan et al. (2016) could not be read (abstract-level extract only); "≳5 cycles" is the commonly quoted figure, not verified text.
- No public repository was identified for the null-signal-template code (Robnik et al. 2024) or for a maintained PSRESP implementation.
- GitHub commit activity for EzTao, JAVELIN, carma_pack, taufit and DELCgen could not be read (the session's GitHub access was limited to the project repository); status above rests on PyPI metadata and third-party listings.
- A paper remembered as a sceptical treatment of AGN periodicity by Barth & Stern (2018) was not found by search and is not cited.

## KQ5 — Independent confirmation tests and what pulsar-timing arrays imply

### Takeaway
Every proposed confirmation test (UV/optical Doppler-boost amplitude ratios, broad-line shifts, self-lensing flares, jet morphology) is itself confounded by ordinary quasar variability, and none has confirmed an optical-survey candidate; the most convincing multi-band evidence is the radio-to-optical coherent sinusoid of PKS 2131−021. Pulsar-timing arrays provide the only independent physical check: population arguments say most CRTS/PTF candidates cannot be real, and NANOGrav's 2025–2026 targeted search of 114 AGN found no continuous-wave signal.

### Cited Findings

**Multi-band consistency and Doppler boosting**
- [V] If the period is orbital, inferred velocities are ~0.1c and optical and UV light curves should vary in tandem with amplitudes set by the spectral slopes. Testing the CRTS/PTF candidates against a 42-quasar aperiodic control sample: for most candidates the hypothesis needs an unusually steep UV spectrum or implausibly large mass/velocity; at most about one-third can be Doppler-modulated binaries; the signature appears by chance in ~20% (near-UV) and ~37% (far-UV) of ordinary quasars — [Charisi et al. 2018, arXiv:1801.06189](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06189)
- [R] The test was introduced for PG 1302−102 (UV amplitude ≈ 2–2.6× optical predicted and seen in archival data); later simultaneous Swift data were inconclusive — [D'Orazio et al. 2015, arXiv:1509.04301](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.04301); [Xin et al. 2020, arXiv:1907.11246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11246)
- [V] PKS 2131−021 passes a strong multi-band test: the same period from 2.7 GHz to optical with a smooth frequency-dependent phase shift — [Kiehlmann et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09647)

**Emission-line, lensing and morphology tests**
- [V] Broad emission-line profiles of periodic candidates were examined in 2019 and "raise a challenge to the Doppler boost hypothesis" for some of them — [arXiv:1912.00560](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00560)
- [V] In Tick-Tock, spectroscopy shows a broad Hα with a central component blueshifted by ~10³ km s⁻¹ and a broader double-peaked component — complex, not a clean binary signature — [Hoshi et al. 2024, PASJ 76, 103](https://dbpia.nl.go.kr/pasj/article/76/1/103/7517615)
- [R] Self-lensing: an edge-on binary should produce periodic, symmetric, achromatic flares; "Spikey" (a Kepler AGN) showed one such flare and was modelled as a ~3 × 10⁷ M☉ binary with predicted repeats — [Hu et al. 2020, arXiv:1910.05348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05348)
- [R] Long-term radial-velocity monitoring of quasars with offset broad lines has not produced a confirmed binary — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22A%20large%20systematic%20search%20for%20close%20supermassive%20binary%20and%20rapidly%20recoiling%20black%20holes%22)
- [V] Radio-jet arguments cut both ways: the Valtonen group reads a secondary jet in OJ 287's RadioAstron image, while others explain OJ 287's variability by jet precession — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Identifying%20the%20Secondary%20Jet%20in%20the%20RadioAstron%20Image%20of%20OJ%20287%22); [Britzen et al. 2023, arXiv:2307.05838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05838)

**Pulsar-timing-array constraints**
- [V] Sesana et al. (2018): the gravitational-wave background implied by the CRTS sample exceeded then-current limits by almost an order of magnitude (preprint), and by a factor 2–3 for PTF; after correcting virial-mass bias the tension eased but remained; reconciling requires masses overestimated by ≳4× or mass ratios q < 0.01; eccentricity or gas coupling only buys a factor ~2 — [Sesana et al. 2018, arXiv:1703.10611](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10611)
- [V] A parallel argument limits the binary fraction of periodic gamma-ray blazars — [Holgado et al. 2018, arXiv:1806.11111](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.11111)
- [V] NANOGrav 15-yr all-sky search for individual binaries: weak evidence for two candidate signals (~4 nHz and ~170 nHz), the latter discounted (no plausible host, unlikely priors) — [NANOGrav 2023, arXiv:2306.16222](https://inspirehep.net/arxiv/2306.16222)
- [V] The observed background amplitude and shape can be reproduced by binary population models, but only with many parameters at the edges of expected ranges or a few notably different from standard expectations — [NANOGrav 15-yr "Constraints on Supermassive Black Hole Binaries from the Gravitational-wave Background", METU record](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/f3387d58-31bf-4316-ae31-e8b65808b2fd/the-nanograv-15-yr-data-set-constraints-on-supermassive-black-hole-binaries-from-the-gravitational-wave-background)
- [R] The NANOGrav 15-yr background amplitude is A ≈ 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ at 1 yr⁻¹, higher than the ≲1 × 10⁻¹⁵ upper limits assumed in 2018 — [Agazie et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.16213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16213)
- [V] **[2025–2026]** NANOGrav targeted search (ApJL 998, 2026): first targeted continuous-wave searches of 114 AGN that may host binaries; electromagnetic priors improve strain/chirp-mass limits by a median factor 2.2 over all-sky limits; the Bayes factor disfavours a signal for all targets (mean 0.73 ± 0.32); two targets with Bayes factors slightly above one are consistent with noise after coherence tests, random-target experiments and trials accounting; constraints on 3C 66B tightened — [NANOGrav 2025, arXiv:2508.16534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16534)
- [V] **[2025]** A separate targeted search for a binary in 3C 66B used NANOGrav 15-yr and EPTA DR2 data — [arXiv:2508.14742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14742)
- [V] A quasar-based binary population model calibrated to the pulsar-timing amplitude finds at most ~25% of binaries can be associated with quasars, ≳95% of the background comes from z ≲ 2.5 and from masses ≳10⁸ M☉ (the extract said "15-yr"; the 2022 paper used the 12.5-yr amplitude as recalled); a 2025 follow-up argues quasars can "signpost" binaries — [Casey-Clyde et al. 2022, NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10361592); [Casey-Clyde et al. 2025, NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10656399)

### Inferences
- Pulsar-timing sensitivity is concentrated at 10⁹–10¹⁰ M☉ and periods of years, i.e. exactly the CRTS-type candidates; it says nothing about lower-mass or shorter-period systems (QPE hosts, ZTF/Gaia short periods), so a PTA cross-match is a useful veto only for the most massive, long-period candidates.
- The measured background amplitude (≈2.4 × 10⁻¹⁵) is higher than the limits used in the 2018 population test, so the "tension" is quantitatively weaker today; but the independent failures of individual candidates (red-noise re-attribution with ZTF, PTF re-assessment, Gaia validation) now carry the conclusion that most candidates are false. This is an inference; no post-2023 paper re-running the Sesana test was retrieved.
- Because "confirmation" signatures occur by chance in 20–37% of ordinary quasars, any confirmation step in the tool must be calibrated on a matched control sample of non-candidates, not judged in isolation.
- A practical confirmation ladder: same period in an independent survey → same period and consistent phase in another band (with wavelength-dependent amplitude recorded, not assumed) → persistence in new data → spectroscopic/radio follow-up; only PKS 2131−021 has climbed most of it.

### Gaps
- The names and electromagnetic properties of the two mildly interesting NANOGrav targets were not retrieved (recalled as SDSS J1536+0441 and SDSS J0729+4008, unverified).
- No retrieved source quantifies how the 2023 background detection changes the allowed number of periodic-quasar binaries; the Holgado et al. numerical limit on the blazar binary fraction was not retrieved.
- Outcome of the predicted repeat flares of "Spikey" is unknown; results of infrared dust-echo tests of periodic candidates were not retrieved.

## KQ6 — Public datasets: quasar catalogues, light-curve sets, access, and what Rubin/LSST changes

### Takeaway
A quasar-periodicity module can today cross-match ~0.75–1.3 million catalogued quasars against public optical light curves from CRTS (2005–2013), ZTF (2018–Oct 2025 in DR24), Pan-STARRS1, Gaia DR3 (872,228 variable AGN, 34 months) and, for blazars, the Fermi-LAT Light Curve Repository (1,525 sources, 2008–present); OVRO 40 m radio data are by request only. The near-term step changes are Gaia DR4 (planned 2 December 2026: 66 months of epoch photometry for all sources) and Rubin/LSST (survey began 29–30 June 2026; alerts flowing since 24 February 2026; catalogue light curves proprietary for about two years, so broadly public data are expected around 2028).

### Inferences
- Maximum observed period that still gives 5 cycles, by baseline: Gaia DR3 (34 months) ≈ 207 d; PTF (~3.8 yr) ≈ 280 d; Gaia DR4 (66 months) ≈ 400 d; ZTF DR24 (7.6 yr) ≈ 555 d; CRTS (~9 yr) ≈ 660 d; LSST alone (10 yr) ≈ 2 yr, reached only in the mid-2030s; CRTS + ZTF stitched (2005–2025) ≈ 4.1 yr; SDSS + PS1 + ZTF in Stripe 82 (~22 yr) ≈ 4.4 yr; Fermi-LAT (18 yr) ≈ 3.6 yr. Published optical claims at 3–5 yr observed periods (PG 1302−102, DES candidates) are at or beyond these limits for any single survey.
- The highest-value public combination today is CRTS + ZTF (+ PS1, ATLAS, ASAS-SN, Gaia) per object, cross-calibrated; the cross-survey offset and differing bandpasses must be fitted per object, because unmodelled offsets inject low-frequency power.
- Gaia DR4 will more than double the number of quasars with public space-quality light curves (all sources, not just the 872,228 DR3 variables) and nearly double the baseline; it is the largest near-term dataset change for an open tool and arrives weeks after this note's date if the schedule holds.

- The Fermi LCR is the only fully public, uniformly processed, 18-year, regularly sampled AGN light-curve set; it is restricted to ~1,500 gamma-ray-variable sources (mostly blazars), where red-noise false positives are well documented.
- OVRO 40 m data cannot be assumed available to an automated public tool.

## KQ7 — Technosignature (SETI) angles involving quasars or AGN

### Takeaway
The only time a quasar was publicly proposed as an artificial beacon was CTA-102 in 1964–65, when its radio variability was read as a sign of a "supercivilisation" before it was recognised as an ordinary quasar; since then the peer-reviewed literature treats AGN as (a) contaminants or false positives in technosignature searches, (b) hypothetical power sources for Dyson-type structures around supermassive black holes, and (c) "one of everything" survey targets. No peer-reviewed proposal was found for artificial modulation of quasar light or for quasars as signalling Schelling points, and quasar redshift periodicity is a fringe claim rejected by mainstream analyses.

### Cited Findings

**Historical precedent: a "pulsing quasar" mistaken for a beacon**
- [V] Kardashev (1963–64) proposed the then-unidentified radio sources CTA-21 and CTA-102 as candidate transmissions from Type II/III civilisations, treating variability as an indicator of artificiality; Sholomitskii (1965) reported ~30% variability of CTA-102 on a timescale of a few months; a TASS telegram announced an artificial cosmic signal and a press conference followed at the Sternberg Institute on 12–13 April 1965, causing a worldwide sensation — [SETI in Russia/USSR, arXiv:1905.03225](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03225); [arXiv:2302.06446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06446); [CTA 102, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTA_102)
- [V] The artificial hypothesis "wound down quickly"; CTA-102's radio variability was confirmed in 1972 and the episode fed the understanding of quasar variability; CTA-102 is today a typical AGN with a relativistic jet pointed near our line of sight — [arXiv:1905.03225](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03225); [CTA 102, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTA_102)

**Galaxy-scale (Kardashev Type III) waste-heat searches**
- [V] G-HAT (Griffith et al. 2015) examined ~100,000 WISE-resolved galaxies for excess mid-infrared emission: none reprocesses ≥85% of its starlight; Type III civilisations are "very rare in the local universe" — [Centauri Dreams summary of G-HAT](https://centauri-dreams.org/2015/04/16/g-hat-searching-for-kardashev-type-iii/); [Wright et al. 2014 (G-HAT II), arXiv:1408.1134](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.1134)
- [V] Garrett (2015) showed the 93 best G-HAT candidates largely follow the mid-infrared–radio correlation, i.e. natural star formation/AGN processes dominate — [Garrett 2015, arXiv:1508.02624](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02624)
- [R] Related null results and arguments: Tully–Fisher outlier searches (Annis 1999; Zackrisson et al. 2015) and Lacki's "Type III Societies (Apparently) Do Not Exist" — [Zackrisson et al. 2015, arXiv:1508.02406](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02406); [Lacki 2016, arXiv:1604.07844](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07844)
- [V] AGN as false positives: high-resolution radio imaging of a Dyson-sphere candidate found at least some candidates' radio/infrared emission is contamination by a background AGN — [Universe Today report](https://www.universetoday.com/170424/high-resolution-imaging-of-dyson-sphere-candidate-reveals-no-radio-signals/)

**Black holes and AGN as engineered power sources**
- [V] Inoue & Yokoo (2011) proposed a "Type III Dyson sphere" that harvests the accretion-disc output of a supermassive black hole, with collectors placed so as not to intercept the jets, and waste heat potentially dumped into the hole — [Inoue & Yokoo 2011, arXiv:1112.5519](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5519)
- [V] Hsiao et al. (2021, MNRAS 506, 1723) analysed Dyson spheres around black holes (stellar-mass and supermassive): the accretion disc is the largest available power source (~10⁵ L☉ for a stellar-mass hole, enough for a Type II civilisation), and such a structure around a stellar-mass black hole in the Milky Way would be detectable (the extract says in the ultraviolet); the paper credits Inoue & Yokoo with the supermassive version — [Hsiao et al. 2021, arXiv:2106.15181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15181)
- [V] **[2026]** "The Dyson Minds 2025 Workshop: SETI around Black Holes" (workshop 3–4 June 2025 at MIT, ~25 participants; report in PASP 2026) considered post-biological intelligences powered by supermassive black holes; consensus that architecture determines observables, mid-infrared waste heat is the most robust signature, and the main recommendation is anomaly detection on archival WISE, JWST and Event Horizon Telescope data; no such structure has been found — [arXiv:2604.21886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21886); [astrobiology.com](https://astrobiology.com/2026/04/the-dyson-minds-2025-workshop-seti-around-black-holes.html)
- [V] **[2025]** A December 2025 preprint considers microlensing signatures of Dyson-sphere-like structures around primordial black holes — [arXiv:2512.07924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07924)
- [R] Lacki (2015) argued that Planck-energy particle accelerators built by advanced societies would be detectable through ultra-high-energy neutrino/gamma emission, a concept that competes observationally with natural AGN accelerators — [Lacki 2015, arXiv:1503.01509](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01509)

**Quasars as survey targets or backgrounds**
- [V] The Breakthrough Listen Exotica Catalog (Lacki et al. 2021, ApJS) deliberately includes "one of everything" — 963 entries/816 distinct targets in the final version, in Prototype, Superlative, Anomaly and control classes — and lists quasars among its targets — [Lacki et al. 2021, arXiv:2006.11304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11304); [DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/ac168a)
- [R] Breakthrough Listen has placed limits on transmitters in background extragalactic objects lying in its target fields, and searched nearby galaxies directly — [ADS title search](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=title%3A%22Constraints%20on%20extragalactic%20transmitters%22); [Choza et al. 2024, arXiv:2312.03943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03943)

**Artificial modulation of natural sources (not quasars)**
- [V] **[2026]** A 2026 review of technosignature possibilities lists engineered pulsar modulation patterns among compact-object concepts; searches found no analogous published proposal for quasars or AGN — [arXiv:2605.21093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21093)
- [R] Published modulation ideas concern stars and pulsars: neutrino-triggered Cepheid phase modulation ("Cepheid Galactic Internet"), a test of an RR Lyrae star for prime-number structure, and pulsar signal modulation — [Learned et al. 2008, arXiv:0809.0339](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0339); [Hippke et al. 2015, arXiv:1409.1265](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1265); [Chennamangalam et al. 2015, arXiv:1311.4608](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.4608)
- [V] The nearest methodological analogue is the Breakthrough Listen 4–8 GHz Galactic Centre search for periodic technosignatures (radio, pulsed) — [arXiv:2305.18527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18527)
- [V] **[2026]** A 2026 conference contribution argues that narrowband-only SETI filtering risks discarding broadband or otherwise structured (modulated) transmissions — [EPSC2026-133](https://presentations.copernicus.org/EPSC2026/EPSC2026-133_supplement.pdf)
- [R] Borra proposed that extremely rapid artificial pulsing (10⁻¹⁰–10⁻¹⁵ s spacing) would appear as periodic modulation in spectra and searched SDSS spectra (stars and galaxies); the stellar result was not accepted as a technosignature by the SETI community — [Borra & Trottier 2016, arXiv:1610.03031](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03031)

**Fringe: quasar redshift periodicity**
- [V] Claims: Burbidge (0.061 periodicity), Karlsson's log(1+z) formula, Burbidge & Napier (2001), Bell & McDiarmid (2006: six peaks in 46,400 SDSS quasars). Tests: Hawkins, Maddox & Merrifield (2002, 2dF) and Tang & Zhang (2005, SDSS + 2dF) found no evidence; the SDSS peaks disappear after correcting selection effects. Described as "a fringe topic with no support from mainstream astronomers in recent times"; a 2024 preprint still claims a 0.051 periodicity in quasar–galaxy pairs — [Redshift quantization, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redshift_quantization); [arXiv:2408.07101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07101)

### Inferences
- Energetics explain the absence of quasar-modulation proposals: a quasar radiates 10⁴⁵–10⁴⁷ erg s⁻¹ (2.6 × 10¹¹–2.6 × 10¹³ L☉); imposing a 10% modulation means controlling 10⁴⁴–10⁴⁶ erg s⁻¹, i.e. ~10¹⁰–10¹² times a Kardashev Type II budget (~4 × 10³³ erg s⁻¹) and 0.25–25 times a Type III budget (~4 × 10⁴⁴ erg s⁻¹) — using Kardashev's 1964 definitions as recalled. Mainstream SETI therefore discusses AGN as power sources or contaminants, not as modulated carriers.
- The CTA-102 episode is the exact historical template for the commissioned feature: variability alone was taken as evidence of artificiality and the claim collapsed once quasar variability was understood. The module's outputs should be framed as "statistically unusual variability requiring natural-explanation checks", never as candidate signals on the strength of periodicity or variability alone.
- The one actionable recommendation in the peer-reviewed literature that matches the tool's design is the Dyson Minds call for anomaly detection in archival data (mid-infrared excess, unusual nuclear sources); a WISE/NEOWISE mid-infrared anomaly channel would be better grounded in published SETI reasoning than an optical "pulsing" channel.
- Redshift periodicity is not a time-domain signal at all and has been explained by selection effects; it should be excluded from the tool or shown only as a documented example of a spurious periodicity.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found proposing (a) deliberate modulation of a quasar/AGN, (b) quasars as Schelling points or synchronisers for signalling, or (c) quasars as back-lights for detecting occulting megastructures; absence is based on a limited number of searches plus recall, so it is "not found", not "proven absent".
- Whether the frequently repeated "100-day period" for Sholomitskii's CTA-102 observations is accurate was not confirmed (retrieved sources say ~30% variability over a few months).
- Details of Lacki (2016), Chen & Garrett (2021), the low-frequency extragalactic searches and Lingam & Loeb's AGN-powered propulsion ideas were not retrieved.
- Whether the Exotica Catalog specifically lists blazars/AGN sub-classes (beyond "quasars") was not confirmed.

## KQ8 — What would actually count as anomalous in a quasar light curve, what must be excluded first, and what sensitivity is realistic

### Takeaway
Periodicity by itself is not anomalous — a strictly periodic quasar is the predicted signature of a natural black-hole binary, and regular flares are the signature of orbiting stars — so "anomalous" has to mean structure that no stochastic, orbital, lensing, propagation or instrumental process produces: long-lived phase-locked periodicity with implausibly high coherence and non-astrophysical pulse shape, modulation carrying information, achromatic events with wrong shapes, or large-amplitude changes faster than the light-crossing time of an unbeamed source. With existing public data the realistic reach is periodic modulations of roughly ≥0.05–0.1 mag with periods of ~0.3–4 yr in ~10⁵–10⁶ bright quasars at low completeness, plus high-cadence anomaly searches in a few thousand Kepler/TESS/Fermi/X-ray sources.

### Cited Findings

**Natural processes that already produce "structured" signals**
- [V] Strict, phase-coherent sinusoid over decades: PKS 2131−021 (47.9 yr; natural explanation: orbital modulation of a jet in a binary) — [Kiehlmann et al. 2024, arXiv:2407.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09647)
- [V] Regular repeating flares: QPEs (hours–days, duty cycle 10–30%), ASASSN-14ko (114 d, >20 flares) — [arXiv:2607.15359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15359); [Payne et al. 2021, arXiv:2009.03321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03321)
- [V] Period drift of either sign: shortening with deceleration (1ES 1927+654), lengthening (Ansky), slow shortening (RE J1034+396, ASASSN-14ko) — [arXiv:2501.01581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01581); [arXiv:2602.16776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16776)
- [V] Few-cycle sinusoid-like variations from pure red noise — [Vaughan et al. 2016, arXiv:1606.02620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02620)
- [R] Variability faster than the black-hole light-crossing time in beamed sources (IC 310, PKS 2155−304, 3C 279) — [Aleksić et al. 2014, arXiv:1412.4936](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4936); [Aharonian et al. 2007, arXiv:0706.0797](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0797)
- [V] Magnitude-scale brightening/fading and changing-look transitions over months–years; ~10% of quasars exceed 1 mag in 15 yr — [Rumbaugh et al. 2018, arXiv:1706.07875](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.07875); [Ricci & Trakhtenbrot 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-023-02108-4)
- [R] Symmetric, roughly achromatic flares from gravitational self-lensing in a binary or microlensing by intervening stars — [Hu et al. 2020, arXiv:1910.05348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05348)
- [P] Repeated copies of the same light-curve pattern with a fixed delay occur naturally in gravitationally lensed quasars (the basis of time-delay cosmography tools such as PyCS3) — [PyCS3 on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/PyCS3/)
- [V] Instrument-induced structure: Kepler/K2 signatures mistaken for AGN variability; TESS sector-scale systematics; gaps amplifying false periodicities — [arXiv:2012.14452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14452); [arXiv:2310.08631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08631); [arXiv:2406.15558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15558)

**Measured detection performance to anchor sensitivity claims**
- [V] Sinusoid recovery by periodogram under DRW noise: ~23–45%; sawtooth: ~1–9% — [Lin, Charisi & Haiman 2026, arXiv:2505.14778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14778)
- [V] Best published scalable pipeline: ~40% true positives at ~0.5% false positives — [Banaszak et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.15379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15379)
- [V] Photometric periodicity alone at >0.1 mag amplitude yields false-positive-dominated samples; amplitudes are overestimated in two-thirds of cases — [Witt et al. 2022, arXiv:2110.07465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07465)
- [V] The DRW describes Stripe 82 light curves to 0.01–0.02 mag, which sets the floor for detecting departures from the null in that data — [MacLeod et al. 2010, arXiv:1004.0276](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0276)
- [V] Gaia DR3 gives an average of 39 transits per AGN over 925 days; Fermi LCR gives 3-day bins for 1,525 sources; ZTF public data span March 2018 – October 2025 — [Carnerero et al. 2023, arXiv:2207.06849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06849); [arXiv:2301.01607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01607); [ZTF](https://www.ztf.caltech.edu/ztf-for-astronomers.html)

### Inferences

**A graded definition of "anomalous" (proposed for the module)**
- Tier 0 (not anomalous, catalogue only): excess power at a period with <3 cycles, or any period consistent with the red-noise null after trials correction.
- Tier 1 (astrophysically interesting, natural explanation expected): ≥5 cycles, phase-coherent across two independent surveys, survives the across-sample trials factor and a held-out-data prediction test. Expected interpretation: binary candidate, jet precession, or repeating partial disruption. This tier already exceeds what almost every published optical candidate achieved.
- Tier 2 (unexplained by standard models): any of — (a) coherence far beyond natural QPO behaviour (no measurable phase wander over ≥10–20 cycles) combined with a pulse shape that is neither sinusoidal, sawtooth/bursty nor lensing-spike-like; (b) strictly achromatic modulation (identical fractional amplitude from optical to UV/X-ray), which neither Doppler boosting nor disc physics predicts; (c) discrete flux levels, sharp-edged (flat-bottomed or square-wave) dips, or recurrence intervals following an arithmetic pattern that is not a single period and its drift; (d) large fractional variability in an unbeamed, radio-quiet quasar on observed timescales well below (1+z)·R_g/c for its measured mass (e.g. ≪ 1.4 hr × (1+z) at 10⁹ M☉).
- Tier 3 (information-bearing): statistically significant non-random symbol structure after removing the stochastic and periodic components. No natural quasar process is known to produce this, and nothing of the kind has been reported.

**Natural explanations to exclude, in order**
- Instrument and survey artefacts: annual and lunar aliases, sidereal-day aliasing from nightly sampling, satellite orbital/precession periods, sector or quarter boundaries, cross-survey zero-point offsets, blending with a foreground periodic variable star in the aperture, host-galaxy seeing-dependent aperture effects.
- Stochastic red noise (DRW/DHO/bending power law), including long-term trends that mimic a fraction of a cycle.
- Propagation effects: interstellar scintillation and extreme scattering in the radio; microlensing (roughly achromatic, single symmetric events; chromatic in lensed quasars because source size varies with wavelength); multiple imaging (delayed copies of the light curve).
- Orbital and precessional clocks: binary Doppler boosting, periodic accretion (bursty/sawtooth), self-lensing spikes, jet precession or helical jets, Lense–Thirring disc precession, star–disc collisions, repeating partial disruptions.
- Discrete accretion events: tidal disruption flares, changing-look transitions, supernovae in the host nucleus, occultation by broad-line-region clouds (changing-obscuration events).

**Realistic sensitivity with public data (derived from the cited numbers)**
- Optical surveys (CRTS, ZTF, PS1, Gaia): per-epoch errors of a few hundredths to a few tenths of a magnitude and intrinsic red noise of ~0.1–0.3 mag on year scales mean periodic amplitudes below ~0.05 mag are effectively undetectable for individual objects, and amplitudes ≥0.1 mag are detectable only with ≥3–5 cycles and still at ≲45% completeness. Period reach for ≥5 cycles: ~0.2–1.5 yr from single surveys, up to ~4 yr with CRTS + ZTF or Stripe 82 composites.
- Sample sizes: ~10⁵ quasars with CRTS + ZTF coverage brighter than ~19–20 mag (143,700 in the ZTF search; 243,500 in CRTS), ~0.87 million Gaia DR3 variable AGN with only ~39 epochs each, rising to all Gaia sources at 66 months in DR4.
- Information-bearing or fast modulation is out of reach in ground-based survey data: at ~1 sample per 2–3 nights with seasonal gaps, the Nyquist period is ~5–6 days and a decade of ZTF yields only ~10³ samples per object; only bit rates far below one per week could be tested. Fast structure requires Kepler (21 AGN), TESS (27-day sectors, systematics-limited), X-ray pointed data (ksec exposures) or Fermi (3-day bins).
- Causality-violation searches are realistic only where mass estimates exist (SDSS/DESI virial masses) and cadence is sub-hour, i.e. TESS/Kepler and X-ray archives; for blazars the test is void without a Doppler factor.
- Because every optical survey candidate list to date has been dominated by false positives, a responsible module should publish, for each run, its injection–recovery completeness and its expected number of false alarms, and should label Tier 1 detections as "binary-black-hole-type candidates" rather than technosignature candidates.

### Gaps
- No published study quantifies sensitivity to non-periodic "coded" modulation in quasar light curves; the limits above are derived from cadence and noise figures, not from a cited analysis.
- Verified per-epoch photometric precision figures for CRTS, ZTF, Gaia and TESS as a function of magnitude were not retrieved in this session.
- Typical coherence (quality factor) values for AGN QPOs and QPEs were not retrieved, so the Tier 2 coherence threshold is qualitative.
- No source was retrieved on occultation (eclipse) events in AGN beyond the changing-obscuration classification; the natural rate of sharp achromatic dips in quasar optical light curves is unknown to this research.

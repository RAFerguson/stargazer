## Follow-up

- The unbinned ZTF measurements are fitted far better by a period of 0.49622 days (r band; 0.49622 days in g) than by 79.9 days.
- Gaia DR3 lists this source as an RR Lyrae star of type RRab with a pulsation period of 0.49624 days and a peak-to-peak range of 0.90 magnitudes. Two instruments and two pipelines agree on the period to 4 parts in 100,000.
- A 0.4962-day pulsation seen about once a night, at an hour that drifts through the year, beats at 80.1 days. The 79.9-day cycle is that beat.
- The object is a pulsating star in the Galactic halo that Gaia's classifier gave a 96% chance of being a quasar. The search was right that it is strictly periodic and wrong, through no test it then had, about the period. An alias gate has since been added to the pipeline; it fails this object.

## Evidence

- The best periodic model has a period of 79.9 days and covers 33.2 cycles in 417 points over 2654 days; its fundamental has an amplitude of 1.23 in the standardised units of the merged light curve (1 is the object's own scatter), and 0% of its power is in higher harmonics.
- Against red noise fitted to this object, with the search over 317 trial periods included, the probability of a periodic model this good is 1 in 1,001 (0 of 1000 simulated nulls matched it).
- After recalibration against the whole population searched, the probability is 1 in 104,966.
- Note: a model at 159.9 days fitted best; the period reported is its harmonic at 79.9 days, which fits nearly as well.
- For contrast, a test that assumes white noise would have given below 1 in a billion (2.3e-74); that number is not evidence, because the noise is not white.
- The period of 79.9 days is 4.1 resolution elements from the nearest calendar period (one year / 4); that is clear of it.
- The observing pattern has 4% of its power at this period; the sampling does not explain the signal.
- Removing any one of 8 years moves the phase by at most 0.01 cycles and the amplitude by a factor of 0.97 to 1.03; the most important year carries 18% of the evidence. The signal does not hang on one year.
- The 3 bands in which the signal is visible agree in phase to within 0.06 cycles.
- Searched alone, the first 70% of the time span finds the same period (80.0 days), and its periodic model predicts the remaining 139 points better than no periodic model (log-likelihood gain +53.4).
- With the time axis warped at random, the search does as well as in true time 0.5% of the time; the variation keeps time.

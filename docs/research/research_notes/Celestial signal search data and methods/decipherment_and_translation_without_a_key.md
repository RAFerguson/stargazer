## 1. Historical decipherments, undeciphered systems, and statistical "is it language?" tests

### Takeaway
Every accepted decipherment of an ancient script rested on at least one external anchor: a bilingual (Egyptian), a known related language plus names and place names (Linear B, Ugaritic), or a known descendant language plus a partial native key (Maya). No script has been deciphered from internal statistics alone, and the systems that remain undeciphered are the ones that lack those anchors: corpora too small, no known related language, no bilingual. Statistical tests (conditional entropy, Zipf, and similar) detect *organisation*, not *language*. Two 2026 preprints show constructively that the standard tests cannot tell language from structured non-linguistic sign systems.

### Cited Findings
**Accepted decipherments: what made each possible**
- Egyptian hieroglyphs: Champollion (1822–24) used the Rosetta Stone's Greek/Demotic/hieroglyphic trilingual, royal-name cartouches (Ptolemy, Cleopatra) as cribs, and Coptic as the descendant language. [R] (standard histories, e.g. Pope, *The Story of Decipherment*, 1975/1999; not re-checked)
- Linear B, with no bilingual: Alice Kober (1946–50) found inflectional "triplets" (sign groups sharing a stem with alternating endings). From those she inferred that the script was syllabic and the language inflected, without assigning sound values. Michael Ventris built a consonant–vowel "grid" of signs that share consonants or vowels. He then tested provisional values on Knossos place names (ko-no-so, a-mi-ni-so), and in 1952 the values produced Greek. The independent confirmation came from the Pylos "tripod tablet" (published 1953): the ideograms of tripods and vessels matched the phonetic words. [R]
- Ugaritic (alphabetic cuneiform found in 1929) was deciphered within about a year (1930) by Bauer, Dhorme and Virolleaud. The key assumptions were that it was Semitic and that a ~30-sign inventory meant an alphabet. Single-sign words were matched to Semitic prepositions, and a Phoenician-inscribed axe helped. [R]
- Maya: Knorozov (1952) argued that the script mixed logograms and syllables, using Landa's 16th-century "alphabet" as a misunderstood syllabary key. Proskouriakoff (1960) showed from date patterns at Piedras Negras that the inscriptions record historical rulers' lives. Living Mayan languages served as the known language. Wide acceptance came only in the 1970s–80s. [R]
- Computational historians of the field summarise decipherment as "the rarest category of achievement", with methods that are "rarely reusable for another language" — quoted by Luo, Cao & Barzilay 2019 from Pope 1975. [P] ([arXiv:1906.06718](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06718))

**Undeciphered systems (status as of 2026)**
- Linear A: the whole corpus is about 7,500 characters ("short enough to fit on a single screen"). In June 2026 a self-taught AI engineer claimed a Semitic reading: 40 sign values and a 408-word dictionary, produced with AI-written scripts. A critique in The Conversation (J. Adkins, Dublin City University) says the claim cannot be validated: "with that little data, almost any hypothesis can find scattered matches." The claim is unrefereed and not accepted. [S] ([Down to Earth / The Conversation republication](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/cracking-the-code-can-ai-help-us-decipher-ancient-languages)). Linear A shares many signs with Linear B, so approximate phonetic values can be borrowed, but the underlying language is unknown. [R]
- Indus script: a 2026 re-digitisation analysis uses 1,916 deduplicated inscriptions, 584 unique signs and 11,110 tokens. That works out to about 5.8 signs per inscription, which shows how short the texts are. [P] ([Nair 2026, arXiv:2604.17828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17828)). No bilingual is known, the language family is unknown, and the texts are very short. [R]
- Rongorongo (about two dozen objects), Proto-Elamite (about 1,600 tablets, partly understood as numerical/administrative), Phaistos Disc (a single text) and the Voynich manuscript all remain undeciphered. [R] For Linear Elamite, a 2022 decipherment claim by Desset et al. exists and is partially accepted; I could not confirm its status today. [R]
- Khipu, Phaistos, Rongorongo, Indus and Voynich were all analysed (2026) with information-theoretic structural reconstruction. The authors explicitly separate "recoverable organisation from semantic decipherment." Phaistos keeps 0.714 bits of excess adjacent-sign mutual information after positional controls. As a control, calibrated BDM recovers the Arecibo message's 23-column width (search-corrected p = 0.005). [P] ([Zenil & Uthamacumaran 2026, arXiv:2610.08861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08861)) (2026 preprint)

**Statistical tests that a symbol system is language, and their reliability**
- Rao et al. 2009 (*Science* 324:1165) reported "entropic evidence for linguistic structure in the Indus script". The method compared the Indus bigram conditional entropy with that of linguistic and non-linguistic sequences. Crossref metadata carries the summary "Analysis of the pattern of symbols confirms the linguistic role of ancient signs." [P metadata] ([doi:10.1126/science.1170391](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170391)). Method details [R].
- Sproat's critique: his 2014 paper in *Language* compared written language with non-linguistic symbol systems (heraldry, Pictish symbols, Mesopotamian deity symbols, and others). He argued that the conditional-entropy and similar measures do not separate the two classes, so Rao et al.'s result cannot establish that the Indus signs encode language. [P title/DOI; argument R] ([Sproat 2014, doi:10.1353/lan.2014.0031](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2014.0031)). Earlier critique: Farmer, Sproat & Witzel 2004, who argued the Indus signs are non-linguistic. [P as cited in arXiv:2604.17828]
- **(2026)** Nair's synthetic-baseline scorecard tests the Indus corpus against heraldic and administrative generators. Neither generator reproduces the Indus profile on four metrics: brevity, formulaic repetition, hapax rate and positional rigidity. Neither do seven real non-linguistic corpora, including Sproat's datasets. Nair replicates a Zipf slope of −1.49 and a conditional entropy of 3.23 bits. [P] ([arXiv:2604.17828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17828))
- **(2026)** Raghavendra's "non-specificity" study built SIGIL, a generative emblem system: 3,000 texts with explicit compositional meanings but no phonology. Before evaluation he pre-registered a registry of 54 published methods. SIGIL "receives the same category as the Indus corpus on every criterion scored this way." A sequential decipherment stress test reached "high dictionary coverage for English, Sanskrit, and Tamil on the same corpus." The conclusion is that these measures "detect organization without being specific to language." [P] ([arXiv:2608.02999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02999)). This is a direct demonstration of the false-positive risk that a "translation" tool would face.
- Voynich: Montemurro & Zanette 2013 (*PLoS ONE* 8:e66344) applied an information-theoretic keyword analysis and argued that the word distribution is consistent with real semantic organisation across sections. [P metadata; content R] ([doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0066344](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066344)). Amancio et al. 2013 proposed a framework of first-order word statistics, network topology and intermittency to test whether a text "is compatible with a natural language … without any knowledge of the meaning". They calibrated it on the New Testament in 15 languages. [P] ([arXiv:1303.0347](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0347))
- **(2026)** Rozanova & Temerev tested the assumptions that Voynich glyphs are letters, that tokens are words and that blanks are word spaces, using matched prose, cipher and pseudo-text controls. None of the three holds. Glyph conditional entropy is 2.7 bits, against about 3.5 for Latin, Italian and English, which is too low for one-to-one substitution of any tested plaintext. Token-to-token predictability is under 1% of token entropy, against 2–10% in the controls. Seventy per cent of types are singletons. Crucially, a published Voynich-imitating cipher and a self-citation generator both reproduce the low entropy, the weak token order and a null substitution-attack result. Neither reproduces the edge-glyph coupling or the open vocabulary. [P] ([arXiv:2608.17096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17096))
- **(2026)** Turenne claims, from LLM-based and probabilistic analyses, that Voynich symbols "behave as letters" and proposes a "pastiche hypothesis". This contradicts Rozanova & Temerev on whether the glyphs are letters. Both are unrefereed. [P abstract] ([arXiv:2609.20835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20835))
- A 2021 evolutionary algorithm mapped Voynich "calendar" tokens onto lists of feminine names. It "consistently" transcribes "dozens among three hundred" tokens. It is an example of crib-fitting that can always find partial matches. [P] ([Hromada, arXiv:2107.05381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05381))
- Zipf's law, Heaps' law, Menzerath–Altmann and long-range correlations are produced by many non-linguistic processes (random typing, music, DNA, generative emblem systems). They are necessary-ish but not sufficient. [R; SIGIL result P above]

### Inferences
- For Stargazer: a positive score on Zipf, entropy or Menzerath tests should be reported only as "organised and non-random," never as "linguistic." The SIGIL and Voynich-imitator results show that matched generative controls are mandatory: a structured non-language, a cipher and a self-citation generator.
- Decipherment success historically came from cribs and anchors: names, numbers, ideograms paired with words, and bilinguals. The signal analogue of a crib is shared physics and mathematics: numbers, the hydrogen line, pulsar periods, and the geometry of the signal itself.
- Corpus size matters. With about 7,500 characters (Linear A) or texts of about 5.8 signs (Indus), hypotheses outnumber constraints. A tool should estimate how many free parameters a proposed key has relative to the corpus, as a minimum-description-length check.

### Gaps
- Full text of Rao et al. 2009 and Sproat 2014 not read; the exact conditional-entropy values and arguments are [R].
- Rao et al.'s replies to Sproat (2010 and later) were not retrieved.
- Current status of Linear Elamite and Proto-Elamite was not checked.
- Montemurro & Zanette's numbers were not read (no abstract returned).

## 2. Computational decipherment: inputs, assumptions and reported accuracy

### Takeaway
Every successful computational decipherment so far assumes a known, closely related "plaintext" language: Hebrew for Ugaritic, Greek for Linear B, German for the Copiale cipher. Many also assume known segmentation or known phonetics. Accuracy is measured against an *already known* decipherment. Applied to a truly undeciphered case (Iberian), the best model correctly declined to confirm the favoured hypothesis (Basque); it produced no reading.

### Cited Findings
- Copiale cipher: a 105-page handwritten encrypted manuscript from the mid-18th century. It was "cracked … by using modern computational technology combined with philological methods" and turned out to be the text of a secret society, the Oculists. The plaintext language was German, found after testing many candidate languages; it was a homophonic substitution with nulls. [P abstract] ([Knight, Megyesi & Schaefer 2012, doi:10.1558/jrff.v2i2.314](https://doi.org/10.1558/jrff.v2i2.314)). Details about roughly 75,000 characters and the cipher type [R].
- Snyder, Barzilay & Knight 2010 (ACL), "A statistical model for lost language decipherment", Ugaritic→Hebrew: Bayesian, with Hebrew morphological segmentations supplied. Its noisy-setting cognate identification accuracy was 60.4%, as reported in Luo et al.'s table. [P via arXiv:1906.06718, Table 2]. That 29 of 30 Ugaritic letters were mapped correctly [R].
- Berg-Kirkpatrick & Klein 2011: in the noiseless Ugaritic setting (cognate pairs known to exist), the "Matcher" scored 90.4% cognate accuracy. [P via arXiv:1906.06718, Table 2]. Berg-Kirkpatrick & Klein 2013, "Decipherment with a million random restarts", for the Zodiac 408 cipher [R].
- Luo, Cao & Barzilay 2019, NeuroCipher (minimum-cost-flow neural decipherment): Ugaritic 93.5% (noiseless) and 65.9% (noisy), a 5.5-point gain on the noisy setting, without Hebrew morphology. Linear B: 70 symbols, 919 Linear B–Greek word pairs, 67.3% of cognates translated correctly (84.7% on a names subset). It assumes a known related language, a cognate-rich lexicon and monotonic character alignment. [P] ([arXiv:1906.06718](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06718))
- Luo et al. 2021 (TACL), undersegmented scripts with an IPA phonetic prior: joint segmentation plus cognate alignment. It is evaluated on Gothic and Ugaritic (deciphered) and on Iberian (undeciphered). A language-closeness measure correctly identifies related languages for Gothic and Ugaritic. For Iberian it "does not show strong evidence supporting Basque." Gothic P@10 averages are 0.771/0.737/0.851 when the known language is close and much lower for distant known languages, for example 0.148/0.346/0.500. Performance rises with more phonological knowledge and more segmentation. [P] ([arXiv:2010.11054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11054))
- Tamburini 2023 cast lost-script decipherment as combinatorial optimisation with coupled simulated annealing (CAWL workshop). [P metadata] ([doi:10.18653/v1/2023.cawl-1.10](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.cawl-1.10))
- Dou & Knight 2013 used dependency-based decipherment for resource-limited MT, treating foreign text as a cipher of English. [P metadata] ([doi:10.18653/v1/d13-1173](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/d13-1173))
- Aldarrab & May 2020: seq2seq models crack simple substitution ciphers multilingually without explicit language identification and are robust to noise. [P] ([arXiv:2012.15229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15229))
- **(2026)** An attention-LSTM for homophonic ciphers reaches "near-perfect" decipherment, but only when trained on aligned ciphertext–plaintext pairs with a known shared code pool. That is a supervised setting. [P] ([Bruton, Beloucif & Megyesi, arXiv:2606.05078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05078))
- **(2025)** Oracle Bone Script: about 4,500 characters are known, of which about 1,600 are deciphered. Vision-language-model methods (OracleFusion; radical and pictographic analysis) *assist* interpretation of new characters. They rely on the descendant Chinese script and are not independent decipherments. [P] ([arXiv:2506.21101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21101); [arXiv:2508.10113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10113))

### Inferences
- These methods transfer to an unknown signal only if a "related language" exists. For an extraterrestrial or unknown-origin signal none does. The nearest analogue is a library of candidate *conventions*: binary arithmetic, raster images, Lincos-style logic, physical constants. A tool can run cognate-style alignment against those as "known languages," and it should report a closeness score for each, as Luo et al. 2021 do, rather than a forced reading.
- Reported accuracies (60–93%) are all scored against known answers. A tool should include a "deciphered-benchmark mode" that hides known keys from solved cases (Ugaritic, Linear B, Copiale, and synthetic messages) to calibrate itself before it is applied to unknowns.

### Gaps
- The Snyder et al. 2010 and Berg-Kirkpatrick & Klein papers were not read directly (ACL Anthology not reachable).
- I found no peer-reviewed 2023–2026 LLM-based decipherment of a genuinely unknown script.

## 3. Unsupervised machine translation and the isomorphism assumption; Goldwasser et al. 2023

### Takeaway
Embedding-alignment unsupervised MT works when two corpora come from similar languages, similar domains and similar embedding methods; that is the approximate-isomorphism assumption. It degrades or fails otherwise. Goldwasser et al. give a formal framework in which translation without parallel data is possible in principle. It needs a prior over plausible target meanings, enough "common ground" and enough source data, and error falls as source-language complexity rises. Their bounds come from stylised models only.

### Cited Findings
- Conneau et al. 2018 (MUSE): an adversarial alignment of monolingual embedding spaces builds bilingual dictionaries with no parallel data and "outperforms existing supervised methods on cross-lingual tasks for some language pairs", including English–Russian and English–Chinese. [P] ([arXiv:1710.04087](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087))
- Lample et al. 2018: unsupervised MT from monolingual corpora only, with BLEU 32.8 (Multi30k) and 15.1 (WMT En–Fr) without a single parallel sentence. [P] ([arXiv:1711.00043](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00043))
- Artetxe et al. 2018 (ICLR): unsupervised NMT with denoising and back-translation, BLEU 15.56 Fr→En and 10.21 De→En on WMT14. With 100k parallel sentences added, it reaches 21.81 and 15.24. [P] ([arXiv:1710.11041](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11041))
- Artetxe et al. 2018 (ACL), VecMap: adversarial methods "often fail in more realistic scenarios". A structural-similarity initialisation plus robust self-learning "succeeds in all tested scenarios." [P] ([arXiv:1805.06297](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06297))
- Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018 cast alignment as Gromov–Wasserstein optimal transport, which matches pairwise-similarity structure across spaces. Performance is comparable to the state of the art "with little or no tuning." [P] ([arXiv:1809.00013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00013))
- Søgaard, Ruder & Vulić 2018 found that unsupervised dictionary induction "performs much worse on morphologically rich languages that are not dependent marking", and also with corpora from different domains or different embedding algorithms. They established "a near-perfect correlation" between induction performance and a graph-similarity (isomorphism) metric. [P] ([arXiv:1805.03620](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03620))
- Goldwasser, Gruber, Kalai & Paradise (NeurIPS 2023) give a framework in which source and target need *not* share domain or structure. The method is MLE over translators, constrained by a target-side prior ρ of plausible translations. The general bound is err = O(ln|Θ| / (α · min(m, |T|))), where |Θ| is the translator family size, α is the "common ground" or agreement, m is the number of source samples and |T| is a measure of source-language complexity. For their knowledge-graph model the error is O(r log n / (α² p r) + r log n / (α m)), with r source nodes and n target nodes. "Error rates are inversely related to the language complexity and amount of common ground." In synthetic knowledge-graph experiments, accuracy rises from 0.10 ± 0.05 to 0.74 ± 0.08 to 1.00 ± 0.00 as the source graph grows (r = 4, 7, 10). A "common nonsense" model and lower bounds show that O(log n) ambiguities can be unresolvable. The bounds are information-theoretic, not computational: "learning this translator might be computationally intensive." On domain gap: "whales do not 'talk' about smartphones", so a broad prior over plausible translations is required. [P] ([arXiv:2211.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11081))
- Lee 2024 frames multilingual LM joint training as a decipherment process. He draws an information-theoretic limit and shows the importance of token alignment when domain, word order or tokenisation granularity differ. [P] ([arXiv:2406.07231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07231))

### Inferences
- For an unknown signal the isomorphism assumption is at its weakest: no shared domain, no shared embedding method, and unknown complexity. Goldwasser et al. imply that the "prior" is the decisive design choice. A Stargazer translator must state explicitly what space of candidate meanings it is choosing among (arithmetic statements, physical constants, images, signal self-description) and report how many alternatives remain consistent (the ambiguity count), not just the best-scoring translation.
- Their theory also implies a minimum-data gate: below some m relative to ln|Θ|/α, no translation claim should be emitted.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical test of the Goldwasser bounds on real animal data. WhAM (§4) is the nearest CETI follow-up, and it is not a translation result.

## 4. Animal-communication decoding, 2020–2026: what is translated versus described

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 **no non-human communication system has been translated** in the sense of mapping signals to propositional meanings. Achievements fall into three groups. First, structural description: combinatorial coda features, vowel-like spectral patterns, Zipfian and Menzerath-type statistics. Second, referential findings for a few call types, grounded by context and playback: individual "names" in elephants and marmosets, and zebra-finch call categories by meaning. Third, foundation-model tooling: classification, detection, generation. The strongest evidence of meaning comes from playback or experimental grounding, not from form-only modelling.

### Cited Findings
**Project CETI (sperm whales)**
- Roadmap (2021): collect massive multimodal data, detect basic units, find "language-like higher-level structures", and validate through interactive playback. [P] ([Andreas et al., arXiv:2104.08614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08614))
- Sharma et al. 2024 (*Nature Communications* 15:3617), "Contextual and combinatorial structure in sperm whale vocalisations". About 150 coda types are known globally, 21 in the Caribbean. The paper reports context-sensitive variations ("rubato", "ornamentation") that are "systematically controlled and imitated across individual whales." Coda types "form a combinatorial coding system" rather than arbitrary inter-click-interval sequences. [P abstract] ([doi:10.1038/s41467-024-47221-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47221-8)). The dataset is 8,719 codas from the Eastern Caribbean clan, about 60 whales recorded off Dominica. Gero cautioned that the components are more like phonemes than "26 letters." [S] ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/thecurrent/sperm-whale-communication-1.7205012); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076547)). The four features (rhythm, tempo, rubato, ornamentation) [S/R]. **No meanings were assigned to any coda.**
- **(2025)** Beguš, Sprouse, Leban, Silva et al., *Open Mind*: coda spectral properties are "highly structured, discretely distributed … uttered in dialogues, rather than a physical artefact of whale movement." They identify two recurrent coda-level "vowels" (a and i) and diphthong-like patterns, by analogy with source–filter theory. [P abstract] ([doi:10.1162/OPMI.a.252](https://doi.org/10.1162/OPMI.a.252)). This is structural description only.
- **(2025, NeurIPS)** WhAM fine-tuned VampNet, a masked acoustic token model pretrained on music, on 10k codas collected over two decades. It generates synthetic codas, evaluated by Fréchet Audio Distance and by expert perceptual studies. Its representations do well on rhythm, social-unit and vowel classification. The title says "towards a translative model", but no translation is reported. [P] ([Paradise et al., arXiv:2512.02206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02206))

**Earth Species Project**
- AVES is a self-supervised transformer encoder for animal vocalisations that outperforms supervised baselines on classification and detection. [P] ([arXiv:2210.14493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14493))
- BEANS is a benchmark of 12 datasets (birds, land and marine mammals, anurans, insects) with classification and detection tasks. [P] ([arXiv:2210.12300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12300))
- NatureLM-audio (2024) is an audio-language foundation model for bioacoustics, evaluated on BEANS-Zero, with state-of-the-art zero-shot classification of unseen species and open weights. Its tasks are detection, classification and context or behaviour labelling, not translation. [P] ([arXiv:2411.07186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07186))

**Google DolphinGemma (Apr 2025)**
- An audio-in, audio-out model of about 400M parameters trained on the Wild Dolphin Project's spotted-dolphin recordings, paired with the CHAT underwater interface. The aim is a shared artificial vocabulary, not translation of natural dolphin signals. An early output was "largely mimicking ocean noise." An open release was announced for summer 2025, but I found no confirmation, and I found no peer-reviewed results. [S] ([Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/ai/dolphingemma/); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/can-ai-let-us-chat-with-dolphins/)). Sceptic A. Kershenbaum: "It's not immediately clear that dolphins have words." [S]

**Humpback whales**
- McCowan et al. 2023 (*PeerJ* 11:e16349) report a 20-minute exchange in Southeast Alaska between a played-back "whup/throp" contact call and a female humpback, Twain. Blind observers identified three phases (engagement, agitation, disengagement) and latency matching. The authors call it "preliminary" and present it as a playback-design lesson for detecting non-human intelligence, which is the "Whale-SETI" framing. [P abstract] ([doi:10.7717/peerj.16349](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.16349)). This is turn-taking, not semantic exchange.
- **(2025)** Arnon et al., *Science* 387:649–653, "Whale song shows language-like statistical structure", used 8 years of New Caledonia recordings. Segmenting by transitional probabilities (an infant-learning method) yields Zipf's law of frequency and Zipf's law of abbreviation. Algorithmic segments were close to, but not identical with, expert segments. The authors attribute the pattern to cultural learning. Commentators stress that "structured like human language" means only the frequency-distribution fit. [S] ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/whale-songs-follow-basic-human-language-rules/); [HUJI record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/whale-song-shows-language-like-statistical-structure/); [Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=73012)). The Science news piece is confirmed by Crossref [P metadata] ([doi:10.1126/science.znkrt4q](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.znkrt4q)).
- **(2025)** Youngblood (*Science Advances*) found Menzerath's law across whale songs, strongest in humpback and blue whales. [S] (via the same coverage; not read)

**Individual "names"**
- Pardo et al. 2024 (*Nature Ecology & Evolution*): African elephants address one another with individually specific name-like calls. [P metadata] ([doi:10.1038/s41559-024-02420-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-024-02420-w)). Method [R]: a machine-learning classifier predicted the receiver from call acoustics above chance, and playback of calls addressed to a subject drew stronger responses than calls addressed to others. This is notable because the labels appear arbitrary, not imitative.
- Oren et al. 2024 (*Science* 385:996): marmosets use "phee calls" to vocally label specific conspecifics and "respond more consistently and correctly to calls that are specifically directed at them." Family members use similar labels, which indicates vocal learning. [P abstract] ([doi:10.1126/science.adp3757](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adp3757))

**Coller Dolittle Challenge (Jeremy Coller Foundation and Tel Aviv University)**
- **2025** prize (US$100k, announced 17 May 2025): Laela Sayigh's team for "first evidence for widespread sharing of stereotyped non-signature whistle types by wild dolphins". The judges cited the field playback experiments. [S] ([Coller Foundation press release](https://jeremycollerfoundation.org/press-releases/researchers-awarded-100000-for-identifying-first-evidence-of-possible-language-like-communication-in-dolphins/))
- **2026** prize (announced late June / 2 July 2026): Julie Elie (UC Berkeley) for a "dictionary" of 11 zebra-finch call types (aggression, hunger, bonding and others), built from more than a decade of behavioural observation plus machine learning. Experiments suggest the birds confuse calls by similarity of *meaning*, not of sound. Challenge chair Y. Yovel said there is "still a long way to go." [S] ([TAU announcement](https://english.tau.ac.il/news/2026-coller-dolittle-prize-winner); [Coller press release](https://www.jeremycollerfoundation.org/news-and-insights/press-releases/scientists-win-us100-000-in-2026-coller-dolittle-prize))

### Inferences
- The pattern is consistent. Self-supervised encoders and unit discovery deliver segmentation and inventories. Meaning claims (names, call categories) come only where behaviour, context and playback supply grounding. For an astronomical signal there is no playback or behavioural context, so "meaning" must be grounded in the signal's own content (numbers, physics, images) or remain unclaimed.
- Tools built for describing structure in communication signals (encoders, unit discovery, sequence models) map onto the structural and symbol-class stages. They do not supply semantics.

### Gaps
- Sharma et al. full text was not read; the exact feature inventory and the "phonetic alphabet" wording come from secondary sources.
- I found no publication of DolphinGemma results through November 2026.
- The Arnon et al. and Youngblood 2025 primary texts were not read (Science and Science Advances are not reachable).
- I found no CETI semantic or playback result for 2026.

## 5. Interstellar message design and decoding experiments

### Takeaway
Designed messages follow anti-cryptography: they are built to be decoded. They bootstrap from counting, arithmetic and logic (Lincos, CosmicOS), or from rasters and physics (Arecibo, the Cosmic Call primers). Blind tests show that humans can recover *structure and mathematical conventions* fairly quickly. Recovering the *intended meaning* of non-mathematical content (pictures, biology) is much less reliable. "A Sign in Space" (2023) was decoded structurally within about a year, and its meaning is still openly debated by design.

### Cited Findings
- Freudenthal, *Lincos: Design of a Language for Cosmic Intercourse*, Part 1 (North-Holland, 1960; Studies in Logic and the Foundations of Mathematics). [P metadata] ([doi:10.1016/s0049-237x(08)x7050-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0049-237x(08)x7050-8)). It teaches mathematics by ostension (pulses for numbers), then logic, then behaviour through "dialogues" between actors. [R]
- CosmicOS (Paul Fitzpatrick) is a message built as executable code from lambda-calculus-style primitives, so that the receiver can run it. [R]
- Arecibo message (1974): 1,679 bits = 23 × 73, a semiprime that implies a raster. [R]. A 2026 compression or BDM analysis recovers the 23-column width with search-corrected p = 0.005. [P] ([arXiv:2610.08861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08861))
- Cosmic Call 1999/2003 (Dutil & Dumas): bitmapped pages with error-tolerant glyphs, defining numbers, operators, physics and chemistry in sequence. Busch & Reddick note it was not tested for decipherability. [P statement; design details R] ([arXiv:0911.3976](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3976))
- Busch & Reddick (arXiv 2009; published in Vakoch (ed.) *Communication with Extraterrestrial Intelligence*, SUNY 2011): one author coded a 75-kilobit message (113,960 "quads", 8-quad words for numbers, operators, variables and delimiters, Lincos-style). The other decoded it blind. "The decryption was accurate." She found delimiters from frequency and order asymmetry: '00000000' and '00010000' occurred in nearly equal numbers, and one order appeared often while the reverse never did. Five undergraduates spent ≤1 hour each without software. Three correctly identified the delimiters, and two also identified '=', '≠' and integer notation. [P] ([arXiv:0911.3976](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3976); book chapter [doi:10.2307/jj.18254198.33](https://doi.org/10.2307/jj.18254198.33)). A Wired report that the rest "came quickly" once the basics were in place [S].
- Heller 2017, "SETI Decrypt Challenge": about 2 million bits posted on social media, encoding a configuration frame, two mathematical slides and four images with spatial and temporal information. Six questions defined success. Within a month there were over 300 replies, and 66 had the correct solutions. "About half … derived fully independently," and the rest profited from public discussion or spoilers. [P] ([arXiv:1706.00653](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00653))
- "A Sign in Space" (artist Daniela de Paulis with the SETI Institute, ESA and INAF): transmitted from ESA's ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter on 24 May 2023. [R for date]. The message was decoded on 7 June 2024 by Ken and Keli Chaffin, a father and daughter, who ran a Margolus reversible 2×2 block cellular automaton on the "starmap." The result is images of five amino-acid diagrams, with blocks of 1, 6, 7 or 8 pixels standing for H, C, N and O. "The interpretation … like any art piece, remains open." [S] ([SETI Institute](https://seti.org/decoding-message-sign-space-year-long-journey); [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/remember-that-alien-signal-sent-by-the-exomars-orbiter-last-year-its-just-been-decoded); [ESA](https://www.esa.int/media/alien-signal-decoded)). The structural decoding took about 12.5 months with a large crowd on Discord.
- **(2026)** "A Sign in Space part 2" is a simulated first-contact test of the global telescope network and of the IAA protocol pipeline, using an ESA spacecraft for validation. [P] ([de Paulis, Melis & Estevez, arXiv:2610.10587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10587))
- Lemarchand 2008 examines the epistemological assumptions behind "mathematics and physics are universal" and suggests aesthetic and ethical universals as alternatives. [P] ([arXiv:0807.4518](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4518)). Zaitsev 2007 argues that composing messages and decoding received ones are complementary problems. [P] ([arXiv:0711.2368](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.2368))
- Vakoch (ed.) 2011 and Vakoch & Punske (eds.) *Xenolinguistics* (Routledge, 2023): contributors debate whether mathematics, logic or a universal grammar can serve as common ground. Several argue that mathematics may be culturally or cognitively contingent, and that even pictures presuppose shared perception. [R; volume not accessible today]
- Anti-cryptography: a message intended for an unknown receiver should maximise redundancy and self-description, the opposite of encryption, and teach its own conventions. [R; term associated with Minsky, discussed by Vakoch and others]

### Inferences
- The empirical record is that mathematical and delimiter structure is recoverable quickly, by a single person within hours to days (Busch & Reddick) or by a crowd within about a month (Heller). Layered encodings (cellular automata) took about a year even with a crowd and a known human sender. Semantic intent beyond mathematics stayed contested even among humans.
- A Stargazer translation module should therefore treat mathematical self-description as the most plausible semantic entry point: counting sequences, primes, operator and delimiter discovery, and an equation-consistency check of the kind used in Busch & Reddick. It should treat pictorial and cultural meaning as speculative.

### Gaps
- Xenolinguistics (2023) chapter-level arguments could not be verified.
- I found no formal statistics on the number of solvers or accuracy for A Sign in Space.
- Dutil and Dumas primer papers were not read.

## 6. The grounding problem: form alone versus shared referents

### Takeaway
Form alone can yield segmentation, symbol classes, syntax-like dependencies and even latent models of the *process that generated the sequence*: board state in Othello-GPT, entity state in text LMs. Whether it can yield reference to the external world is disputed. Bender & Koller say no, and Quine's indeterminacy says reference is underdetermined even with behavioural data. In practice the anchors that work are referents shared by construction: numbers, physical constants, images and the physics of the signal.

### Cited Findings
- Bender & Koller 2020 (ACL), "Climbing towards NLU: On Meaning, Form, and Understanding in the Age of Data". [P metadata] ([doi:10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.463](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.463)). The octopus thought experiment: a hyper-intelligent octopus that taps an undersea cable can model the form of two islanders' English. It cannot respond appropriately to a novel situation, such as building a coconut catapult or fending off a bear, because it lacks grounding. They conclude that meaning, as the relation between form and communicative intent, cannot be learned from form alone. [R]
- Counter-arguments include an Occam's-razor critique offered for teaching. [P] ([Guerzhoy 2024, arXiv:2407.21070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21070))
- Li, Nye & Andreas 2021: BART and T5 contain contextual representations that act as models of entities and situations evolving through discourse. They support a linear readout of properties and relations and can be manipulated with predictable effects. This is "learned with only text as training data." [P] ([arXiv:2106.00737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00737))
- Li et al. 2022 (Othello-GPT): a GPT trained only on move sequences develops an emergent internal board-state representation, and interventions on it change the outputs. [P] ([arXiv:2210.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382))
- Goldwasser et al.'s footnote observes that congenitally blind people can understand vision verbs, which is evidence that rich structure can partly substitute for direct grounding. [P] ([arXiv:2211.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11081))
- Quine, *Word and Object* (1960): "gavagai" and the indeterminacy of translation. Even with ostension, the reference of a term (rabbit, undetached rabbit part, rabbithood) is underdetermined, and whole translation manuals are underdetermined by all possible evidence. [R]

### Inferences
- What form alone can deliver for a symbol stream is a generative model of the *process*: grammar, state variables, invariants. That is valuable and testable through prediction on held-out data. Mapping those states to referents needs an anchor. For a signal the candidate anchors are: (1) quantities that match known constants, such as ratios equal to the hydrogen-line frequency, π or the electron–proton mass ratio; (2) internal definitions, such as counting sequences paired with symbols; (3) rasterised images; (4) properties of the signal itself, such as its own frequency, period or polarisation used as units. Any semantic claim should name which anchor it relies on.
- Quine's indeterminacy implies that even a successful partial reading should be reported as one of a set of consistent manuals, with an ambiguity count. This matches the Goldwasser et al. framing in §3.

### Gaps
- Bender & Koller's text and formal replies (for example by Piantadosi & Hill and others) were not read today.
- The empirical literature on how far "form-only" world models scale is large and was not surveyed in depth.

## 7. LLM-era capabilities, 2023–2026, and documented failures

### Takeaway
Long-context LLMs can learn to translate an unseen language *in context* from a grammar book plus a word list, approaching a human learner who used the same materials. A 2024 ablation shows that the gain comes almost entirely from the book's parallel examples, not from grammatical explanation. So LLMs exploit bilingual anchors; they do not decipher. LLMs can complete arbitrary token patterns, but they remain weak on linguistics-olympiad puzzles involving writing systems and rich morphology. AI "decipherment" announcements (Linear A, 2026) remain unvalidated. The Vesuvius Challenge, by contrast, is *reading* a known script (Greek), not deciphering one.

### Cited Findings
- MTOB (2023): translate English↔Kalamang (fewer than 200 speakers) from several hundred pages of field-linguistics materials. The best 2023 baselines scored 44.7 chrF (kgv→en) and 45.8 chrF (en→kgv), against 51.6 and 57.0 for a human who learned from the same materials. [P] ([Tanzer et al., arXiv:2309.16575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16575))
- Gemini 1.5 (2024) on Kalamang→English, human evaluation on a 0–6 scale with BLEURT in brackets: 0-shot about 0.14–0.24 for all models ("essentially random"). With half the book, Gemini 1.5 Pro scored 4.14 (63.9) and Claude 3 Opus 3.74 (58.3). With the full book (~100k tokens plus a wordlist), Gemini 1.5 Pro scored 4.00 (64.6), against 5.52 (70.3) for the human learner. En→Kalamang was described as "similar quality to a human who learned from the same materials." [P] ([arXiv:2403.05530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05530), Table 4 and Fig. 4)
- Aycock et al. 2024: "almost all improvements stem from the book's parallel examples rather than its grammatical explanations." They found "no evidence that long-context LLMs can make effective use of grammatical explanations for XLR translation," and fine-tuning a small encoder-decoder on the same parallel data matches the LLM. [P] ([arXiv:2409.19151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19151))
- Mirchandani et al. 2023: LLMs complete sequences from probabilistic context-free grammars and ARC patterns, and keep partial ability "even when the sequences are expressed using tokens randomly sampled from the vocabulary". In other words, they act as general in-context sequence modellers. [P] ([arXiv:2307.04721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04721))
- **(2025)** ICL CIPHERS: bijective token substitutions are used to separate "task learning" from "task retrieval" in in-context learning. [P] ([Fang et al., arXiv:2504.19395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19395)). Prabhakar, Griffiths & McCoy 2024: LLM shift-cipher decoding with chain-of-thought depends on output probability, memorisation and the number of steps, which shows "noisy reasoning" rather than a clean algorithm. [P] ([arXiv:2407.01687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01687)). CipherBench and related 2024 work show that stronger models decode novel user-defined ciphers better, framed there as a security vulnerability. [P] ([arXiv:2402.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10601))
- **(2025)** Linguistics-olympiad benchmarks: across 629 problems in 41 low-resource languages, LLMs "struggle with puzzles involving higher morphological complexity" and do better on features shared with English. [P] ([arXiv:2508.11260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11260)). Another study finds LLMs beat humans on most puzzle types "except for those centered on writing systems." [P] ([arXiv:2509.21820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21820)). IOLBENCH: "even the most advanced models struggle." [P] ([arXiv:2501.04249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04249))
- Time-series foundation models: Chronos tokenises real values by scaling and quantisation and trains T5-family LMs (20M–710M parameters). It shows zero-shot forecasting comparable to models trained on the target data, across 42 datasets. [P] ([arXiv:2403.07815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)). This is relevant to prediction and anomaly detection, not to semantics.
- Disputed AI decipherment: **(2026)** the Linear A claim (40 sign values, 408 words, "Semitic") is unrefereed and criticised as unverifiable. [S] ([Down to Earth / The Conversation](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/science-technology/cracking-the-code-can-ai-help-us-decipher-ancient-languages)). The SIGIL experiment shows how such claims arise: a decipherment stress test reached "high dictionary coverage for English, Sanskrit, and Tamil" on the *same* meaning-only corpus. [P] ([arXiv:2608.02999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02999)). Other publicised LLM "solutions" of the Voynich manuscript, for example claims of Hebrew or Turkish readings, have recurred since 2018 without acceptance. [R]
- Vesuvius Challenge (reading, not deciphering): X-ray CT, virtual unwrapping and ML ink detection on carbonised Herculaneum papyri. The script and language (Greek) are known, so the problem is imaging. Reported milestones: passages from PHerc. Paris 4 in 2023–24 (Philodemus, on pleasure; [R]); the title of PHerc. 172, *On Vices* (2025); the title of PHerc. 139, Philodemus *On Gods* Book 8; and PHerc. 1667, described as the first papyrus "digitally unrolled and read in full, end to end" (June 2026). Sources disagree on some prize attributions and dates. [S] ([scrollprize.org](https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll); [CP24, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/06/26/papyrus-scroll-burnt-to-a-crisp-during-vesuvius-eruption-deciphered-with-help-of-ai/); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ancient-roman-scrolls-destroyed-by-mount-vesuvius-digitally-unrolled-in-full-for-first-time/))

### Inferences
- LLMs are strong *hypothesis generators and pattern completers* and weak *validators*. In a translation toolkit they should propose candidate conventions and readings, but every proposal must be scored by held-out prediction and matched null corpora, not by the LLM's own fluency. Their pretraining also makes them prone to "find" human languages in noise, which is the SIGIL and Linear A failure mode.
- The Vesuvius contrast is useful for scoping. Most "AI decodes ancient X" headlines concern reading or restoring known scripts. Claims about unknown systems need a far higher bar.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed benchmark of LLMs deciphering a *constructed unknown script with no bilingual* for 2025–2026; one may exist but was not located.
- Any formal retraction of an AI decipherment could not be confirmed. The Linear A case is "under review/criticised," not retracted.
- Frontier-model MTOB scores after 2024 were not retrieved.

## 8. A credible decoding workflow and evidence standard; post-detection guidance

### Takeaway
A defensible workflow makes staged, falsifiable claims. Each stage has matched null and generator controls, held-out prediction, pre-registered decision rules, blind re-analysis and open data and code. The IAA's 2026 updated Declaration of Principles explicitly calls for recording detected *content* in confirmation reports and for openly preserving and sharing the data, methods and code. Post-detection scholars (St Andrews Hub; Garrett 2026) warn that an information-rich signal turns the problem into one of information stewardship and competitive decoding.

### Cited Findings
- The IAA SETI Committee's Declaration of Principles dates from 1989, with a draft reply protocol in 1995. It was streamlined in 2010. A Task Group formed in 2022 presented a draft revision at IAC 2024 in Milan and sought community feedback. [P] ([Garrett, Denning, Tennen & Oliver 2025, arXiv:2510.14506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14506))
- **(2026)** The "2026 update to the Declaration of Principles … addresses information content directly: a confirmation report should record any detected content, and the underlying data, methods, and code should be openly preserved and disseminated." A signal with recoverable content "would shift much of the post-detection challenge from verification to information stewardship, while encouraging competitive efforts to decode." Corporations with AI capabilities and receiving infrastructure might pursue decoding without disclosure. [P] ([Garrett 2026, arXiv:2610.21645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21645))
- Reported features of the 2026 declaration: institutions should protect researchers from harassment; there is a new obligation to announce promptly when a candidate is ruled out; there is support for engaging media and social platforms; a permanent Post-Detection Sub-Committee is planned; and the reply restriction is unchanged (no reply without international consultation). [S] ([University of Manchester news](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/beyond-disclosure-day-the-real-world-protocols/); [astrobiology.com](https://astrobiology.com/2025/10/17/seti-post-detection-protocols-progress-towards-a-new-version/)). I did not read the full declaration text.
- The St Andrews SETI Post-Detection Hub (established 2022) provides an interdisciplinary readiness framework spanning science, society, governance and communication. The 2025 white paper recommends funding post-detection research infrastructure and international collaboration. [P] ([Genevieve et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.11587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11587))
- Methodological exemplars for controls: Rozanova & Temerev's matched prose, cipher and pseudo-text controls with quire-level resampling and a "small blind ink audit" [P] ([arXiv:2608.17096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17096)); Raghavendra's pre-registered method registry with source-defined decision rules [P] ([arXiv:2608.02999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02999)); Zenil's "executable controls" that detect dependence and then eliminate it by intervening on the generator [P] ([arXiv:2610.08861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08861)); Heller's predefined six success questions [P] ([arXiv:1706.00653](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00653)).
- Historical validation standard: Linear B was accepted after *independent new data* (the Pylos tripod tablet) read correctly with the proposed values. Successful predictions on unseen texts are the decisive test. [R]

### Inferences: a proposed staged claim ladder for Stargazer
1. **Non-randomness / organisation.** Compression, entropy and mutual information against shuffles and surrogates. This is covered by the structural-layer researchers. Claim: "structured."
2. **Segmentation.** Unit and boundary discovery (transitional probability, MDL, learned units). Must beat matched generators, and boundaries should be stable under resampling across independent segments of the data.
3. **Symbol classes.** Distributional clustering (Kober and Ventris-style grids: symbols sharing positional behaviour), delimiter detection by frequency parity and order asymmetry (Busch & Reddick). Must replicate on held-out data.
4. **Syntax-like dependencies.** Grammar induction or sequence models scored by held-out log-likelihood relative to n-gram, PCFG and self-citation baselines. Language-like statistics (Zipf, Menzerath) are reported but never treated as diagnostic (SIGIL).
5. **Partial semantics, anchored.** Only via named anchors: number systems (counting sequences, arithmetic identities that hold across all instances), physical constants, raster images, or self-reference to signal properties. Each reading must predict unseen parts of the message, for example that the next equation must hold. Report the ambiguity set: how many alternative conventions fit equally well (Quine; Goldwasser et al.).
6. **Interpretation of non-mathematical content.** Label as speculative. Require independent blind teams (Heller, Busch & Reddick and A Sign in Space all show that crowds help but spoilers contaminate independence).
- Required controls at every stage: matched null generators (structured non-language, cipher, self-citation, natural astrophysical or RFI sequences), pre-registered decision rules, a deciphered-benchmark mode on known keys (Ugaritic and Linear B data, synthetic Lincos, Arecibo, Cosmic Call), independent replication, and full data and code release in line with the 2026 IAA declaration.
- Do not let an LLM assign meanings without these gates. The SIGIL result (high dictionary coverage in three unrelated languages on a meaning-only corpus) is the canonical failure to guard against.

### Gaps
- The full text of the 2026 IAA Declaration was not read; the details are secondary except for the information-content clause quoted by Garrett 2026.
- I found no formal published "message-handling" standard for semantic content beyond these principles; the St Andrews Hub's specific recommendations on decoding were not located.

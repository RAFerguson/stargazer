## 1. Space photometry: MAST holdings for Kepler, K2 and TESS; lightkurve, astroquery.mast, AWS S3; MAST API/authentication changes 2025–2026

### Takeaway
TESS is operating in its third extended mission (EM3, Sept 2025–Sept 2028) and is observing Sector 110 (31 Oct–26 Nov 2026) with 200-second full-frame images; Kepler and K2 are closed, static archives. All of it is public and anonymous through MAST (astroquery.mast, lightkurve/lksearch, TESSCut) and through the `s3://stpubdata` open-data bucket, which on 14 Nov 2026 already held FFIs for the first week of Sector 110, target (2-min/20-s) products through Sector 108 and 1,760 TESSCut cube files totalling about 426 TB. The 2025–2026 changes are incremental (TICA cutouts dropped in Aug 2025, astroquery parameter renames, cloud-first downloads, TESS support in `MastMissions`); no MAST authentication change was found.

### Inferences
- For bulk periodicity searches the cheapest robust path is to read light-curve FITS files (SPOC 2-min/20-s, TESS-SPOC, QLP, TGLC; Kepler/K2 long/short cadence) straight from `s3://stpubdata` with anonymous `fsspec`/`s3fs`, using MAST API calls only for discovery; TESSCut/astrocut/tesscube on the S3 cubes covers positions without a pipeline light curve (e.g. faint quasars).
- Because the cubes alone are ~426 TB (45–65 GB per CCD-sector), any all-sky FFI work must run in AWS us-east-1 (TIKE, Fornax) or go through TESSCut with its 10-concurrent-request cap; a laptop tool should default to per-target cutouts and cache them.
- HLSP light curves stop at S98 (QLP), S81 (TESS-SPOC) and S55 (TGLC), so data from 2025–2026 sectors require mission SPOC target products or self-extraction from FFIs.
- Sector lengths are no longer uniform (four-orbit and split sectors in Cycle 8), so code must not assume a 27.4-day window or a fixed per-sector baseline.
- The search of API changes implies a tool should pin `astroquery>=0.4.11` and tolerate both `objectname` and `object_name` spellings until 0.4.12 is released.

## 2. Ground-based optical surveys: ZTF, Pan-STARRS DR2, ATLAS, ASAS-SN Sky Patrol v2, Catalina/CRTS, OGLE, and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory

### Takeaway
ZTF Data Release 24 (22 Jan 2026; data through 22 Oct 2025; ~5.4 billion light curves) is the current public release, with DR25 scheduled for 20 Jan 2027 and operations funded only "through 2026"; it and Pan-STARRS DR2 (72.3 billion single-epoch detections) are both available as anonymous HATS/Parquet catalogues on S3 as well as through IRSA/MAST APIs. Rubin began the 10-year LSST on 29 June 2026 but was off-sky from 15 July to 27 October 2026 after an extreme weather event; alerts have been world-public through brokers since 24 Feb 2026, whereas catalogues and images (DP1, Early DP2, and DR1 expected July–Nov 2028) are restricted to US/Chile/in-kind data-rights holders.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For non-data-rights developers (the default for an open-source tool), Rubin is reachable **only** through broker APIs/streams (alerts and their 12-month histories) until the PPDB opens; catalogue light curves (DP1/DP2/DR1) need an RSP token, so Rubin support should be a pluggable, credential-gated backend.
- Rubin time baselines are short and patchy in 2026: DP2 has a ~5-month primary baseline, the survey lost 15 Jul–27 Oct 2026, and Year-1 alerts depend on template availability — unsuitable for period searches beyond weeks, useful for anomaly triggers.
- ZTF DR24 (7.6-year span, ~3-night cadence, 5.4 billion light curves on S3) is the primary optical resource for quasar periodicity today; the HATS route avoids the REST service's load problems and scales to millions of targets.
- Pan-STARRS DR2 adds 2009–2014-era epochs (sparse: ~10 per filter) that extend baselines for sources also in ZTF; joining PS1 `otmo`→`detection` via LSDB is the scalable pattern.
- ZTF's future beyond 31 Dec 2026 is not documented in anything I could read; the tool should treat DR25 (Jan 2027) as possibly the last regular release until proven otherwise.
- The ASAS-SN client's plain-HTTP port-9006 endpoint will fail behind HTTPS-only proxies; the tool needs graceful degradation for that source.

## 3. Alert brokers and their APIs: ALeRCE, Fink, Lasair, ANTARES, AMPEL, Pitt-Google (and Babamul) — and whether they serve Rubin alerts

### Takeaway
Seven full-stream brokers (ALeRCE, AMPEL, ANTARES, Babamul, Fink, Lasair, Pitt-Google) have received Rubin alerts since 24 Feb 2026, and current client libraries for six of them expose explicit LSST endpoints alongside ZTF; AMPEL is the exception with no evidence of public LSST access. All are free but each needs its own credential (API token, Kafka username/password, or a Google Cloud project), and limits differ sharply (e.g. Lasair 100 calls/hour by default).

### Cited Findings
- `[D]` Rubin lists nine endorsed community brokers; "the seven full-stream brokers have been receiving the early Rubin alert stream" since 2026-02-24; alerts continue "on a best-effort basis" during Early Science. - `[D]` **ALeRCE** — `pip install alerce` (2.4.0, 2026-11-05). ## 4. Gaia: DR3 epoch photometry and variability, Gaia Science Alerts, DR4 (2 December 2026), ESA archive TAP, astroquery.gaia, DataLink limits

### Takeaway
Gaia DR4 is confirmed for **Wednesday 2 December 2026** and will be the largest time-domain release in this inventory: ~400 TB covering 66 months (25 Jul 2014 – 20 Jan 2020), with per-transit G/BP/RP photometry for essentially all ~2.8 billion processed sources (121.8 billion rows), plus epoch astrometry, epoch BP/RP and RVS spectra and the full variability tables. The ESA archive is **offline from 25 Nov 12:00 CET to 2 Dec 12:00 CET** and changed quotas and DataLink names on 14 Oct 2026, so anonymous use is now capped at 50 sources per DataLink call; per-source time series stay DataLink-only, not TAP. On 21 Nov a Gaia TAP sync query worked, but the DataLink data server returned HTTP 503. The spacecraft stopped observing on 15 Jan 2025, so Gaia Science Alerts are a closed historical stream.

## 5. Infrared: WISE/NEOWISE single-exposure photometry (mission ended 2024), unTimely and related time-domain catalogues, SPHEREx public data

### Takeaway
NEOWISE ended on 1 Aug 2024 and its final data release (14 Nov 2024) closes a ~15-year mid-IR (3.4/4.6 µm) time series: roughly two visits a year with a burst of exposures at each visit, ~200 billion single-exposure rows, available through IRSA TAP and as a 42 TB anonymous Parquet copy on S3. SPHEREx (0.75–5 µm, 102 spectral channels) published its first full Data Release (DR1) at IRSA in November 2026, and its weekly Quick Releases give a new all-sky, roughly six-monthly spectro-photometric time series.

### Inferences
- For quasar/AGN work, NEOWISE (and unTimely, which is deeper because it coadds each visit) gives a ~2010–2024 mid-IR baseline at about six-month sampling; it is complementary to ZTF in time (it reaches back before 2018) and in wavelength (dust/torus response). It is useless for periods shorter than ~1 yr except within the ~1-day visit bursts.
- SPHEREx DR1 can provide 102-channel spectra at two or more epochs per sky position; it is a candidate for "spectral anomaly" and line-emission searches, but its time sampling is sparse.

## 6. Spectroscopy: SDSS (DR20, Black Hole Mapper), DESI (DR1; DR2 pending), LAMOST — spectra and quasar catalogues

### Takeaway
SDSS DR20 (paper posted 28 Jul 2026) is the current SDSS release and the most useful spectroscopic source for this tool, because the SDSS-V Black Hole Mapper takes **repeat spectra of quasars** (AQMES and reverberation-mapping programmes) and coordinates with eROSITA. DESI DR1 (March 2025; 18.7 M redshifts including 1.6 M quasars; >280 TB) is public under CC BY 4.0, with programmatic spectrum access through NOIRLab SPARCL/Data Lab; DESI DR2 is not yet public (expected by early 2027). LAMOST DR12 v1.0 (Mar 2025) holds 12.6 million low-resolution spectra, but its hosts could not be reached.

## 7. High energy and multi-messenger: HEASARC (Swift, NICER, MAXI, RXTE, Fermi GBM), Fermi-LAT Light Curve Repository, eROSITA-DE, Chandra and XMM catalogues, IceCube, GWOSC

### Inferences
- A single TAP client (pyvo) pointed at HEASARC covers 4FGL, GBM triggers, Swift/NICER/RXTE/XMM/Chandra observation and source catalogues, eRASS1 and the 2008–2018 IceCube events; table uploads allow batched cross-matching of a quasar list. Parse VOTable; do not expect CSV.
- The Fermi LCR is the only high-cadence (3-day) public gamma-ray light-curve set spanning 18 years for ~1,500 blazars, which makes it the natural gamma-ray counterpart for quasar periodicity checks (e.g. PG 1553+113-type claims), but its endpoint is undocumented: wrap it defensively and cache.
- Swift's 2026 suspension creates a coverage gap from Feb–Aug 2026 (and possibly beyond for one instrument), which a periodicity search across 2026 must treat as a gap, not a signal.

## 10. Optical SETI: is any data public (PANOSETI, LaserSETI, Harvard OSETI, VERITAS, laser-line searches in archival high-resolution spectra)?

### Takeaway
No public raw-data archive of a dedicated pulsed optical SETI instrument was found (PANOSETI, LaserSETI, Harvard OSETI). The only optical SETI-relevant public data are (a) Breakthrough Listen's Automated Planet Finder (APF) high-resolution spectra in the BL Open Data Archive and (b) general-purpose public spectrograph archives (ESO HARPS, Keck KOA), which several papers have searched for laser lines with published pipelines. ## 11. Fit to Stargazer's three use cases, main systematics, and summary table

### Takeaway
(a) For periodic/pulsed signals the strongest public datasets are TESS/Kepler (dense, short-baseline), ZTF DR24 (7.6 years, ~1–3-day cadence), Gaia DR4 epoch photometry (from 2 Dec 2026), and the Fermi LCR (18 years at 3 days). (b) For quasar/AGN light curves: ZTF + PS1 + Gaia DR4 `vari_agn`, plus NEOWISE/unTimely in the IR, SDSS-V BHM multi-epoch spectra, Fermi LCR for blazars, and CRTS/ATLAS/ASAS-SN where reachable. (c) For general anomaly search: broker streams (ZTF + Rubin), Gaia DR4 classifier/outlier tables, SPHEREx spectra and the X-ray/neutrino/GW catalogues for coincidences. Each source has sampling artefacts (seasonal gaps, orbit/scanning-law periods, daily aliasing) that will generate false periodicities unless modelled.

### Cited Findings
- `[P]` ZTF: light curves exist only where reference images exist; single-exposure coverage does not include all fields of both grids, so some light curves are missing; i-band fringe corrections; per-epoch `catflags` (bad if ≥32768 / `BAD_CATFLAGS_MASK=32768`); private-survey epochs lag public ones by 18 months. — [ZTF DR24 notes](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/ZTF/docs/releases/dr24/ztf_release_notes_dr24.pdf)
- `[P]` Gaia: DR4 provides `commanded_scan_law`, `gaia_gap` and a `spurious_signal` table explicitly for "scan-angle related (spurious) signals". — [Gaia DR4](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4)
- `[P]` Fermi LCR: per-bin `fit_convergence`, `fit_tolerance`, TS and upper-limit flags; ROI models include all variable neighbours within 12°; the analysis window ends a few weeks before "now" (last weekly bin ~23 Oct 2026 on 21 Nov). — [LCR about](https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/access/lat/LightCurveRepository/about.html); live response
- `[D]` TESS: non-uniform sector lengths in Cycle 8 (four-orbit, split sectors); 13.7-day orbit; Fornax notes TESS/Kepler light curves are "heavily oversampled for AGN purposes". — §1 sources
- `[D]` Rubin: Year-1 templates change, making usable light curves "extremely difficult"; survey off-sky 15 Jul–27 Oct 2026. — §2 sources
- `[S]` Swift: science suspended Feb–Aug 2026. — §7 sources
- `[R]` General systematics (not re-checked): ground-based surveys have 1-day and 1-sidereal-day aliases, ~29.5-day lunar and 1-year seasonal windows; saturation brighter than ~12–13 mag in ZTF/PS1 and ~6–7 mag in TESS; TESS 21″ pixels cause blending and scattered light near orbit perigee and momentum-dump spikes; Kepler 372.5-day orbit and quarterly rolls; Gaia's 6-h spin, 63-day precession and two fields of view separated by 106.5°; NEOWISE ~6-month visit cadence; ASAS-SN 8″ pixels and blending; LAT 3-hour sky survey, the Sun's annual path through the ecliptic, and 55-day precession (orbital precession of Fermi); GW strain has instrumental lines (60 Hz harmonics, violin modes, calibration lines) catalogued by LVK.

### Inferences
- **(a) Periodic or pulsed signals.** Best: TESS 20-s/120-s/200-s (minutes–weeks), Kepler (to ~4 yr, 1-min/30-min), ZTF DR24 (days–years), Gaia DR4 (hours–years, irregular), Fermi LCR (days–years), NEOWISE (≥1 yr). Seconds-or-faster pulses are not accessible in any survey here except GW strain (16 kHz) and photon-level X-ray/gamma-ray event lists (HEASARC: NICER, RXTE, Fermi GBM TTE, eROSITA DR1 events). A tool should run every period search with the window function and an injection/recovery test per source.
- **(b) Quasar/AGN light curves.** Optical backbone: ZTF DR24 (2018–2025) + PS1 detections (2009–2014) + Gaia DR4 (2014–2020) + CRTS (2005–2013, `[S]`) gives ~20-year baselines for bright quasars; target lists from SDSS DR20/DESI DR1/Gaia `qso_candidates`; IR: NEOWISE/unTimely; gamma: Fermi LCR for ~1,500 blazars; X-ray: eRASS1 (public), Swift/XRT products; spectral variability: SDSS-V AQMES/RM. Main traps: heterogeneous photometric systems between surveys, seasonal gaps producing spurious ~1-yr periods, and red-noise variability masquerading as periodicity over few cycles.
- **(c) General anomaly search.** Streams: Rubin and ZTF alerts via brokers (§3); catalogues: Gaia DR4 `vari_unsupervised`, `ap_class` outlier analysis, `spurious_signal` (as a veto), SPHEREx DR1 spectra, eROSITA DR2 counterparts, IceCube tracks, GW event catalogues for spatial-temporal coincidences.
